## Compare decisions across the batch before assigning IDs

Before writing the goal or assigning Update IDs, compare the operations exposed by the relevant runs. Look for the same choice under recognizable task and workbook conditions, including across different transformations. For example, filtering and aggregation may both depend on choosing evaluated cell values rather than formula text, and distinguishing an unresolved formula result from a genuinely blank cell. Runs supporting that same correction belong under one Update. Examine relevant successes and counterexamples: a run may support the action, constrain its trigger, or leave the cause unresolved. Cite a boundary case when it materially determines the proposed condition or exception, without treating an out-of-scope run as directional evidence inside the trigger.

Ask whether the proposed corrections change the same operation under compatible conditions, and whether one complete instruction can preserve the valid alternatives. Shared words such as formula, label or verification do not establish a shared decision. Text equality, containment and semantic category membership may be branches of one matching decision when task wording and actual labels distinguish them; a numeric condition used alongside that match is not automatically part of the same change. These examples illustrate how to compare proposals, not rules to add without current evidence.

One execution can expose independent decisions. Respecting quantity bounds during residual allocation and restricting writes to populated records can each matter when the other does not apply. They may warrant separate IDs even if found in the same program. Conversely, keep an action with the prerequisite, exception, or check-and-repair path necessary to make that action complete. Do not split a formula-delivery correction into overlapping write and verification proposals when verification is its necessary completion condition. Do not combine independent repairs merely because they jointly affect one task's score or would be easier to attribute as one patch.

Compare each surviving operation with the closest Parent rule. If the same decision is already governed there, propose the supported change to its condition, action or exception and preserve useful branches. A new task setting does not itself require another copy of the existing rule. Keep genuinely new selection or calculation behavior distinct from already supplied reading, saving or cache-handling instructions. Include dependencies needed for a usable correction, but do not repeat the entire locate-read-calculate-write-verify workflow in every task-specific proposal.
