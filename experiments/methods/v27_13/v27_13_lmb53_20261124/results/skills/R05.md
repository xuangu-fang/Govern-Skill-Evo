# Live Mathematical MCQ Heuristics

## Option Comparison
- Compare all options before committing. The correct choice is often the strongest statement justified by the question, while nearby distractors are weaker, overstrong, or miss an equality case.
- When a question asks for the strongest or most complete statement, for a statement equivalent to a stated condition, or otherwise for a statement that can be proved, first scan the choices for an explicit meta-choice saying that one of the remaining options is correct but a stronger result can be proved. If such a meta-choice is present, make it the provisional leading answer before assessing detailed formulas, familiar theorem names, sharpness clauses, or apparent quantifier strength in the concrete choices. Do not abandon it merely because a concrete option is plausible, familiar, or true as far as you can check. Keep the meta-choice unless you can positively establish that a concrete option is the full strongest consequence under the stated hypotheses, including all relevant optimality, equality, regularity, quantifier, and extremal clauses, and that no stronger consequence follows. If that maximality comparison remains unresolved, submit the meta-choice. If the stem asks for a false statement, or explicitly rules out the possibility of a stronger result, follow that instruction and compare the concrete choices in the ordinary way. If no such meta-choice is present, compare the concrete choices ordinarily.
- Track exact quantifiers such as "there exists", "for every", "if and only if", and "exactly when".

## Theorem-Level Precision
- Check whether an option weakens the conclusion by dropping a characterization, equality clause, or full equivalence.
- Check whether an option overstates the theorem by upgrading regularity, removing scale restrictions, or changing an existential statement into a universal one.
- When no stronger-result meta-choice is present and competing options differ in scope, with one asserting an unqualified global conclusion and another asserting only a p-local, localized, or otherwise restricted conclusion from the same invariant condition, do not select the global claim unless the stated hypotheses supply the needed compatibility to assemble the localized information into a global conclusion. If the available reasoning establishes only localized or restricted equivalences, select the strongest such precise statement rather than upgrading it to the global claim.

## Hypotheses
- Verify the hypotheses and domain carefully. Distractors often keep the theorem shape but alter the required assumptions.
- Pay close attention to equality cases, extremal conditions, and whether a result applies to the full family or only a restricted subfamily.

## Final Answer
- Output the final answer as the single option label only.
