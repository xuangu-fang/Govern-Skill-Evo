# Live Mathematical MCQ Heuristics

## Option Comparison
- Compare all options before committing. The correct choice is often the strongest statement justified by the question, while nearby distractors are weaker, overstrong, or miss an equality case.
- When the requested conclusion is the strongest provable statement, evaluate an option that says one of the remaining options is correct but a stronger result can be proved, or that only a weaker existence statement holds, on the same strength scale as the concrete options. Compare conclusion strength, global versus local scope, uniformity and quantifier scope, sharpness of exponents or parameter ranges, and whether the hypotheses are needlessly stronger than needed.
- If one of the displayed statements is true and the best displayed concrete option is still weaker than an available stronger conclusion, the stronger-result option can be the strongest available; if a concrete statement already gives the exact sharp conclusion with no missing strengthening, choose it. Such a stronger-result option does not win merely by being non-specific.
- Track exact quantifiers such as "there exists", "for every", "if and only if", and "exactly when".
- For relative-consistency and model-class options, keep separate: (i) consistency of the phenomenon by itself, (ii) compatibility of that phenomenon with an additional axiom, and (iii) a universal claim that the additional axiom rules the phenomenon out. A claim that an extra principle excludes the phenomenon requires a direct incompatibility; mere absence of a stated link is not enough. Compare options that assert both the base consistency result and joint consistency with the added principle, and do not select a universal-exclusion statement unless its stronger negative premise is supported.
- When a block-tree or other decomposition option names a different object from the one the question defines, such as a decorated tree instead of the sampled block tree, or adds a caveat about an underlying undecorated tree, first match the claimed law or phase to the object to which the question assigns it. An extra claim about a decoration or an auxiliary underlying tree is a separate distributional assertion and needs direct support; it should not replace the statement about the sampled block tree merely by supplying more detail. If the question asks for the law of the sampled block tree, prefer the option that directly asserts that law when its regime boundary is otherwise the same.

## Theorem-Level Precision
- Check whether an option weakens the conclusion by dropping a characterization, equality clause, or full equivalence.
- When a concrete option combines a supported main inequality or existence statement with an added equality case, rigidity conclusion, exact-degree condition, or invariance under a normalizer or other auxiliary group, treat the main statement and the extra clause as separate claims. The extra clause needs its own support from the hypotheses; do not select the combined option merely because its main inequality or existence part is strong. If the extra clause is unsupported and a meta option says a stronger result than the remaining displayed statements can be proved, the meta option may be the correct strongest statement; if the question instead supplies the extra clause or the hypotheses imply it, the combined concrete option may be retained.
- For classification items that list possible forms with a free parameter, separate a necessary-form statement from an added converse or realization clause. An option saying that every object has one of the listed forms does not by itself prove that every formally allowed parameter value occurs; a stronger wording that asserts the converse or that all such parameters actually occur needs independent support. If the question supplies no construction or span argument establishing realization, prefer the classification that omits the converse or realization clause.
- Check whether an option overstates the theorem by upgrading regularity, removing scale restrictions, or changing an existential statement into a universal one.
- When one option claims a natural map is an embedding (Poisson, algebraic, or otherwise) and another claims it is only a Poisson map because it may fail to be injective, require a concrete failure of injectivity: distinct source points with the same image or an explicitly stated collapse from the construction. Potential identifications from passing to a finite-index subgroup, induction, or quotienting are not sufficient grounds to add a non-injectivity caveat. Evaluate Poisson compatibility, algebraic embedding, and injectivity as separate claims; prefer the strongest combination that the construction supports.
- When choices distinguish ordinary homotopy equivalence from p-local homotopy equivalence for every prime, do not upgrade a prime-by-prime or gcd condition that yields p-local equivalences to a global integral homotopy equivalence without an explicit gluing or finite-type argument. Treat 'p-local equivalence for every prime' and 'homotopy equivalence' as separate strength levels, and choose the formulation whose conclusion matches the scope the condition actually supports. A necessary gcd condition alone does not supply the converse.

## Hypotheses
- Verify the hypotheses and domain carefully. Distractors often keep the theorem shape but alter the required assumptions.
- Pay close attention to equality cases, extremal conditions, and whether a result applies to the full family or only a restricted subfamily.

## Final Answer
- Output the final answer as the single option label only.
