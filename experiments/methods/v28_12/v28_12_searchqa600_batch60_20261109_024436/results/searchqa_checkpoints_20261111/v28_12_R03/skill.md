# Question Answering Skill

- When the clue already identifies the item by a specific name, stop at that name: no parenthetical translation, acronym expansion, actor/portrayer, or clue-supplied category. Answer 'LPGA' (not the expanded name), 'Conan' (not 'Conan (played by ...)'), and 'Anastasia' (not 'Anastasia Romanov' when the clue calls her a Romanov). For a person, use the conventional name form in the evidence, including an honorific such as 'Sir' when consistently attached ('Sir Francis Drake', not bare 'Francis Drake'). Keep initials or honorifics that are part of the established name (e.g., 'Dwight D. Eisenhower'), but do not invent an honorific absent from the evidence.

- If the question is only a bare name or a list of capitalized names with no interrogative or relation word, do not answer with a descriptive sentence or a generic role. Return the single shared enclosing entity—especially the state, country, or other place that contains or is associated with all the listed items—and stop at that entity. If the prompt has a relation word, a quoted fragment, or wordplay enumeration, treat it as a different case.

- Match the answer's specificity to what the clue asks: follow the noun category (for 'this type of chewing gum,' return the specific variety, not 'modern chewing gum'; for 'the alliance of these Chinese businesses,' return 'laundries,' not its proper name) and do not add an evidence qualifier that narrows a requested base term—answer 'C' for 'this vitamin,' 'trains' for a series featuring the Daylight and 20th Century Limited, and 'balloon' for the circle enclosing comic dialogue, not 'vitamin C,' 'passenger trains,' or 'speech balloon.' Apply the same check to 'this kind/variety/organization': answer the member of the requested category, not a broader or enclosing entity, and keep a qualifier that is part of an established proper name, such as 'South Sea Bubble.'
