# Desklamp-Conditioned Inspection

Use this procedure only when the task explicitly requires inspecting, examining, or using an object under or with a desklamp. For ordinary put, clean, or transfer tasks where no desklamp is required, do not spend steps searching for, turning on, or co-locating with a desklamp; follow the general retrieval, cleaning, and placement procedure below instead.

## Procedure

1. Locate the desklamp and one matching target object.
2. Establish co-location at the desklamp's receptacle before completing the task:
   - If the target is already on the desklamp's receptacle, remain there.
   - If the target and lamp are on different receptacles, pick up one candidate target and carry it to the desklamp, or place it at the desklamp's receptacle.
3. Turn on the desklamp.
4. Pick up the target there (or keep holding it) and perform the requested look/examine, or use the lamp, while the target is held or otherwise co-located at that receptacle.

## Constraints

- Ensure the desklamp is on and the target is held or otherwise co-located at that receptacle before finishing.
- Inspecting the receptacle alone is not a substitute for interacting with the requested object.
- Do not repeatedly toggle the lamp while the target is at a different receptacle.
- Do not move the target away after turning on the lamp.
- Do not shuttle multiple same-type candidates between locations; remain at the lamp and finish with one co-located target.

## General Non-Desklamp Retrieval, Cleaning, and Placement

For ordinary put, clean, or transfer tasks where no desklamp is required, use this procedure instead.

1. Identify and search for the exact requested target. If it is not visible, run a single-pass sweep that spreads across distinct receptacle families rather than exhausting or looping over one type. Treat each individual closed container (cabinet, drawer, fridge, microwave, safe, etc.) and each open surface as a separate candidate, and do not treat a container family as finished merely because one or two of its members were empty. Open each closed container once upon reaching it and read its contents; if it lacks the target, continue to a receptacle you have not yet inspected. Use your most recent observations to avoid returning to a receptacle you just confirmed empty. After arriving at a receptacle that is already open and empty, or whose visible contents match a location already inspected, do not open, re-examine, or linger there; leave immediately for a receptacle that has not yet been inspected. When one family (for example drawers) yields nothing, switch to a different uninspected family (such as cabinets, shelves, countertops/other surfaces, or appliances) before revisiting any earlier location. Keep the sweep within the step budget so that unopened containers and uninspected surfaces are checked before any loop back; if the same small set of locations keeps reappearing in your recent observations without yielding the target, stop revisiting them and continue to new candidates while the step budget allows. Do not repeatedly check inventory while carrying nothing. Continue until the exact target is found or all plausible candidate receptacles have been inspected once.
2. Manipulate, condition, or place only the exact requested target category. Before acquiring, cleaning, cooling, heating, or placing an object, confirm that its exact category name matches the requested target. Do not complete the task with a functionally similar but differently named object: treat a knife as distinct from a butterknife, a soapbottle as distinct from a soapbar, a cup as distinct from a mug, and a spraybottle as distinct from a soapbottle. If only a near-match is present at the current location, leave it untouched and continue searching for the exact category; never clean, cool, heat, or place a near-match in place of the requested object. If the exact target has not yet been found, continue the systematic search rather than completing the transfer with a non-target object.
3. Acquire the exact target. If the task requires the object to be clean, carry it to an appropriate cleaning receptacle such as a sinkbasin and perform the clean action. If no cleaning is requested, skip the cleaning detour. If the task requires heating or cooling the target with an appliance (microwave or fridge), bring the target and the appliance together and perform the conditioning action directly on the held target as soon as the explicit heat/cool action is admissible, even before opening the appliance; open the appliance only if the action is not otherwise available, and a single heat/cool action is sufficient. Do not impose extra close/open cycles on the appliance door, and do not repeatedly move the target into and out of the appliance between conditioning attempts; for cooling, perform the cool action on the held object at the fridge without any door toggling. Once the target is conditioned, carry it directly to the requested destination and complete the placement without detours.
4. Go directly to the requested destination. Open it first if it is closed, then place or move the target there. Do not detour to unrelated receptacles or release the target before the requested placement is completed.

Keep the successful search, acquisition, and placement behavior used in non-desklamp transfer tasks.
