# Question Answering Skill

## Answer Span and Form
- Determine the expected answer type before extracting. Return the shortest span that uniquely answers the clue, not an explanatory phrase or expanded noun phrase.
- Omit category labels, role descriptions, and unnecessary name expansions. If the clue already supplies the category, give only the specific member; if the source gives a concise answer form (a surname, single name, letter, or group name), use that form rather than adding surrounding words.
- Match the grammatical form requested by the clue. For nationality, language, or adjective answers, return the adjective rather than the country/place noun; for names, use the form the clue expects rather than automatically expanding to a fuller name.

## Clue Interpretation
- If the question is a declarative statement with a pronoun such as 'his', 'her', or 'this', answer the entity the pronoun refers to; do not answer True/False or merely restate the statement.
- If the question is a quoted fragment, ellipsis, fill-in-the-blank, or possessive/partitive placeholder (e.g., `this city's Sabres`, `Rose of this capital`), complete or identify the known title/phrase and return only the requested missing component or placeholder category (the city/capital itself), not the named team/person, surrounding descriptor, or the entire completed title. Match the full phrase/title implied by the clue rather than a document whose title only contains the visible words. Ignore truncated, duplicated, or garbled fragments; extract the coherent core clue and do not treat artifacts as additional constraints.

## Exact Relation Verification
- For relational clues (e.g., 'named for', 'written by', 'located in'), locate the passage where the exact relation is stated between the queried subject and the answer. Do not choose a nearby name that merely co-occurs in the same document or belongs to a different relation.
- When several names appear, prefer the one explicitly linked to the queried object and descriptor; if a passage mentions a name in another construction, treat it as a distractor.

## Context Reading and Disambiguation
- Treat the question as a Jeopardy-style clue: identify the single entity or phrase that satisfies all constraints in the clue (date, role, work, title, location, distinguishing descriptors), not just one.
- Use document titles (`[TLE] ...`) as a fast candidate list; the answer often appears as the subject of a relevant title or clearly in the passage best matching the clue.
- Pull distinctive/rare terms from the question (proper names, quoted phrases, numbers/years, unusual nouns) and choose the passage where several appear together. Ignore navigation, sidebars, related-idea lists, boilerplate, and unrelated archives; a passage matching every constraint beats many passages sharing only generic topic words.
- Prefer explicit, direct statements that name the answer. If the same fact is repeated across independent passages, treat that agreement as a strong confidence signal.

- The supporting sentence usually echoes several distinctive clue terms together—a proper name, date, place, or relation verb (e.g., 'married to', 'known by', 'capital on', 'limited ... on')—and gives the answer as the direct object or noun filling that slot. Find that near-verbatim sentence first, then extract only the queried noun, especially in archive, Q&A, listicle, or social-post excerpts where the clue wording reappears almost word-for-word with the answer beside it (after a pipe, dash, or colon, or in the next clause).
- Map question type to answer type: who = person, where = place, which/this X = the name of X.

- Treat nicknames, epithets, and descriptive paraphrases as aliases for the primary entity they conventionally designate: e.g., 'Peach State'/'peachy state' -> Georgia, 'Cyclone State' -> the state, 'Uncle Walter' -> the person. Return the canonical name, not the nickname or an incidental same-phrase team, fanbase, or nearby place.

- Distinguish the direction of an alias clue: if the clue asks what someone/something was called, known as, or referred to as (e.g., 'his name was Zeb, but they called him this'), return the called name/nickname/title itself (e.g., `Grandpa`), not the formal name.

- Resolve puns, wordplay, catchphrases, and short quoted fragments to the single entity or term they conventionally evoke, not the literal wording, a compound phrase built from the visible words, or a titled work that merely reuses those words. A word embedded in a quoted title or phrase may itself be the answer name (a pun on a person); a famous catchphrase points to the character, show, or brand it belongs to rather than a later film/song whose title borrows it; a playful 'this market/animal/thing' clue answers the concrete entity the pun describes, not a multi-word compound noun assembled from the clue's own words.
- For 'this [country/play/movie/prison/work/etc.]' clues, return the entity being described and do not return a related nearby entity.
- When a clue combines two facts, lists parallel examples, or gives a definition (e.g., 'emit vapor' AND 'show irritation'; a "hasty" dessert or tapioca; 'the cry of a sheep or goat'), return the single term or common headword whose definition covers all senses/examples (e.g., `pudding`, `bleat`), in base/singular form for 'term for [definition]' clues (e.g., `insulator`); do not combine separate candidates or return a synonym/explanatory phrase.
- For definitional or dictionary-style clues ('An X that...', 'A Y used to...'), return the generic class/category term being defined, not a specific product, remedy, or example of it that merely appears in the passages.
- For translation or parenthetical-title clues, return the translated phrase/title itself (e.g., `My Crazy Life`), not the original-language phrase, a full sentence, or extra explanation.
- Canonical form details: preserve the exact spelling, capitalization, internal punctuation, and stylization used in reliable context (e.g., `M*A*S*H`, not `MASH`); preserve a leading article when the source states the answer as that exact noun phrase (e.g., `the atmosphere`) or when it is conventionally part of the proper name/title (e.g., `The New York Times`), but do not add an article otherwise; preserve parentheticals or other name elements only when they are part of the established name (e.g., `(Lou) Gehrig`, `Lord Robert Baden-Powell`); for title answers, use the exact title wording and capitalization from a matching `[TLE]` or the clearest supporting sentence and stop at the title boundary unless a subtitle/parenthetical is conventionally part of the established title; prefer exact wording found in the supporting context over paraphrases.
- Return the name form the clue expects. If the clue already supplies part of a person's name (a given first name, or it names a companion/associate in the same construction), give only the minimal completing form — usually the surname alone (e.g., clue 'joined Cassius' → `Brutus`; clue 'a President named Chester' → `Arthur`) — rather than repeating the full name.
- Do not strip honorifics or titles (e.g., `Sir`, `Dame`, `Lord`) when they are part of the person's established name as consistently given in the context; keep them even though the bare name would be shorter.

- Calibrate the name to the exact form the supporting passage uses. Do not prepend given names, fuller legal names, or extra descriptors that neither the clue nor the answering passage supplies (e.g., if the qualifying passage gives only a surname for a clue beginning `He...`, answer the surname, not a full first+last name). Conversely, do not strip an element that is integral to the established proper name, such as a geographic designator (`Lake Superior`, `Mount X`, `X River`), even when the clue's category word already implies it. Drop only bare category labels or role words that are not part of the name itself.
