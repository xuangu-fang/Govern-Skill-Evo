# Question Answering Skill

- Answer with the concise entity or term that fills the clue's slot; do not substitute or supplement a performer or portrayer when the clue requests a character or title entity. Omit role labels, parenthetical glosses, synonyms, and category words already supplied by the clue. When the supporting sentence gives the requested term directly, return that term alone, since a correct term plus extra material can still miss the requested answer.
- When the question consists only of a complete quoted sentence or dictum from a literary work that the passages attach to a person, with no explicit request for the work's title, return the author or attributed creator the passages attach to the line; do not apply this author/speaker rule to a bare quoted title. When the entire question is a work title in quotation marks and the passages identify it as the opening song or number from a named musical or film, answer the containing work (for example, Oklahoma!), not a lyricist or composer. Do not return the work title merely because the line is famous or a document heading uses that title. For a bare title/year clue, use the relation the passages state for that title: if they explicitly call the named work an adaptation or retelling of another named work, return that source work rather than production credits, directors, or performers; if they attach the title to a person or group, return that associated person or group. Do not default to a creator merely because the title is famous. If the question explicitly asks for a title, translation, meaning, or another named slot, answer that slot instead.
- For a clue made only of a speaker/character label followed by a quoted line, when the passages identify that line as dialogue in a named play or other work, return the work title rather than the author or playwright. Treat the speaker label as a work cue, not as a request for the creator. Keep the general author/dictum rule for a freestanding quoted sentence with no speaker or work cue, and keep the existing bare-title rules.
- When a plural clue asks for the general source (for example, 'these animals') and passages identify a particularly or characteristically preferred source alongside other possible or occasional sources, answer that preferred source alone instead of listing every alternative. Enumerate multiple sources only when the clue explicitly asks for all possible sources.
- Treat a fragmentary question as a fill-in-the-blank. Use the passages to identify the missing word or entity: complete a leading-ellipsis quotation with the proper noun that makes the known phrase, and expand a trailing place code or abbreviation to the place name. For a leading-ellipsis geography fragment whose visible predicate is 'That contains...' or 'Home to ... a grove of...', treat the blank as the subject and answer the containing state, region, or continent that the passages locate the resource or grove in (for example, Antarctica for the freshwater percentage; California for the bristlecone grove), not the contained material or the feature's own name; return the feature name only when the clue explicitly asks for that grove, forest, or site. Do not instead return a prominent song, work or artist, or a landmark associated with the visible words, unless the question explicitly asks for that item.
- When the question is only a bare proper name or a list of names or identifiers, return the associated title or named entity, not a descriptive role/group label or a longer variant of the clue. Strip a title or role word shared by all listed names and return the place or collective that completes it; for a single place name, return its containing state/country when the passages repeatedly state that relation. Do not apply this to cryptic clues with an explicit word-length/wordplay cue, or when the clue itself asks for the role or group.
- When the entire question is only a bare company or brand proper name and the passages repeatedly attach it to a country of origin, return that country of origin rather than the full legal company name or another longer variant of the clue. If the passages instead attach the bare name to a person, group, work, or title, return that associated entity as already instructed; keep the legal name only when the clue explicitly asks for the manufacturer or full company name.
- Match the answer span to the clue's slot. Do not append a generic category word or repeat a descriptive modifier that the clue already supplies, and do not replace a general category with a named subtype the clue does not request. After a plural demonstrative naming a beverage or style, use the ordinary mass or singular category form the passages use rather than inventing a plural; keep a plural count noun when the clue's 'these' asks for multiple objects. When both a short form and a longer form are supported by the passages, start with the short form. Read it back in the clue. Keep an extra word only if removing it changes what the answer refers to or leaves the requested expression incomplete. A category word already supplied by the clue can usually be omitted.
  - 'Hallmark' not 'Hallmark Cards'
  - 'Roanoke' not 'Roanoke Island'
  When the clue asks for a general item ('this … fish', 'one of these', 'this … material') or already supplies the category or measure word (for example, 'this thick pile fabric', 'some called Grande Vitesse', 'negative rate of this'), answer the base noun alone (velvet, trains, savings), not a named subtype or an extended description such as high-speed trains or personal savings rate. If the clue asks for 'one of these [plural noun]' or otherwise explicitly makes the slot a single member of a plural category, return the singular base noun even when an illustrative passage lists the category in plural; do not apply that singular choice when the clue itself uses a plural category such as 'species of these.' Keep a specific subtype only when the clue itself names it or explicitly asks for the subtype or variety. If the slot is 'this type' after a plural category (for example, muscles are striated, cardiac or this type), include the category head to complete the phrase (smooth muscles). For a company clue that supplies the word 'company', return the distinctive brand (Coca-Cola), not the full legal form (The Coca-Cola Company), unless the company word is integral to the brand.
- For a person answer, use the name form in the passage sentence that states the clue's identifying fact. If that sentence gives only a surname, return the surname alone; if it gives a formal full name, do not substitute a nickname from a heading or another passage. Do not expand a surname to a fuller name found elsewhere unless the clue itself requires that fuller form. When the clue already supplies the person's given name or another distinctive part of the name and asks for that individual, return the remaining surname or title instead of a full name that repeats the supplied part. Preserve a full-name answer when the clue supplies only a nickname and the passages present the full formal name as the requested person.
- For a title answer, start from the passage phrase that states the clue's fact, but do not copy a descriptive or award-category suffix that the clue already supplies as the category. Drop a generic category word the clue uses for the type (for example, 'party' or 'network') and return the distinctive title without that same word; keep the full title when the word is an integral part of the title rather than a category supplied by the clue. If the clue instead asks for a publication and uses only a broader synonym for the type (for example, 'journal'), keep the publication word that the passage gives as part of the title (for example, 'magazine'). For a work title with a colon subtitle, return the base title when the clue already identifies the work and the shorter form is sufficient; do not extend with a subtitle such as 'A Town, a Team, and a Dream.' Apply this to books as well as films. Keep the full title when the second part is part of the title proper rather than a colon subtitle.
- When the clue already names the person and asks for a moniker or nickname, return only the nickname itself, not the nickname followed by the person's surname. Use the surname only when the clue does not already supply the person or when the requested answer is the full name rather than the moniker.
- When a bare campaign slogan or paired-name phrase names a principal candidate by an epithet and also names a running mate or secondary person, return the principal candidate alone; treat the second name as context unless the question explicitly asks for both people or for the running mate.
- For a named landmark or venue with multiple passage names, use the name form in the passage sentence that directly states the clue's identifying fact; prefer the English or historic name attached to that fact over a foreign-language equivalent or a later corporate renaming.
- Resolve an undated historical 'in charge of' clue to the department tied to the named period or initial enforcement. If passages say enforcement was allocated to one cabinet department and later transferred to another (for example, Prohibition enforcement to Treasury, with a 1930 transfer to Justice), answer the initial or period department, not the later transferee; use the later department only when the clue specifies the later date or asks about the transfer.
- When a clue gives a specific year plus a count or record, answer the entity that the year-specific passage ties to that clue; do not choose a person from a general biography that mentions the same category but not the stated year/count.
- When a geography fragment has the form 'In [region]:ALLCAPSWORD', treat the all-caps word as letters to rearrange into a place in that region; answer that place, not a literal organism or object named by the word.
