# Question Answering Skill

## Answer Form

- Return the shortest complete answer form that still identifies the requested referent.
- When the clue already supplies the requested category, role, location, unit, or corporate/government designator, use the established shorter name or the missing noun rather than repeating that supplied material: do not add a province/state after a city already located by the clue, a corporate suffix after a company clue, a rate/unit word after 'rate of this', a national prefix when the clue's setting already supplies the country, or a category adjective when the clue asks for a broad class and the passage's label is the broader noun.
- When the clue's own wording supplies a descriptive modifier for the requested item—for example, a color or qualifier directly attached to the requested noun, as in 'this black candy'—return the distinctive answer term without repeating that supplied modifier, even when a passage lists a longer label for the same item. Do not apply this when the shorter form would be ambiguous or would not identify the requested referent, or when a passage presents the longer form as an explicit answer to the same clue.
- Shorten only when the shorter form is established and unambiguous for the requested referent in light of the clue and passages, and not when a passage presents a longer form as an explicit answer to the same clue. Keep an article, full name, honorific, hyphenation, or qualifier when it is part of the established title/name, needed to distinguish the referent, or explicitly requested; do not keep a designator that merely repeats a category the clue already supplies when the shorter established name is unambiguous. Do not reduce a class answer to a bare adjective when the head noun is required to name that class, and retain the head noun and grammatical number supported by the clue and passages.
- When the passages identify a preferred or especially cited representative and separately mention occasional alternatives, answer with the preferred representative rather than combining all alternatives into a parenthetical or disjunctive list.
- When the answer is an acronym or short name, give it alone; append a spelled-out expansion only if the clue asks for the expansion.
- When a clue is a character's name followed by a colon and a quoted line from a play, with no question stem and no explicit request for the next or missing words, the author, or the speaker, treat it as asking for the play or work containing that character and line: return that work title rather than a continuation of the quotation or the author. This speaker-labeled work-title rule controls over the general bare-quotation rule that would otherwise return the author for a quoted literary passage. Continue to complete the quotation only when the clue explicitly asks for the missing word or phrase, and return the author or speaker only when the clue explicitly asks for that role.
- When the clue consists only of a direct quotation or a bare work title (optionally with a year) and has no question stem, identify the associated creator rather than restating the quotation or work title: give the author for a quoted literary passage and the director or other passage-supported creator for a bare film/work title; use the person the passages explicitly connect to that work, and do not return the title as the answer unless the clue asks for the title.
- When a clue quotes an incomplete line and asks for the next or missing word or phrase, including a clue ending in 'this', complete the quotation with the missing word or phrase; do not return the work title or its author or creator merely because the clue mentions the work.
- When a clue presents a recognizable distorted, misquoted, or spoonerized phrase but does not explicitly ask for the name of the error, return the intended original phrase rather than the error-category label or the speaker or creator. This intended-phrase rule controls over the general bare-quotation rule above that would otherwise return a creator when the passage identifies the quoted fragment as a misquotation or wordplay.
- If the clue explicitly asks for the work title, author, speaker, or error term, return that requested item.
- When a clue consists of a year plus a quoted phrase or slogan and the passages identify that phrase as a film's tagline or advertising slogan, answer the film or work title associated with that tagline and not the director, cast member, or other creator. This tagline rule controls over the general bare-quotation/bare-work-title rule that would otherwise return a creator. If the clue explicitly asks for a person, or asks for the tagline's author or speaker, return that requested person instead of the work title.
- When a clue consists only of a bare work title, optionally with a year or evaluative descriptor, and has no question stem, and the passages state that the work is an adaptation, retelling, or reworking of an earlier source work, answer that source work when the clue does not explicitly ask for a person, the title itself, or another role.
- If the passages present a chain of adaptations, give the original source work that the passages identify as the basis of the retelling, not an intermediate adaptation.
- This source-work rule controls over the general bare-work-title rule above that would otherwise return the creator or director. If the clue or passages explicitly ask for a creator or the work title itself, or if no source work is identified, return the requested item or creator rather than replacing it with a source work.
- When the clue is only a song title, with no question stem and no explicit request for a person, and the passages identify that song as a number from or the opening song of a named musical, film, or other containing work, answer with that containing work's title rather than its composer, lyricist, or performer.
- Use the general bare-work-title creator rule only when the passages do not identify a containing work or the clue asks for the creator, and preserve the established containing work title's wording and article.
- When the clue consists only of a comma-separated or ampersand-separated list of people or items with no question stem, identify the shared proper jurisdiction, nationality, family/house, source, work, title, or group and return that shared proper name alone. Do not answer with a descriptive role phrase or add a category noun for the listed people, and do not repeat the listed names in the answer.
- When the clue asks for a nickname, moniker, alias, or sobriquet, return only that nickname label and do not combine it with the person's full name or repeat a name already supplied by the clue.
- When the clue is only a personal title or name with no question stem, identify the associated proper jurisdiction, nationality, house, or group and return that proper name alone; do not answer with a role phrase such as 'Sultan of X' or otherwise restate the title. Apply this rule to single-name clues as the existing list-only rule applies to listed names.
- If the shared basis is a place or royal house, give the proper place/house name rather than an 'X senators' or 'X princesses' style label; preserve an established shared title or work only when that title or work is the actual unifying answer.
- When a clue with no question stem names a landmark or place along with a subnational location, or gives a locational/containment description such as 'home to' or a fragment beginning '...that contains', and the passages explicitly place the described feature, grove, or resource within a larger state, country, or continent, answer that enclosing jurisdiction's proper name rather than a descriptive superlative, a nearby material/category, or a named sub-area.
- Return the jurisdiction at the level the passages supply, and keep a full place name only when it is the requested jurisdiction or needed to distinguish it; if the clue explicitly asks for a city, country, or other named entity, answer that requested entity instead of its container.
- When a clue asks for 'these' animal parts or ingredients and lists examples such as an organ or cut, return the collective term for those listed parts that the passage defines; do not substitute the prepared dish, recipe, or sauce made from them.
- When the clue asks for a term, type, shape, or class using a basic noun or definition, or for one member of a class, answer with the generic term at the level the clue requests, using the distinctive term without appending a category noun the clue already supplies; do not substitute the proper name of a specific event or object for the requested generic term, and do not narrow to a species, brand, or compound subtype mentioned in a passage unless the clue itself supplies the distinguishing detail that selects the subtype. When the clue instead asks for a specific named entity, give that entity's established name, retaining a designator such as a category suffix when the shorter form is ambiguous or not an established name for that entity, but dropping a suffix that merely repeats the clue's category when the shorter established name is unambiguous. Preserve a more specific term when the clue explicitly asks for a particular species or object, or when the broader term would not identify the requested referent.
- Preserve required identifying components: when the clue describes the requested person by role or description rather than supplying the name, use the name form by which the passages ordinarily identify that person. Prefer a distinctive stand-alone surname, a familiar short name, or an established one-word stage or character name when that is the ordinary identifying label; do not default to a fuller first-plus-surname, a different formal given name, or a formal expansion merely because the full form appears in a page title, birth-name clause, or first sentence. Keep a full name, middle initial, or honorific when the clue asks for the full or formal name, when the passages use the fuller form as the established public identifying label, when the short form would be ambiguous with another plausible person, or when the clue otherwise depends on the fuller form; do not drop an honorific or name component that the passages consistently attach to the requested person.
- Apply concision only by removing genuinely redundant additions; do not drop a component needed to identify the requested entity or change its grammatical number merely to make it shorter.
- When the clue asks for a kinship relation with phrasing such as 'this relative of his/her/their' and the supporting passage identifies the relation with a possessive noun phrase, answer with the possessive noun phrase rather than only the bare kinship term even though the bare term would otherwise be shorter; keep the possessive only when the clue establishes whose relative is requested and the passage uses that possessive form. Do not add a possessive when the clue or passage does not supply one.
- For a multiword answer term, follow the word-spacing form used in the passage's defining label; do not insert hyphens between separate words unless the passages establish that hyphenated form as the name.
- Preserve an established hyphenated name when the passages present it that way.
- When a title or name appears with both accented and unaccented spellings, use the form supplied by the clue when the clue itself contains that title or name; if the clue does not contain it, use a consistently accented passage form only when the passages consistently present it as the established name. When the passages mix accented and unaccented forms and the clue does not select one, use the unaccented form already present in the passages rather than adding diacritics from background knowledge. Preserve an accented form when the clue or passages consistently present it as the established name.
- For a class or phenomenon, use the singular lexical headword when the clue uses a plural generic phrase and the supporting passages define the class in the singular; preserve a plural answer only when the clue and passages call for a specific plural set, the passages expressly give that plural noun as the requested label, or the passages give a plural conventional name for an instrument or object (such as a class whose established name is plural). In that last case keep the established plural form even when the clue says “this instrument” or otherwise uses a singular generic noun. Do not pluralize merely to echo the clue's grammatical number, and do not preserve a plural merely because the passages use a plural class label that is not the established conventional name; apply singularization only to ordinary generic class terms that are not conventionally plural names.
