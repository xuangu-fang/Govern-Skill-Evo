# Question Answering Skill

## Learned Rules

- Determine the exact noun slot the clue asks for before answering. When a clue asks for 'these' parts or ingredients used to make a dish, give the parts or ingredients themselves, not the finished dish. When a clue asks for an animal's usual home or covering and a passage states a general category term, give that general term without adding a species or material qualifier from another passage unless the clue requests the variant. Keep a specific proper name when the requested entity is itself that name rather than a broad category. This does not override the existing rule that a bare name or list of names is answered with the shared or containing place the passages attach to the items.
- When the clue is a bare phrase or single term, a list of names, or a declarative statement with an unidentified pronoun rather than an explicit question, treat it as an entity-seeking clue: infer the missing answer type from the clue's grammar and the supporting passages. For a bare name or list of names, prefer the shared or containing place (such as a state or country) that the passages attach to the item(s); if no place is supported, use another identifying affiliation rather than a role, family relationship, or definition. For a declarative clue with a pronoun, answer the antecedent entity the statement is about.
- When the entire clue is a prepositional phrase beginning with 'In' followed by one or more place names, treat it as a request for the associated adjective, demonym, nationality, or language rather than the containing country or region; keep the containing-place answer for a bare place name or a bare list without that prepositional frame.
- Except for the 'In ...' prepositional frame above, when the clue is only a place or landmark phrase, or an incomplete phrase saying that a place is 'home to' or 'contains' a named feature or resource, without an explicit 'this [category]' request, answer the broadest containing geographic or political place that a passage directly attaches to that feature or resource (country over state when both are supplied; the state, region, or continent when it is the direct container). Do not answer the landmark, park, material, or a superlative description instead.
- Keep the explicitly requested entity when the clue says 'this [category]' or otherwise asks for the landmark, city, hotel, state, or country itself; this containing-place preference does not override such an explicit entity request.
- In the answer element, return only that entity or shared/containing attribute, without a definitional or biographical sentence, relationship description, Yes/No restatement, or opening repetition of the clue.
- In the answer element, as a specific exception to the general shortening and relationship-description rules, when the clue asks for a relative (for example, 'this relative of his/her') and the directly answering passage phrases the answer with a possessive relative such as 'his mother' or 'her father', include that possessive relative phrase rather than reducing it to the bare relative noun; do not expand it into a relationship explanation.
- When a passage names an answer-level type and also a narrower variety of that same type (a species, color, or subtype), return the answer-level type unless the clue itself uses the narrower term or the narrower term is needed to distinguish the requested referent.
- Do not replace an answer-level type with a narrower variant merely because the variant appears prominently in the same passage; keep a specific answer when the clue asks for a particular product, office, title, or named thing.
- When the clue's plural demonstrative 'these' refers to examples or instances of a mass-noun category or named style rather than to multiple distinct countable items, answer with the conventional singular or mass form of that category; keep the plural form only for inherently plural group names or for distinct countable items explicitly requested.
- Before selecting a candidate, resolve the answer type and relation the clue requests: if the clue defines a product, role, or class by function, return the class-level designation named by the passage rather than a specific example or substance from the same passage; if the clue describes a process step or event, prefer the candidate whose own passage states that step or event together with the requested answer, not a candidate that appears only as an alternative in another context; if a work title is labeled as a classic and a passage explicitly identifies the earlier source work it adapts, answer that source work rather than a creator. Use the narrower example, alternative candidate, or creator only when the clue explicitly asks for that role and a passage states the full requested conditions.

- In the answer element, use the form given by the passage sentence that directly ties the requested entity to the clue, rather than a fuller or shorter variant taken from another passage. Within that form, build the answer from the distinctive name core and use the shortest answer unit that fully identifies the requested referent. When the clue already supplies an object category, prefer the shortest passage-attested conventional designation for that object and do not repeat a category noun or descriptive qualifier already supplied; when the clue supplies a first name, category noun, or descriptive role, do not repeat that supplied word and do not add a fuller personal name, title, subtitle, or national/regional qualifier that the directly answering sentence does not use, unless the supplied word is an inseparable part of the ordinary name; in that setting prefer the shorter surname, core, or title if it still uniquely identifies the referent. Do not append an artist, state, campus, middle name, suffix, title phrase, parenthetical expansion, alias, later subtitle, or explanatory clause merely as extra detail, and drop a trailing generic category word or close synonym that merely repeats the clue-supplied category unless it is an inseparable part of the ordinary name and no shorter conventional designation for the same referent is supplied. Keep a leading article, honorific, numeral, middle initial, or conventional designator when it is part of the established name or title or is needed to identify the exact referent. Follow the directly answering form's spelling, accent, and word divisions: do not add diacritics the passage omits, and use the passage's spaced form of a multi-word term rather than inserting hyphens.
- In the answer element, as a specific application of the rule against repeating a clue-supplied category noun: when the clue supplies a broad object type (for example a university, company, missile, or mountain) and asks for the entity by its distinctive name, give the shortest passage-attested name core and omit a leading or trailing generic type, institutional, or corporate designator that merely repeats or classifies the supplied type (for example a leading 'Mount' or a trailing 'University'/'State University', 'Corporation'/'Motor Corporation', or 'missile'); keep a type word that is inseparable from the conventional proper name (for example a sea name), and keep honorifics, titles, middle initials, or other established name components when the directly answering sentence or clue uses them as part of the identity. When the clue's category is a broad type and the passage that directly answers uses a more specific designator as part of the ordinary name, retain that designator rather than shortening to a bare surname or core only when the designator is inseparable from the conventional name; when the trailing designator is the clue's own category word, do not repeat it unless it is an inseparable part of the ordinary name and no shorter conventional designation for the same referent is supplied.
- When the clue asks for a nickname, moniker, or alias, return only the nickname itself, not the nickname plus the person's surname or another identifier.
- Do not repeat a title fragment that the clue uses only to describe the requested entity; when the requested answer is the work, person, or title itself, give the conventional full name or title rather than an incomplete fragment.
- Preserve honorifics, titles, conventional name forms, and other components needed for the requested entity's identity. Use the person-name form supplied by the clue; when the clue gives no name, use the form in the sentence that directly states the answering facts, not a fuller or shorter variant from another passage. Retain a royal title, a knighthood, a peerage title, an honorific title, or a numeral that the directly answering sentence (or the clue) uses as part of the person's established identity; do not drop it merely to shorten the answer, and do not add an ordinary political, occupational, or descriptive role that the clue already supplies or implies. Do not add a first name absent from that form; do not replace a formal name in that answering sentence with a nickname found only elsewhere; and do not replace a familiar form supplied by the clue or a context title with a more formal variant found only in another passage. Do not add a middle initial, expanded middle name, or longer name variant unless the clue or that directly answering statement uses it as the established form; otherwise use the shorter form. When the clue blanks part of a proper compound name and asks for the named entity, give the complete conventional compound name, not only the word that fills the blank.
- When the only passage occurrence that directly ties a person to the clue is a document title or heading, preserve that occurrence's exact name form, including the punctuation and spacing of initials; do not insert spaces between initials or replace it with a differently spaced or fuller form taken from another passage.
- When a wordplay clue places a prefix or partial term directly before 'this' (for example, a form such as 'multi-this'), treat 'this' as a request for the missing word or suffix and answer only that missing element, not the full compound formed with the prefix. Do not keep the prefix merely because the completed compound is a familiar product, brand, or proper name, unless the clue explicitly asks for the complete term, product, or work. This is an exception to the proper-compound blank rule above, which still requires the full conventional compound name when a clue blanks part of a proper compound name and asks for the named entity.

- When an English-language clue asks about a landmark, palace, venue, or similar institution and the passages attest both a conventional English or traditional name and a local-language or current sponsored name, answer with the conventional English or traditional name that the clue's identifying details support; do not substitute the local official form or later rebrand unless the clue asks for the current or official designation. Preserve any additional name components required to identify the specific referent.
- When the clue uses a demonstrative in a relational phrase (for example, 'the energy from this' or 'a series on these'), resolve the demonstrative to the entity that fills that grammatical role and answer that entity.
- Do not substitute the instrument, measurement, collection, or other predicate noun that the clue also mentions; keep the requested entity type.
- For a clue with several identifying conditions, choose the candidate for which one passage states those conditions together; do not assemble an answer from a name in one passage and an incidental phrase from another, and do not prefer a more prominent item that satisfies only part of the clue.
- When the clue says "seen here" or otherwise refers to what is shown, use the passage wording that ties the described kind or action to the requested item, rather than a nearby name that matches only a keyword.

- Treat a quoted phrase in the clue as a constraint on the requested answer, not merely as a keyword. A work or name that merely appears in a passage with the quoted words is not automatically the answer.
- If the quote is an incomplete title or phrase marked by an ellipsis, return only the missing word(s) or the entity that completes the phrase; do not substitute a performer or author merely because it appears with the fragment. Do not return the full visible or full conventional title unless the clue explicitly asks for the complete work, and keep the full title only when the clue asks for the work identified by a title or subtitle.
- If the quoted phrase contains a word that is also part of a person's name and the clue asks for a person type, prefer the person whose name is embedded in or completes the quoted phrase over a literal cast or credit match.
- Apply this quoted-phrase treatment even when the entire clue is only the quoted fragment; it takes priority over generic bare-clue handling.
- When the entire clue is only a quoted exclamation or catchphrase and does not itself ask for a speaker or character, apply the real-person slogan rule below when it applies; otherwise, unless the clue marks an object slot such as 'this' in a product slogan, answer the source work or series associated with that phrase in the passages; do not expand the phrase into a longer derivative title that merely contains the quoted words unless the clue explicitly asks for that title.
- Treat a bare slogan or signature catchphrase presented as a real person's slogan or signature catchphrase, with no explicit question, as a request for that person when the passages support it: when a passage attributes the phrase to that real speaker, answer the speaker; when a campaign slogan pairs a nickname with a running mate, answer the candidate denoted by the nickname or the top of the ticket, not both names or the surrounding organization. Do not apply this rule when the clue marks an object slot such as 'this' in a product slogan; answer the product or brand in that case. Keep the existing handling for explicit requests for a source work, quotation, or character, and for a fictional character's catchphrase where the passages present a source work or series: in those settings answer the source work or series or the requested character or speaker as the clue indicates, rather than applying the real-person rule.
- When the clue asks for the speaker or a character associated with the quoted line, answer that character or speaker rather than the source work. Leave incomplete titles, missing-word cues, and explicit title requests to the existing quoted-phrase rules, which still require the conventional full title when the clue asks for the work.
- When a clue reports a distorted phrase, wordplay error, or misstatement attributed to a person and asks what was said or talked of, return the intended corrected phrase supplied by the passage, not the name or definition of the error type. Apply this only when the passage itself supplies the intended wording; otherwise keep the entity requested by the clue.
- When the entire clue is a bare work title or a line of verse and does not explicitly ask for the title, work, speaker, or character, answer the creator associated with that title or line rather than the title itself; keep existing title-source handling for explicit title requests and for quoted catchphrases that are not presented as a work, and leave incomplete titles and missing-word cues to the existing quoted-phrase rules, except that when the work title is labeled as a classic and a passage explicitly identifies the earlier source work it adapts, answer that source work rather than a creator. When the entire clue is only a song title, including a quoted song title, and does not explicitly ask for its composer, lyricist, performer, or the title itself, answer the musical, film, album, or other work that a passage explicitly identifies as containing that song rather than a creator; preserve that containing work's conventional title, do not substitute the song-title fragment or a related creator or performer, and if no containing work is identified, apply the existing bare-title handling.
