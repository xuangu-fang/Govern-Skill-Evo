# Desklamp-Conditioned Inspection

Use this procedure only when the task explicitly requires inspecting, examining, or using an object under or with a desklamp. For ordinary put, clean, or transfer tasks where no desklamp is required, do not spend steps searching for, turning on, or co-locating with a desklamp; follow the general retrieval, cleaning, and placement procedure below instead.

## Procedure

1. Locate the desklamp and one matching target object.
2. Establish co-location at the desklamp's receptacle before completing the task:
   - If the target is already on the desklamp's receptacle, remain there.
   - If the target and lamp are on different receptacles, pick up one candidate target and carry it to the desklamp, or place it at the desklamp's receptacle.
3. Turn on the desklamp.
4. Pick up the target there (or keep holding it) and perform the requested look/examine, or use the lamp, while the target is held or otherwise co-located at that receptacle.

## Constraints

- Ensure the desklamp is on and the target is held or otherwise co-located at that receptacle before finishing.
- Inspecting the receptacle alone is not a substitute for interacting with the requested object.
- Do not repeatedly toggle the lamp while the target is at a different receptacle.
- Do not move the target away after turning on the lamp.
- Do not shuttle multiple same-type candidates between locations; remain at the lamp and finish with one co-located target.

## General Non-Desklamp Retrieval, Cleaning, and Placement

For ordinary put, clean, or transfer tasks where no desklamp is required, use this procedure instead.

1. Identify and search for the exact requested target. If it is not visible, search systematically, inspecting locations and containers you have not yet examined rather than reworking locations you have already opened or seen to lack the target: open any closed cabinet, drawer, or fridge as soon as you reach it and inspect its contents before leaving. Include visible open surfaces (tables, countertops, shelves, sidetables, stoveburners, dresser, bed, and sofa tops) and less obvious listed receptacles, not only closed drawers or cabinets. Do not drop same-type coverage: after inspecting one receptacle type, still inspect the remaining unexamined receptacles of that type, as well as a different type, before returning to any location you have already inspected. Prefer any unexamined listed location or unopened container over returning to a known-empty location, and do not repeatedly check inventory while carrying nothing. If a location you want to reach is not available in the current admissible list, move on to a different unexamined admissible location instead of re-examining the spot you are already at. Continue until the exact target is found or all plausible candidate receptacles have been inspected once.
2. Manipulate only the exact requested object category; a similar category is not a substitute. A knife is not a butterknife, a pot is not a pan, and a cup is not a mug. When the exact requested object has not been observed, do not acquire, clean, cool, heat, or place a different but related object; for example, do not transfer a soapbottle when the task requests a dishsponge. If a container or surface holds only a non-matching or near-miss object, leave that object unmanipulated and continue searching for the exact target. Do not treat cleaning, cooling, heating, or placing a non-target object as progress toward the task; only the exact requested object satisfies the goal, and manipulating a substitute consumes steps without completing the task.
3. Acquire the exact target. If the task requires the object to be clean, carry it to an appropriate cleaning receptacle such as a sinkbasin and perform the clean action. If no cleaning is requested, skip the cleaning detour.
4. Go directly to the requested destination. Open it first if it is closed, then place or move the target there. Do not detour to unrelated receptacles or release the target before the requested placement is completed.
5. Before committing to a navigation or manipulation command, verify that the command is present in the current admissible action list; if it is not, do not issue it. When a desired navigation destination is absent but a direct action on that receptacle is listed, use that direct action if appropriate; otherwise choose a different admissible action that advances the search or task.

Keep the successful search, acquisition, and placement behavior used in non-desklamp transfer tasks.
