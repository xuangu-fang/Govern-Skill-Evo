# Desklamp-Conditioned Inspection

Use this procedure only when the task explicitly requires inspecting, examining, or using an object under or with a desklamp. For ordinary put, clean, or transfer tasks where no desklamp is required, do not spend steps searching for, turning on, or co-locating with a desklamp; follow the general retrieval, cleaning, and placement procedure below instead.

## Procedure

1. Locate the desklamp and one matching target object.
2. Establish co-location at the desklamp's receptacle before completing the task:
   - If the target is already on the desklamp's receptacle, remain there.
   - If the target and lamp are on different receptacles, pick up one candidate target and carry it to the desklamp, or place it at the desklamp's receptacle.
3. Turn on the desklamp.
4. Pick up the target there (or keep holding it) and perform the requested look/examine, or use the lamp, while the target is held or otherwise co-located at that receptacle.

## Constraints

- Ensure the desklamp is on and the target is held or otherwise co-located at that receptacle before finishing.
- Inspecting the receptacle alone is not a substitute for interacting with the requested object.
- Do not repeatedly toggle the lamp while the target is at a different receptacle.
- Do not move the target away after turning on the lamp.
- Do not shuttle multiple same-type candidates between locations; remain at the lamp and finish with one co-located target.

## General Non-Desklamp Retrieval, Cleaning, and Placement

For ordinary put, clean, or transfer tasks where no desklamp is required, use this procedure instead.

1. Identify and search for the exact requested target. If it is not visible at the start, search all plausible receptacle categories, not only closed cabinets and drawers: also inspect open surfaces (coffeetable, diningtable, sidetable, dresser top, countertop, sofa, armchair, ottoman) and appliances (fridge, microwave, stoveburner, sinkbasin, toaster, coffeemachine), because targets are frequently placed plainly visible on a surface or in an appliance. Inspect each candidate receptacle on first arrival (open it and read its contents) and prefer a candidate you have not yet observed over one you have already seen empty. Continue this first pass until the exact target, or the required number of target instances for a multi-target task, is located, or until all reachable candidate receptacles have been inspected once. Do not repeatedly check inventory while carrying nothing.
2. Avoid re-walking known-empty receptacles. After inspecting a receptacle that visibly lacks the exact target, do not re-open, re-examine, or re-walk that same receptacle when another candidate remains; choose a next destination other than the receptacle you just left whose contents you have not yet seen, using the current and previous observations rather than assuming you remember every receptacle visited. Do not cycle back and forth among the same small set of receptacles; before making any second pass over a container cluster, complete one inspection of the remaining untested receptacle types or instances. If the current and previous observation both show you at an already-open receptacle that lacks the exact target, treat that as a stuck-search signal: move to a different untried receptacle, including an uninspected unopened drawer or cabinet, an uninspected surface, or an uninspected appliance, instead of re-examining a known-empty location or repeating the loop.
3. Manipulate only the exact requested target category. Do not pick up, clean, or place a substitute object when the exact target is absent; for example, do not transfer a soapbottle when the task requests a dishsponge. If the exact target has not yet been found, continue the systematic search rather than completing the transfer with a non-target object.
4. Acquire the exact target. If the task requires the object to be clean, carry it to an appropriate cleaning receptacle such as a sinkbasin and perform the clean action. If no cleaning is requested, skip the cleaning detour.
5. Go directly to the requested destination. Open it first if it is closed, then place or move the target there. Do not detour to unrelated receptacles or release the target before the requested placement is completed.

Keep the successful search, acquisition, and placement behavior used in non-desklamp transfer tasks.
