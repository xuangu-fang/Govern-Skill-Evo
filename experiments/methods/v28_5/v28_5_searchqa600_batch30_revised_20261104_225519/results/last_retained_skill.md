# Question Answering Skill

## Learned Rules

- When a clue describes a figure through paired film-title epithets or role labels of the form "in [year] he was a [role]", identify the character or title figure indicated by those labels and return that character/work name; do not substitute the actor or performer unless the clue explicitly asks for the performer or another person.
- When the clue is a bare list of names or character labels without an explicit question, infer that the requested answer is the work, series, or title containing them and return that title alone; do not answer with a sentence describing the characters, their role, or the relationship among the names.
- Keep the answer unit no larger than the requested referent. If the clue already supplies a family name or category for a person, return only the distinguishing given name or name component and do not append the already-given family/category. When the requested answer is an organization, term, role, or label and the passages offer alternative names, return one established name form—the single label that the most directly defining passage uses as its subject or headword—and do not return an acronym plus a parenthetical spelled-out expansion, a disjunctive pair, or another alternative label unless the question explicitly asks for all names or all possible alternatives; if the passage marks one term as usual or primary, use that one and do not append secondary synonyms. Preserve a distinguishing qualifier or necessary additional name component only when needed to distinguish the requested referent.
- Subject to the requested answer unit, for a person answer, use the name form that the directly relevant passage uses as the person's subject label or common designation; prefer a familiar or nickname form when the passage uses it as that common designation, and do not expand a shorter established name to a fuller formal name. Preserve a full conventional first-and-last name when the requested unit allows it and that is the established subject form or is needed to identify the requested person. Do not apply the honorific/title-preservation preference to a military or civil rank or office: do not prefix such a rank/office (for example, "Lieutenant Colonel" or "President") merely because the passage places it before the name; omit it unless the clue asks for the title/office itself or it is needed to distinguish two people with the same name. A social honorific such as "Sir" is not a rank/office for this purpose: preserve it when the directly supporting passage uses it for the answering person, and do not add an honorific the passage does not use.
- If a clue requests a place, business type, or other category and a passage supplies a containing institution, organization, or descriptive phrase (e.g., "University of [place]" or "[category] Alliance"), answer with the contained place/category, not the full institution/organization or a general summary; retain any distinguishing qualifier. When the clue itself supplies the answer's category or container (for example "this island", "this city", or "this gift company") and the distinctive proper name alone identifies the requested entity in the passages, return that distinctive name and omit the supplied category or any appended corporate/product descriptor. Preserve a category word or qualifier when it is an inseparable part of the conventional name and the modifier alone would not identify the referent, or when it is needed to distinguish the requested entity from another similarly named entity.
- If a clue asks what material comes from "these animals" or a similar source category and passages distinguish a primary or preferred source from secondary or occasional alternatives, answer only the primary or preferred source; omit parenthetical alternatives unless the question explicitly asks for all possible sources, but keep a needed plural or qualifier if the primary answer is inherently plural.
- When the clue says "one of these [plural noun]" and asks for the thing or type, return a singular instance or singular type rather than echoing the plural; when the clue asks for a generic natural phenomenon or event type, use its singular dictionary label even if the clue uses a plural demonstrative. Keep a plural only when the answer denotes multiple members or a collective group such as a people or an animal category, or when the question explicitly asks for multiple items.
- If a supplied passage gives the exact clue followed by a short answer gloss or field, use that short form rather than expanding it from other passages when it unambiguously identifies the requested referent; do not substitute a neighboring entity or adopt a form ambiguous with another entity in the context.
