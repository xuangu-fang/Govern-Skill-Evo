# Question Answering Skill

(No learned rules yet. Rules will be added through the reflection process.)
- Keep the answer element to the shortest conventional unit that identifies the requested referent: do not append parenthetical code names or aliases, explanatory phrases, secondary alternatives, or an extra generic category noun when the clue already supplies the category. When the clue supplies the answer class ('this [class]', 'these [class]', 'term for ...', or a demonstrative pointing to the class), return only the distinguishing word(s) or exact established name that completes the clue; drop a generic class noun only when the clue supplies it and the remaining term still identifies the referent. Do not add a subtype adjective or descriptive phrase unless the narrower form is needed to distinguish the referent, and keep a class word that is an integral part of the established proper name.
- When the clue supplies a descriptive modifier for the requested answer, return only the distinguishing word(s) or established name that completes the clue; do not repeat that adjective or add an unnecessary subtype qualifier unless the modifier is an integral part of the established name. When the requested answer is a work title presented with a subtitle after a colon, return the main title alone unless the clue explicitly asks for the subtitle or full title, the main title alone would not identify the work, or the exact delimiter-adjacent unit rule applies. Preserve articles when they are part of the conventional identifying form; do not shorten a full personal name or plural form below the granularity the clue requests or in a way that changes the referent.
- When a clue already identifies the referent as a party or organization and asks what it was nicknamed, return the distinctive nickname words alone. Do not append the already-supplied category noun ('party', 'organization') merely because the full nickname often appears with it or forms a familiar proper name; notwithstanding the general proper-name exception stated earlier, keep that noun only when the clue asks for the full formal name or the nickname would not identify the referent without it. When a clue already names a person and asks for that person's nickname or moniker, return the distinctive moniker alone rather than appending the already-supplied name; keep a moniker as the full answer only when it is itself the conventional identifying name and the clue supplies no separate name.
- When the question is only a fragment (a bare name or place, a list of names, or a quoted phrase/title with missing words), treat it as an incomplete clue: use the passages to identify the type of answer the clue calls for and return that missing unit, not the fragment itself or a sentence describing it; however, when a fill-in clue presents a joint entity as '___ & [Name]' under a plural partnership or group label (for example, 'Publishing partners:___ & Wagnalls'), return the complete established joint name supported by the passages, including the named partner, rather than only the word filling the blank; do not apply this to a single-word blank inside an official title or an ordinary paired phrase where the clue asks only for the omitted word. When the entire question is only a complete quoted literary line or passage or a bare work title (at most with a year), with no visibly omitted word or name and no wording explicitly asking for the work itself, return the creator (author, director, or equivalent) whom the passages consistently identify, rather than the work title or quoted words. Except that a bare year-plus-title label presented as a classic, version, adaptation, or inspired-by work, or a bare quoted song title that the passages consistently identify as a number from a named musical, film, or other show, returns the source work, not the creator(s) or the songwriters, composers, or performers, unless the clue explicitly asks who created or directed the work or who wrote, composed, or performed the song (for example, 'Romeo and Juliet' for a 1961 'West Side Story' clue). Do not apply the song-title exception to a bare literary quotation whose target is the speaker or author. A fragment with an omitted word or name still follows the fragment rule above. If the passages identify the work but do not consistently identify a creator, or the clue explicitly asks for the title or work, return the work unit instead.
- When the clue is a literary quotation introduced by a named speaker or character and does not explicitly ask who wrote, created, or spoke the line, return the work from which the quotation comes; do not return the author or the named speaker merely because a passage also identifies that person. If the clue explicitly asks for the author, creator, or speaker, return that person; if no speaker is attributed and no wording asks for the work, the bare-literary-line creator rule above remains available.
- When a fragment names a tribute or cover band and the passages identify the original act, return that original act's name alone; do not append 'tribute band', 'cover band', or a descriptive relationship to the answer.
- For a list of names, when the passages show a shared state, country, or location that links them, return that shared unit alone; do not answer with an interpersonal relation or a descriptive phrase that embeds the location. For a bare place name that is a sub-location (such as a city, neighborhood, or district), return the containing state or country when the passages support it as the shared or containing unit; but when the fragment is an abbreviation or shortened form of the place itself, expand it to the full place name rather than substituting its containing state or country.
- When a quoted title or phrase appears as part of a larger clue, determine the answer role requested by the rest of the clue and use the title or phrase as a clue to that role, including a name or word embedded in it; do not select another person or same-titled work merely because it co-occurs in a passage. For a quoted phrase with an omitted leading word or name, supply the missing word or name rather than returning the visible phrase.
- When quoted or paraphrased wording is accompanied by multiple biographical constraints such as an office and a state, select the person who satisfies all of the constraints; do not return a different person merely because that person is quoted near the wording or shares one attribute. If the clue explicitly asks who made a quoted statement, the speaker remains the target.
- When the answer is a personal name, choose the conventional form that matches the clue's requested granularity: if the clue asks for a first name, return only that first name; otherwise, if the clue does not itself supply the person's name and a direct identifying passage uses a title or honorific as part of the established name, retain that title or honorific. Do not add a surname or descriptor already supplied by the clue, and do not add a location qualifier, middle initial, or fuller legal-name form solely because a passage also displays it, unless the clue itself requires that component, the shorter form would not identify the referent or would be ambiguous among passage candidates, or the component is an integral part of a requested title or proper name. Genuinely distinguishing components and integral titles or honorifics remain, but a fuller name form that appears only in a passage is not added when the clue already gives or asks for the shorter form.
- Preserve grammatical number, except that when the clue frames the answer as 'one of these [plural noun]' or 'one of the ... [plural noun]' and asks for a single example, return the singular noun form, for example 'a sinkhole' for 'water-filled one of these holes'. Also, when the clue uses 'these' for a class of product, substance, or category and the passages present the answer as a singular mass noun or singular established category label, return that singular or mass form rather than pluralizing it. Apart from those exceptions, keep a plural answer when the clue asks for multiple entities or when the direct supporting passage presents the requested item as a count noun in plural; do not apply the singular/mass exception to an ordinary count noun that the clue asks as a class. Preserve genuinely identifying proper-name components and the conventional form of the direct supporting term when the clue does not require a different or shorter form; shortening must not change which entity or relation is requested. When the clue does not itself require or supply a shorter form, use the conventional name form presented by the direct supporting passage.
- When a clue asks for the source or origin of a measured thing (for example, energy coming from an object and measured by an instrument), answer the source entity, not the measured quantity, radiation type, or measuring process. Preserve the relation requested by the wording rather than substituting an intermediate property from the passage.
- When a clue defines an item by its function, return the general term or category defined, not a specific example substance or remedy mentioned in a passage.
- When a clue describes an organism cutting a material and then uses a relative clause for what it grows on that material, return the cultivated organism, not the material or substrate.
- When a clue quotes a distorted or malapropian phrase and the passages give the intended wording, return the intended phrase, not the name of the error type.
- When the clue itself supplies a person's first name and asks for the person by another identifying fact, return the surname or remaining identifying component alone; do not repeat the supplied first name or add middle initials in the answer (for example, 'Arthur' for a clue naming Chester; 'Clark' for a clue asking which 2004 Democratic contender won the Silver and Bronze Stars).
- When the clue does not supply any part of the person's name, use the conventional identifying form presented by the direct supporting passage. If direct passages present both a bare personal name and a form with a true honorific conventionally attached to that name (such as 'Sir', 'Dame', or 'Lord'), use the honorified conventional name rather than the bare first-and-last form. Do not add an honorific that the direct passages do not attach to the name, and do not add an occupational, political, military, or similar office title (such as 'Senator', 'President', or 'General') merely because it appears beside the name; keep such a title only when the clue asks for that titled form or the title is integral to the established identifying name. Do not add alternate spellings the direct supporting passages do not use.
 - When the clue asks for a relative with a possessive phrase such as 'this relative of his/hers', return the matching possessive pronoun plus the relation rather than the bare relation, and do not append the person's name or other explanatory detail.
- Notwithstanding the general conventional-form rule, when a passage reproduces the clue and immediately supplies an answer after a delimiter such as '|', return the complete delimiter-adjacent unit exactly as printed, including any comma-separated place or identifying qualifier; do not shorten it to the first element and do not omit the qualifier under the general preference against adding location details, and do not expand it with a fuller name form found elsewhere in the passages, even when the fuller form is a conventional proper name; the complete delimiter-adjacent unit is the intended answer granularity for that clue. Apply this exact-unit rule only to the delimiter-adjacent answer, not to material drawn from other passages.
- When direct supporting passages render a proper name without diacritics, preserve that unaccented spelling rather than adding diacritics from outside the provided context.
- When a bare quoted political campaign slogan names both a presidential candidate and a running mate, return the presidential candidate, the head of the ticket, as the identifying answer, not both names and not the running mate alone, unless the clue explicitly asks for the ticket or for both people. Do not treat a campaign slogan as a literary title whose creator is the target.
- When the entire clue is only a title plus a personal name, such as 'President [Name]' or the equivalent, and the passages identify the country or institution associated with that title, return that country or institution as the concise missing unit; do not return the person's full office description, dates, or a biographical sentence.
- When a geographic clue is only a named natural feature followed by a containing region, or a descriptive fragment locating such a feature, and the clue does not explicitly ask for that feature or site by class, return the broader containing geographic unit supported by the passages rather than a superlative property, descriptive phrase, or more specific park/forest name: if the clue already names the containing state or region (but not the country), return the country that contains it; if the clue names only the feature or site, return the containing state (or the country when no containing state is supported). Keep the specific feature or sub-location when the clue explicitly requests that class or site, and still expand an abbreviation or shortened form of the place itself rather than substituting its container.
- When a clue states that a company or provider was founded or used to provide something for these [descriptive plural items], answer the described item requested after 'for these', not the company or provider that appears as the grammatical subject. If the clue instead explicitly asks for the company, provider, or buyer/seller, return that requested entity.
- Match the submitted unit to the category or naming form the clue supplies. When a clue refers to items by a named example or examples and asks for the general category they belong to, return the plain category rather than adding a property or subtype inferred from the examples. When the clue calls the target a generic institutional or corporate category (such as 'this university/univ.' or 'this company') and identifies a specific organization by a ticker, abbreviation, or other distinguishing description, return the distinctive root or brand name alone when it uniquely identifies the referent; omit supplied generic class words and formal organizational descriptors or suffixes (such as 'Company', 'Inc.', or 'Corp.'), but retain an explicitly requested full formal name, any component needed to distinguish the referent, and an integral part of the established identifying name that is not merely a generic class or formal suffix. When the clue calls the target a department, use the established short '[Name] Department' order when the passages present it and the clue does not ask for the full official name. When the clue supplies the relation or rate, do not append that supplied relation or add an unnecessary subtype qualifier. Do not strip a required class noun when the distinguishing word is an adjective and the bare adjective is not an established name for the entity; retain the class noun in the grammatical number supported by the clue. Preserve genuinely distinguishing proper-name components and integral class words.
