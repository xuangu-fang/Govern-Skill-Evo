# Question Answering Skill

## Answer Selection and Form

- For a single requested answer, keep the `answer` element to the concise term supplied by the context. Do not append parenthetical glosses, acronym expansions, alternate names, test codenames, or explanatory synonyms unless the question explicitly asks for multiple labels or details, or the extra material is necessary to distinguish the requested entity.
- When a context passage restates the clue and immediately appends a short answer after a clear separator (such as a vertical bar, colon, or dash), use that supplied short form as the `answer`; do not replace it with a fuller name or elaboration assembled from other passages, and keep the labeled form when the labeled item is itself a full name. This supplied labeled form controls the answer's length, except that an honorific or title that the context presents as part of the person's established name is retained under the existing person-name rule.
- When the clue asks for a general class or kind, what something is, what it is like, or what a foreign-language term means, and the context supplies both a basic category noun and a descriptive variant such as a species or subtype, or a variant carrying an extra type, material, color, geographic, or national modifier, answer the basic category noun alone unless the clue explicitly asks for that variant or for a specific named occurrence, or the basic noun alone would be ambiguous. Do not promote a species mentioned in a related passage into the requested category, and do not retain an extra descriptor merely because the identifying passage writes the fuller phrase. This includes clues asking for the term for a described phenomenon: answer the general term rather than a named specific occurrence.
- For common-noun answers, retain the grammatical number supported by the clue and context: use the plural category form when the context names the requested category with a plural noun and the clue treats it as a category; use the singular when the clue points to one instance or the context supplies only a singular form.
- When a clue asks for 'the term' or 'this is the term' for a class of materials or objects, and the context supplies a singular form of the noun for the requested class (with or without an article), answer the singular common noun even if the defining sentence or another context sentence uses the plural; keep the plural only when the clue itself treats the requested category as plural, such as 'these people' or 'these materials.' This specific number instruction controls over the general common-noun grammatical-number preference above when the clue asks for 'the term' for such a class.
- When the answer is a person's name and the context introduces or presents that person with a genuine honorific such as `Sir` as part of the established name, include it even if later mentions use only the surname or a bare given-name-plus-surname. When the clue identifies the person by an event, office, or date and the passage containing that same identifying event presents the person as `Sir [Given Name Surname]`, retain `Sir` even if other passages use only the surname or a bare given-name-plus-surname, and do not treat such a separate bare mention as triggering the civil, political, military, or noble-title omission described later in this bullet. Do not add `Sir` when the clue explicitly asks for a given name, surname, or bare form, or when the context never presents it as part of the person's name. Do not add a separate occupational descriptor such as a rank or office unless the clue requests it. When the context also supplies the same person's bare personal name in ordinary prose, use that bare name and omit a preceding civil, political, military, or noble title or rank (such as `Sen.` or `Count`) even when the context writes it alongside the name, unless the clue explicitly asks for the titled or formal name; this omission takes precedence over the honorific-inclusion preference, but a genuine honorific such as `Sir` that the context presents as part of the established personal name is still retained. Do not let a civil, political, military, or noble title or rank in a headline, caption, or parenthetical override the bare identifying form.
- For a person answer, subject to the title and rank omission rule above, use the name form that the context's identifying passage uses for that person when that passage directly answers the clue, and do not add a middle name or middle initial, expand a given name, or otherwise substitute a fuller personal-name variant that the context does not use for that person, unless the question explicitly asks for the full legal or formal name. When the context supplies both a formal full-name opening and an ordinary given-name-plus-surname form in the identifying passage, use the ordinary form rather than adding middle names; honorifics or titles presented as part of the established name remain governed by the existing person-name rule, and the existing rule against dropping required given names still applies.
- As an exception to the ordinary given-name preference above, when the clue identifies a person only by a specific event and date, with no personal name in the clue, and one passage states that same event and date using a formal given-name variant while other passages use a nickname, answer the formal variant from the event-matching passage. Do not apply this exception to clues that already supply a name or when the formal variant is not tied to the matching event.
- When the clue already states the entity's category or role (for example an island, city, company, or empire) and the context names the same entity by a distinctive proper-name element alone or as the identifying part of a compound (for example `Hallmark` for Hallmark Cards or `Roanoke` for Roanoke Island), answer with that distinctive name and omit an optional category word or corporate suffix, unless the full form is the only unambiguous name or the question explicitly asks for a full formal or legal name; do not drop given names or other distinguishing modifiers from personal names unless a clue restatement or labeled answer supplies a shorter established form as the requested answer, while retaining any honorific or title presented as part of the established name.
- For a publication, when the identifying passage gives its name with a generic publication noun as part of that name, keep that noun in the answer rather than reducing the publication to its distinctive personal or brand element. This applies to supplied publication names, not to optional category descriptions that are not part of the name, and it controls over the general category-stripping preference above.
- When a clue already supplies the answer's category noun (for example `this market` or `this vitamin`) and the context gives a compound formed from that category noun plus a distinguishing term, answer with the distinguishing term alone (for example `bear` or `C`) rather than repeating the category. Keep the full compound when it is an established proper name or title (for example `South Sea Bubble`), when the category noun is an integral part of a person, work, or institution name rather than a generic category word the clue supplies, when the shorter term would be ambiguous, or when the question asks for the full formal or technical label. When the context names the entity with a distinguishing acronym or proper element plus the same generic category noun the clue supplies (for example `SCTV` from `SCTV Network` when the clue asks for a comic `network`), answer the distinguishing element alone unless the clue asks for the formal organization name, the extra word is needed to distinguish the entity from another named entity, or the publication-name rule above requires retaining it; do not treat the mere repetition of the clue's category noun as an established title requiring retention. This refining rule limits any labeled-answer preference that would otherwise repeat a category already present in the clue.
- When a clue explicitly asks for a nickname or informal label rather than a formal organization name, answer the nickname's distinguishing phrase and omit a category noun, such as a political-party word, that the clue already uses for the entity; retain the category noun only when the clue asks for the formal name or the shorter nickname would be ambiguous.
- When a clue mentions a relative of a named work's creator and links that work to the relative, resolve the possessive to the work's creator and answer the creator; do not answer the relative who appears only as a biographical detail unless the clue explicitly asks for the relative.
- When a work is introduced with a primary title plus a translated, parenthetical, or alternate release title, answer the primary title used in the identifying passage and omit the secondary translation or alternate title unless the clue asks for the full release title, a supplied labeled answer uses the fuller form, or the primary title alone would be ambiguous. Keep a subtitle when the context presents it as part of the work's established title.
- For multiword proper names, preserve the spelling, spacing, and hyphenation used in the context's ordinary identifying prose; do not add a hyphen that appears only in a headline or a derived adjective, and when both spaced and hyphenated variants appear, use the spaced form unless the identifying sentence consistently establishes the hyphenated form.
- When a clue describes a title, role, or character rather than asking for a performer, answer the described character or role, not an actor or other associated person. Return the performer only when the clue explicitly asks who played or performed the role.
- When a clue contains a deictic phrase such as 'seen here' or 'shown here' but no image is actually present, select the named item in the passage whose stated description directly matches the clue's remaining predicate rather than an item that is merely mentioned repeatedly or appears in a document title. If several candidates match, prefer the passage where the clue predicate and the candidate occur in the same sentence. Answer with the specific item noun from that matching description; if the clue already names the brand or maker, omit that brand or maker rather than repeating it, and use the singular when the clue points to one shown item.
- When the question consists only of a short quoted phrase or a title with no interrogative, and the phrase identifies a named work, series, character, or other entity rather than a personal-name or place-name clue governed by the shared-jurisdiction rules below, answer with that named work, series, character, or entity; do not answer with a descriptive definition, genre summary, or a sentence about a related adaptation.
- When a question with no interrogative consists only of one or more place names (possibly joined by 'and' or '&') or an 'in [places]' phrase, and the context identifies those places as cities in a single country, answer the associated country or a context-supported adjectival demonym for that country rather than repeating a city name; use the country name for a bare place-name clue and the adjectival demonym when the clue is an 'in [places]' phrase, unless the clue explicitly asks for the city, a place-specific fact, or no single country is supported. Preserve explicit city, region, and institution requests, and do not add a country to an answer that already names the requested entity.
- When a question with no interrogative consists only of one or more personal names, or a title plus personal name, and the context identifies the person or people through a country, constituency, or other shared jurisdiction, answer that jurisdiction or shared affiliation rather than a phrase describing an office or relationship; for a single monarch or titled person, give the associated country, not an office phrase. If no single shared jurisdiction or constituency is supported for multiple names, give the shortest directly supported common affiliation.
