# Question Answering Skill

- When a clue pairs a role or category with a quoted title or phrase and the passages split the candidates—one satisfying the stated role/category and another also connected to the quoted work—resolve the clue by requiring both the stated role/category and an overlap between the candidate's own name and the quoted phrase; do not treat a candidate's mere appearance in or association with the quoted work as the required overlap. If the question asks for the performer in a named work or for a formal title rather than a role-plus-phrase match, answer that requested entity instead.
- When the question is only a quoted phrase fragment marked by a leading or trailing ellipsis or blank, treat it as a completion request: find a passage that presents a complete title or fixed phrase containing the fragment and answer the omitted word or words that complete it, not another person, work, or item merely associated with the visible fragment. Prefer the completion whose omitted portion is a proper name and whose passage gives the full phrase as a title or heading; among multiple such completions, prefer one whose full phrase matches the quoted fragment's capitalization, spacing, and punctuation as printed, and do not select a completion that is only a performer or other person associated with a different title containing the fragment. If the question explicitly asks for the full title, give the full title instead.
- When a question is a leading-ellipsis fragment that forms a descriptive relative clause beginning 'that' or 'which' rather than a title or fixed phrase, treat it as asking for the specific named subject the passage says has that property; answer the named place or entity rather than a generic material, object, or category from the same passage, even when the generic class is the immediate grammatical subject. For such a descriptive relative clause, this property reading takes precedence over the general leading-ellipsis completion rule; do not force the fragment into title completion unless a passage presents a title or fixed phrase containing it, in which case follow the completion rule.
- When the question consists only of a quoted phrase that matches a catchphrase or partial title and the passages associate it both with a derivative film or television work whose full title adds a decisive leading word and with the original series or work on which that derivative is explicitly based, answer the original series or work title when the quoted phrase omits that leading word; if the question asks for the derivative work or gives its full title, answer that work instead. This applies to a bare quoted phrase without a leading or trailing ellipsis or blank; if the question is an ellipsis- or blank-marked completion fragment, follow the completion rule instead.
- Subject to the completion and partial-title rules above and the dramatic-dialogue rule that follows, when the entire question is only a quoted literary line or a bare work title that is not presented as an adaptation or retelling, and no visible wording asks for the work itself, use the passage's attribution to answer the person credited with creating the quoted work—author or poet for a quoted literary line, director or other credited maker for a bare film title—rather than echoing the work title; if the question explicitly asks for the title/work or the passage identifies no such creator, answer the requested work or entity instead.
- When a quoted literary excerpt is introduced by a character or speaker label or is otherwise framed as dramatic dialogue, answer the named work from which the excerpt comes rather than the author or other creator, unless the clue explicitly asks for the author/creator or for a different requested entity.
- When the entire question is only a quoted song title and no visible wording asks for its composer, lyricist, performer, or full title, and a passage identifies it as a song from a named musical, film, or other source work, answer that source work's title rather than the song's creators; this source-work answer takes precedence over the general bare-work-title credited-creator rule; if no source work is identified, or the question explicitly asks for creators or the title, follow those requests or the existing credited-creator rule for quoted works.
- When the question is only a bare or truncated slogan, campaign phrase, or signature catchphrase and does not separately ask for a work, event, phrase, or list of participants, answer the person the passage credits as the primary speaker or campaign candidate rather than completing the omitted words, echoing the phrase, or listing all named ticket members; for a campaign phrase naming a candidate and running mate, use the candidate at the top of the ticket. Do not use this person-answer rule when the quoted phrase is a catchphrase or partial title that the passages connect to a derivative work and an explicitly named original series or work; in that case follow the catchphrase/partial-title rule and answer the original work instead. If the question marks a leading or trailing ellipsis or blank, uses a completion pointer such as 'this', or explicitly asks for the phrase or missing words, follow the completion request instead.
- When a clue asks for the unnamed class or series represented by named examples and does not itself name a narrower subtype, use the base category term that the examples are presented as instances of; do not add an incidental subtype or descriptive modifier from nearby context merely because that modifier also appears. Use a more specific subtype only when the question asks for that subtype or when the base term alone would be too broad or ambiguous. When a clue asks for an unnamed category with wording such as 'one of these' or uses or asks about a foreign-language term as a synonym, appositive, or definition for the target, and the context offers both a basic common-noun category and a longer compound, proper, or descriptive variant, answer the basic common noun alone unless the question explicitly asks for the specific variant, proper name, emblem, or the base term would be ambiguous; do not add descriptive or proper modifiers such as 'hot air' or 'White' merely because nearby context supplies them, even when a nearby passage prints that longer variant as a heading, emblem name, or exact term.
- When a clue asks for the category or type represented by one or more named examples using a plural demonstrative such as 'these, like [example]', answer the singular category or mass-noun term used in the defining passage for that example, not a pluralized version, unless the question explicitly asks for multiple items or a plural class label.
- When a clue already quotes or otherwise supplies a title suffix, place qualifier, or other part of a full name and asks for the group or designation denoted by the remaining portion, answer that remaining designation without repeating the supplied quoted portion; use the full title or name when the question asks for the work, book, song, or other entity whose title contains the quoted portion, when the question explicitly asks for the complete title, or when the remaining designation would be ambiguous. Do not apply the remaining-designation rule merely because the quoted portion is a title word.
- When the entire question is only a local place name and the passages introduce that place as located within a larger jurisdiction such as a state or country, answer the named state or country that the passage gives as the place's primary containing jurisdiction, preferring the state when both state and country are given, rather than restating the place name, a county, or a compound census-designated name; do not apply this when the question has relational wording that asks for a city, site, island, feature, or the place itself.
- When the entire question is only a place name followed by a containing state, province, or similar regional qualifier ('X in Y'), and a passage identifies a higher-level country containing that place, answer the country rather than repeating the supplied regional qualifier; for this supplied-region form, this country answer takes precedence over the general local-place rule's preference for the state when both state and country are given. Keep the state or province when the question explicitly asks for it or when no country is stated.
- When a question explicitly asks for a city within a named region, prefer a candidate that a passage explicitly predicates as a city of that region or as its extent boundary over a candidate that appears only in a parenthetical region, AVA, or nearby list without an explicit city relation; if the question asks for a sub-area or region rather than a city, answer that requested sub-area or region instead.
- When several name forms denote the same person, choose the form used in the passage whose statement most directly matches the clue's identifying event, date, or relation rather than a familiar nickname repeated in headings or background. If that statement gives a surname alone, use that surname rather than expanding it with a given name; if it gives a full given name plus surname, use that full form. Use a fuller conventional name when the direct answer, question, or disambiguation requires it, and do not drop an honorific that the passage presents as part of the established name merely to shorten the answer.
- When a clue asks for a person's nickname or moniker and the passages give a distinctive nickname alongside the person's name or surname, answer the nickname alone; do not append the surname or restate the person's full name unless the question asks for the person themselves rather than the nickname, or the nickname alone is ambiguous. When a phrase combines a nickname with the person's surname, such as 'Boss Tweed', treat the surname as part of the person's name and not as part of the moniker unless the passage explicitly presents that surname-including phrase itself as the moniker being requested; do not withhold a printed nickname merely because it appears next to the full name.
- When a clue asks for a company or brand and the context gives both a short established name and a longer version that adds only a generic corporate or product descriptor, answer with the short established name unless the descriptor is needed to distinguish the entity or the question asks for the formal company name or the service. If the clue already names the service or product category and the passage prints the brand followed by that same generic service or product phrase, answer the brand alone and do not treat the generic phrase as an established title; keep a format noun only when it is integral to a publication title or a named scale.
- When the entire question is only a company or brand name, including a surname used as a brand and especially with 'The', and the passages state its national origin but do not ask for a product, service, or formal corporate name, answer that named country of origin alone rather than the full corporate name or a product; if the question asks for the company, brand, product, or formal name, answer that requested entity instead.
- When a clue asks for the term or category of a phenomenon and already identifies the object or actor involved, answer with the general phenomenon term itself rather than appending the identified object as an occurrence qualifier; do not use this to suppress a legitimate subtype or type answer that the question actually requests, and give the full specific event name only when the question asks for that event name or the shorter term would be ambiguous.
- When a clue already supplies a generic feature type in its wording, treat that type as part of the clue, not as a request to repeat it: use the distinctive proper name without duplicating the supplied feature word when it unambiguously identifies the feature; a phrase such as 'this island' or 'this river' that merely identifies the target does not itself request the feature word. For this case, keep the feature word only when it is integral to the established name, needed for disambiguation, or explicitly requested as part of the answer.
- When a clue asks for a mountain range with wording such as 'these mountains' and the passages contain both an adjectival '<Name> Mountains' form and a distinct plural range name used as a noun, prefer the plural range name that remains after treating the supplied word 'mountains' as the clue's generic type; use the full '<Name> Mountains' form only when the question explicitly requests the formal range name or no distinct plural range name is printed.
- When a question asks for a type, kind, subcategory, or class within a named category, or asks what particular businesses or entities make up a named group, answer with the specific class or subtype term found in the context, not a broader umbrella label or the name of an organization or alliance formed from it. Keep the answer concise, and still give direct named-entity answers when no subtype slot is requested.
- When a definition-style clue asks for a type or class and already contains the generic head noun for that answer slot, and the context states the answer as a distinctive subtype term plus that same noun, use the distinctive subtype term alone rather than appending the generic head noun; this applies even when the clue describes the target with that noun in a phrase such as 'a house with...' and the subtype term alone still identifies the class. Keep the full phrase only when the generic word is integral to an established proper name or the question explicitly asks for the full name. This subtype preference does not apply when the generic unnamed-category rule above requires the basic common noun.
- When a clue already supplies a qualifier or adjective for the requested thing and the passage states the answer as that same qualifier plus a distinct head noun, answer the distinctive head noun alone if it unambiguously names the requested thing; do not repeat the clue's qualifier. Retain the full phrase when the qualifier is integral to the established name or the question asks for the full phrase, and do not strip a generic type word when that type word is part of the established answer name, such as a named scale or publication title.
- When a clue asks where a product or material comes from and the passages distinguish a usual, particularly used, or primary source from occasional alternatives, answer with the single most representative source term—the animal or item the evidence marks as especially or usually used—rather than appending occasional alternatives; a plural phrase such as 'these animals' does not by itself request a list. If the context gives no single primary item, give the short set of usual sources, but add occasional alternatives only when the question explicitly asks for all possible sources or for a stated number of distinct answers.
- When the requested answer is a source, category, or property already identified by a generic noun in the question, use the shortest complete term that fits the slot and avoid appending an unnecessary generic head noun merely because it appears nearby; use a longer noun phrase when omitting it would change the entity or leave the answer incomplete.
- When a clue asks for a single person or role and the context gives both a conventional name/term and a longer formal version with rank, occupation, honorific, or an 'A or B' alternative, answer with the single conventional name/term that directly fills the slot; omit separate rank, occupation, or office prefixes that are not part of the name, but keep an honorific that the passage presents as part of the person's established name, and do not append a second alternative unless the question explicitly asks for a formal title, multiple names, or the short form would be ambiguous. If the context marks one alternative as usual or first-listed, use that one. When the clue asks for a character or office and the context also names a performer or other associated person, answer the character/office, not the performer.
- When the context directly identifies a person with a plain first-and-last name and also supplies a longer form with a middle initial, middle initials, or an added middle name, use the directly stated plain form unless the question or a same-name ambiguity requires the fuller form; do not add middle names or initials merely because they are available, and keep any honorific or suffix that the passage presents as part of the established name.
- When a clue identifies a geographic feature through its relations and does not itself name the feature type in the answer slot, answer with the bare proper name used for that feature in the clue-bearing passage; add a generic feature word such as River, Island, or Park only when the question explicitly asks for that type or the bare name would be ambiguous. Keep the established type word when it is part of the named answer requested by the clue.
- When a landmark clue supplies an address and a distinctive historic feature, and the passages give both a historic or original name and a later corporate-sponsored or renamed name for the same landmark, answer the historic or original name associated with that feature rather than the later corporate name, unless the question asks for the current, corporate, or official name; do not apply this to a shorter common name that is not a later sponsor or renaming.
- When the clue identifies the target as a state university or parent institution and already supplies the campus location, answer with the parent institution name rather than repeating the campus qualifier; retain the campus-specific name only when the question asks for that campus or the parent name would identify a different institution.
- Except for the single-member category rule that follows and the plural-demonstrative category rule above, when a clue asks for a short term, category, color, or phrase that the context states directly, use that term's exact spelling, spacing/hyphenation, and grammatical number from the defining or directly answering statement; do not pluralize, singularize, hyphenate, or otherwise re-form it merely to match the clue's grammar, unless the context's own answer statement uses the other form. Apply this to common-noun category terms as well as fixed phrases. When the context presents multiple spelling or transliteration variants and the question asks for the term through a stated meaning or definition, use only the variant in the passage that explicitly states that meaning or definition; do not append another variant in parentheses or as an alternative merely because the context lists it elsewhere. Include multiple variants only when the question asks for alternative names or the single form would be ambiguous.
- When a clue frames the answer as one member of a plural common-noun category (for example, 'one of these ... holes') and the passage supplies the category term in plural, answer the singular common-noun form without an article; do not copy the plural form solely because the defining passage uses it, and do not apply this reduction when the question asks for the category as a plural set or the established answer is a plural proper name.
- When the question consists only of a comma-separated list of two or more named characters or people, answer with the shared work title or group name that the context associates with all of them, using the title/name as written; do not answer with a descriptive phrase about the group or only one listed name. If no shared title or group is stated but the context gives a shared proper affiliation such as a state, city, institution, or organization, answer with that shared affiliation alone rather than a descriptive phrase that combines it with their role; use a shared role/category only when no shared proper affiliation is stated.
- When the entire question is only a comma-separated list of two or more work titles, and the passages present those works as examples of a shared subject, era, conflict, or category, answer that shared base subject or category as stated by the passages rather than adding a format or genre subtype or answering only one title; use a subtype only when the question explicitly asks for genre or format or the base term would be ambiguous.
- When the entire question is only one personal name or an honorific-plus-name title and the context identifies that person through a named country, state, or institution, answer that proper affiliation alone; do not answer with the person's name, repeat an honorific or role word from the question, or add a descriptive class.
- When a clue asks for what a named group or party was nicknamed and the clue already supplies the generic class word, answer the distinctive nickname term alone rather than appending that class word; do not add the class word merely because the nickname is often printed with it, and give the fuller nickname or official name only when the question asks for the complete party name or the distinctive term would be ambiguous.
- When a clue asks for a film or other narrative work and the context gives a non-English title followed by a colon and an English translation or subtitle, answer the non-English short title alone; do not include the translated subtitle after the colon merely because it appears in the context heading. Give the full title, the English title, or the subtitle only when the question explicitly asks for it or the subtitle is needed to identify the work.
- When a clue describes an animal by general features and the context contains both a general common name and a more specific species label, answer the general common name alone unless the question explicitly asks for a particular species or proper species name, or the general name would be ambiguous; do not prepend a species modifier such as a habitat or descriptive label merely because it appears in a heading, a named example, or one passage.
- When the clue asks for the name given to a journal, magazine, or newspaper and the context states that publication's title with a format noun such as magazine or journal, include that format noun as part of the title even if the founder's surname alone is also used; do not answer with the bare surname or brand name when the clue asks for the publication's name and the context prints the title with a format noun. The short-company-name rule does not justify stripping a publication-format word when the requested entity is the publication itself.
- When a clue quotes a generic designator such as 'network' as part of its description and the context supplies both a short or acronym title and a longer award or formal title containing that designator, answer the short or acronym title alone unless the question explicitly asks for the exact formal or award title; the existence of the longer printed title does not by itself make the quoted designator part of the requested name. This does not apply when the requested entity is a journal, magazine, or newspaper and the context prints its title with a format noun; keep that format noun as part of the publication's name.
- When a clue's identifying description uses a specific action or relation phrase and the passages offer multiple candidate items in the same brand or category, prefer the candidate whose passage directly predicates that phrase of it; treat a paraphrase such as 'split open' or a different wording as not satisfying the clue when another candidate's passage uses the clue's exact phrase. Do not choose a candidate merely because it is the most familiar example or because a different action phrase appears near it.
- When a clue identifies an entity by a superlative or all-encompassing comparison such as 'greater than all other' or 'surpasses all other', and passages offer both an explicit all-encompassing superlative about one candidate and an ordinary comparative or mere shared word about another, answer the candidate with the explicit all-encompassing superlative; do not select a candidate merely because its passage repeats a word from the clue in an ordinary comparison. If a passage explicitly predicates the clue's named measure of a candidate, prefer that passage; use this rule only to resolve otherwise competing candidates.
- When a fill-in question supplies a category label and a blank embedded inside a multi-word proper name or firm name (for example, a blank joined to a remaining partner surname by an ampersand), and a passage prints the complete name, answer the complete printed name rather than only the word that fills the blank; answer only the missing component when the question explicitly asks for that component alone, and do not extend this rule to title suffixes or place qualifiers where the question asks only for the remaining designation.
- When a clue quotes an incomplete line or fixed phrase and follows the fragment with a completion pointer such as 'this', treat the pointer as asking for the next word or words that complete the quoted sequence; answer that exact continuation from the matching passage rather than the work title or author that surrounds the quotation, unless the question separately asks for the work title or author.
- When a clue names a tribute or cover act and the context identifies it as a tribute to or model of a named original group, answer the original group's name alone rather than a descriptive phrase such as '<original> tribute band'; if the question explicitly asks what kind of act it is, answer the descriptive category instead.
- When a clue consists of a work title with only a year or classic label and the passage explicitly identifies that work as an adaptation or retelling of another named work, answer the named source work that the passage presents as the underlying or original work; when an adaptation chain is described, do not stop at an intermediate adaptation that shares the title merely because it is named first, and when no visible wording asks for the work's makers, cast, or the titled work itself, give that underlying source work; do not answer the director or other credited makers merely because they appear in the same passage, and still answer the requested entity when the question explicitly asks for those makers or for the titled work, including when it explicitly calls the target a film or work and asks for that entity.
- When a clue asks for a character by an in-work alias, opening title, or noble title and the passage identifies the character by both that descriptor and a fuller canonical or regnal name, answer the fuller canonical or regnal name alone rather than a rank-plus-place descriptor; answer the office or title itself only when the question explicitly asks for the office or title.
- When a clue defines an item by its function and a passage directly names a general class or product category that performs that function, answer that directly named class or category rather than a specific brand, natural remedy, or example that is merely described nearby as having one relevant property; use the specific example only when the question asks for a particular product or remedy or no general class is stated.
- When the question's identifying description already supplies a person's given name and asks for that person, answer with the surname alone if the passage gives it, rather than repeating the supplied given name or adding middle names; use the fuller name only when the question explicitly asks for the full name or the surname alone would be ambiguous. This specific shortcut overrides the general preference for the name form used in the direct passage only when the question itself supplies the given name; keep any honorific or suffix that the passage presents as part of the established name, and do not apply this shortcut to a historical figure conventionally known by a single name.
- When the question asks for the painter or visual artist of a named artwork and a passage that ties that artwork to the artist uses the surname alone, even if a fuller name also appears elsewhere, answer the surname alone unless the question explicitly asks for the artist's full name or the surname would be ambiguous. This specific artwork-relation shortcut overrides the general preference for the name form used in the direct passage only for that surname-alone relation, and it does not justify stripping a surname when only a fuller name is printed.
- When a personal name is printed in the directly answering passage without diacritics and the question does not use diacritics, preserve that unaccented printed spelling rather than adding accents or normalizing it to a more conventional accented form.
- When the directly answering passage identifies a historical figure by a single conventional personal name and also supplies or nearby gives a longer given-name-plus-surname form, answer the single conventional name unless the question asks for the full formal name or the single name would be ambiguous.
- When a definition-style clue asks for a unit or term that a passage presents as a dictionary-style headword, take the answer's spelling and hyphenation from that headword, adjusting only grammatical number to the clue, rather than copying a differently spaced or punctuated variant from the definition sentence.
- When a fill-in blank sits inside an official title, committee name, institution name, or similar multi-word designation and the question already supplies the surrounding title words, answer only the missing component rather than restating the complete official name; keep the complete printed name only when the question explicitly asks for the full name. This is a specific limit on the existing complete-name preference for fill-in blanks, which remains available for firm names whose blank is joined to a partner surname by an ampersand.
- When the question already supplies a generic institutional or facility type word for the requested entity, such as a mint or similar type, and the passage gives a proper name plus that type, answer the distinctive proper name alone unless the type word is integral to the established name or the question asks for the full official name.
- When the question asks for a cabinet department or similar named institution and the context supplies both a formal 'Department of X' form and an established shorter 'X Department' form, use the shorter '<X> Department' form unless the question explicitly asks for the formal name or the shorter form would be ambiguous; do not expand a directly answering shorter departmental name into a Department-of phrase.
- When a passage gives a television series both a short title and a longer formal title that adds a location or descriptive phrase, and the question asks what the show is without requesting the full formal title, answer the short title used for the work rather than the longer printed title; give the longer title only when the question asks for the full title or the short title would be ambiguous.
- When the clue already supplies a generic object or body-part designation for the answer slot, such as a gland or missile, and a passage states the answer as a distinctive subtype or proper name plus that same designation, answer the distinctive subtype or proper name alone rather than repeating the supplied designation; retain the full phrase only when it is an established work or publication title, when the question itself explicitly asks for the full or official name, or when the bare term would be ambiguous. Do not strip a type word when it is integral to a conventional geographic or publication proper name, and do not treat a passage's capitalized '<Name> missile' or '<Name> gland' compound as an official-name request when the clue only supplies the generic designation and asks for the named item.
- When the clue's only identifying relation is that a highest mount or peak shares its name with a country, and the question does not separately ask for the mountain's name or the full 'Mount <Name>' form, answer the shared country name alone rather than the 'Mount <Name>' form; give the requested mountain name only when the question separately asks which mountain or peak has that property or explicitly asks for its full name.
- When the entire question is only a comma-separated list of two or more named people and the passages present them as spouses, ex-spouses, or comparable relations to one common named person, answer that common person's name alone rather than a possessive role phrase such as '<person>'s wives'; this takes precedence over answering with a shared role/category when the passages identify the common person, but shared work titles or shared proper affiliations remain preferred when the passages establish those.
- When the clue asks for a relative and explicitly frames the answer with a possessive relation such as 'this relative of his/hers', include the corresponding possessive pronoun with the kinship term when the answering passage states it, rather than returning the bare kinship noun; omit the pronoun only when the question asks for the relation category alone or the passage does not supply it.
- When a shared work title is printed in the context both with diacritics and without, and the question's spelling uses no diacritics, answer the unaccented printed form rather than adding accents from another occurrence; use the accented form only when the question explicitly asks for it or the accented form is the only printed title.
- When the question consists of a place or region label, optionally introduced by a locative preposition such as 'in', followed by a colon and an uppercase word or short phrase that can be rearranged into a place name within that region, treat it as an anagram clue and answer the rearranged place name alone rather than a literal passage term; do not apply this when the wording explicitly asks for the literal word or no plausible place name is formed.
- For a color adjective covered by the qualifier rule above, do not retain the color merely because the passage capitalizes it as a flavor, menu, or product label; keep the color only when it distinguishes the item from another same-noun candidate or is part of an explicitly requested full name.
- When a clue asks for a sport by the generic word 'sport' and a passage names a sport with a preceding medium or subtype adjective, such as 'ice hockey', answer the distinctive sport term alone, dropping that adjective, unless the question asks for the full or official sport name or the shorter term would be ambiguous with another sport. Do not retain the medium adjective merely because it appears in a passage heading; for a clue that only asks for the sport, this overrides the general preference for a more specific subtype.
- When a clue asks for an animal by a role or property such as 'this predator' and several animal names appear, prefer a candidate whose answering passage explicitly states that it is that predator or has that property over a candidate whose passage only names it as a species, prey, or nearby comparison; do not select a color-variant animal merely because a color word from the clue appears in its passage. Use another candidate only when the question explicitly asks for that color variant or the role passage identifies a different animal.
- When a clue supplies a gerund before 'this type of [noun]' and a passage presents the whole as a fixed expression rather than a simple type label, such as 'turning state's evidence', answer the full printed expression, including the supplied gerund, when the passage directly defines that expression; do not treat the supplied gerund as a removable qualifier. Give only the noun phrase when the question explicitly asks for the category or term by itself and no fixed expression is presented; this fixed-expression rule takes precedence over the general preference for dropping a supplied modifier.
- When the answer is a personal name containing initials, preserve the punctuation and spacing of the compact printed name form used in the passage that directly supports the answer; do not insert spaces between initials or between an initial and a surname when that passage prints them joined, for example keeping 'W.C. Fields' rather than 'W. C. Fields'. Use a spaced or expanded form only when the question or a same-name ambiguity requires it.
- When several printed forms denote the same mountain or other geographic feature, prefer the form used in the passage whose statement most directly supplies the clue's distinctive relation, such as an observatory located on that mountain; do not expand a compact 'Mt.' form to 'Mount' when the observatory-bearing statement prints 'Mt. <Name>'. Use the full printed form when the question asks for the full official name or the compact form would be ambiguous; for this name-form choice, the direct-relation passage's printed form takes precedence over the general bare-feature-name preference.
