# Question Answering Skill

- When a question is only a bare list of names or short phrases with no interrogative, and the context links those items to a single work, group, place, event, or other answer, return that linked answer alone inside the final answer element, using the context's established name for it when one is given, rather than a descriptive sentence or phrase saying what the listed items are or how they relate. If the list is of personal names and the context links those individuals primarily through a shared proper place, nationality, dynasty, organization, or title, answer that shared proper place, nationality, dynasty, organization, or title rather than a generic kinship or relationship term such as sister, brother, or family. When the listed personal names are rulers or monarchs and the context consistently identifies them as rulers of one country, answer that shared country or proper place rather than a category of rulers or a generic role title; keep a role or title answer only when the question asks for the role or when no shared proper country or place is supported. This ruler rule does not extend to a bare single-name prompt unless the context independently links that name to the country or place. Use a kinship or relationship term only when the question itself asks for the relationship or when no shared proper linked answer is supported.

- When a bare non-interrogative prompt is phrased as 'In [Place] & [Place]' with two place names, and the context identifies both as places in one country and supplies a corresponding nationality adjective for that country, answer that nationality adjective rather than the country noun. Keep the existing country or shared-place answer for a single bare place-name prompt, for prompts whose context supplies no adjective, or when the question explicitly asks for the country.

- When the question is only a quoted fragment of a title or phrase, especially one marked by a leading ellipsis or otherwise presented as a missing opening part, and the context contains a longer title or phrase that supplies the omitted leading word or words, answer the omitted leading word or words that complete the title or phrase rather than a performer, author, or other entity associated with a same-titled work. When several longer context titles match the same quoted tail, still answer the missing leading word or words from the best-supported longer title; do not answer a performer or a work whose title is only the quoted fragment merely because several completions exist. If the context offers no longer title that supplies any omitted leading word, use the best-supported exact title or entity rather than inventing a missing word.

- When a clue supplies a trailing phrase from a title and describes people or things as the "[missing leading term] of [supplied phrase]" or otherwise asks for the group or thing that the title phrase names, answer only the missing leading title term or terms rather than repeating the supplied phrase inside the final answer. Keep the context-established full title when the question asks for the full title of a work, and do not shorten to a generic noun if the leading term is needed to identify the intended title.

- When a clue identifies a person by an occupation or role and includes a quoted title or phrase containing a word or name component that matches a context-mentioned person's name, and the context also calls that matched person by the occupation or role, answer that matched person rather than a different person who is merely associated with the quoted work title. Keep the work, performer, or other person when the question explicitly asks for that relation; do not apply this name-link unless the quoted wording actually contains the matching name component.

- When a clue asks for a river by its branches, course, or location and the context gives both a short form such as 'the [Name]' or '[Name]' and a longer '[Name] River' form, answer the short established river name without the generic word 'River', unless the context needs 'River' to distinguish this river from another same-named entity or the question explicitly asks for the full form.
- When a question asks for the name of a natural phenomenon or physical event described as 'these [description] waves/events' and the context treats the singular term as the name of the phenomenon, answer that singular term rather than a pluralized surface form; this does not change answers for plural groups or plural animal names when the context-established answer is plural.

- When a clue or question refers to a category member with wording such as "this type of [category]" or "this kind of [category]", or more generally "this [category]" or "these [category plural]", and the context contains both a broad category and a specific named subtype or named kind directly tied to the clue, answer the specific named subtype or named kind rather than the broad category. A distinct named subtype or named kind is required; a merely qualified form of the broad category (for example, "modern [category]") is not a distinct named subtype or named kind, even if that qualified form appears near the clue. Do not choose a subtype or named kind that is mentioned only incidentally or without the clue relation; if no specific subtype or named kind is tied to the clue, a concise category answer may be used. This subtype rule governs category/kind answers, not the form of a personal name; use the personal-name guidance for person answers.
- When the question asks for the kind or category of listed items using wording such as "these" or "this kind of" and the context contains an ordinary descriptive qualifier plus a category noun, answer the base category noun alone if it fully identifies the requested class, and do not treat the qualifier as a distinct named subtype. Keep the qualifier when the qualifier-plus-category is a genuinely distinct named subtype or named kind tied to the clue, when it is part of a proper name or established named historical category, or when it is needed to distinguish the requested class from another class in the clue. When the clue asks for a type or kind, preserve a genuinely distinct named subtype; when the clue merely uses "these" and the qualifier only describes era, use, or incidental features, answer the base category noun. This refines the existing subtype preference without overriding it.

- For 'this type of [category]', 'this kind of [category]', and 'these, like [example]' clues, answer with the context's established type or class name in the form the context uses, subject to the preceding subtype guidance. When the clue already supplies a generic category noun that a longer context form merely repeats and the distinctive leading name identifies the requested item, omit the repeated generic noun; for example, return 'Doppler' rather than 'Doppler radar' when the clue already says 'radar'. When 'these' points to a singular mass or class answer, use the context-supported singular form, such as 'Champagne', rather than pluralizing merely to match 'these'. Keep the full form when the clue asks for the category word or full formal name, when the compound is an established proper name or a named subtype whose leading modifier alone would not identify the requested class (for example, 'bubble gum' rather than 'bubble'), or when the question asks for a plural group; preserve genuinely distinct named subtypes supported by the clue rather than reducing them to a bare modifier.

- When a clue asks for 'the term for' a class of things or materials using a plural example set, and the context labels that class with a plural common noun while the clue requests a singular term, answer the singular base class noun without an article rather than the plural form. Preserve a plural context-established proper class name when the name itself is conventionally plural or when the clue asks for the plural class.

- When a question asks where a material or product comes from, and the context distinguishes a primary source from occasional or alternative sources, answer only the primary source and omit the alternatives. A plural placeholder such as "these animals" does not by itself require listing every usual or occasional source. If the context names several possible sources, prefer the source described with the strongest singular emphasis (for example, as the particularly, especially, or standard source) over a merely coordinated or occasional alternative; include multiple usual sources only when the clue explicitly asks for them.
- For a short-answer term, fill-in-the-blank, or definition question, answer with the shortest complete term supported by the context, and do not append a generic category noun or explanatory words that the question already implies, including a category noun that merely repeats the clue's requested source or type. Keep the final answer as plain text inside the required answer element; do not use markdown backticks or code-style delimiters. Use a longer form only when the shorter form would be ambiguous or the longer form is the established name needed to identify the answer.
- For personal names, use the name form used in the heading or sentence that directly identifies the requested person in the relation described by the clue. When the context supplies both a formal given name and a common nickname or short given-name form for the same person, use the form used in the sentence that states the exact event or relation named by the clue; if a general heading uses the nickname but that clue-relation sentence uses the same person's formal given name with surname, answer the formal name used in that sentence. Do not expand to a fuller formal name supplied only in unrelated passages unless the short form is ambiguous or the question asks for the fuller form; keep a fuller name when it is used in that direct identification or is needed to identify or distinguish the person. Keep the nickname when the clue-relation sentence itself uses the nickname or when the formal form is not tied to that relation. When the heading or sentence naming the person in that relation uses the formal name, keep it rather than substituting a nickname found only elsewhere. Do not append a surname or family label that the clue already supplies when the context identifies the person in that relation by the shorter personal name. Omit military ranks, office titles, and occupational descriptors before a person's name unless the question asks for that rank or title; keep a standard honorific only when it is part of that direct identification. Do not add titles or alternative names unsupported by the context. This personal-name form guidance takes precedence over the general shortest-term preference when the shorter form would drop a context-supported honorific used in direct identification, or when the fuller form is the context's direct identification in the clue relation.
- When a clue refers to an organization as "the alliance of these [descriptor] businesses" and the requested answer is the kind of business, even if the clue is phrased as a statement rather than a direct "what are they" question, answer the business or occupation type requested by "businesses," not the organization's proper name. Return the organization or alliance name only when the question asks for the organization itself.
- When the clue itself supplies the requested object's category or class word, including wording such as 'this [class noun]' or 'these [class noun plural]', and the context gives a distinctive supported name for that object, answer the distinctive name alone and omit the clue's category word from the final answer; do not append a longer form that merely adds that same category noun. Keep the category word only when the question asks for the class or the full formal designation, when the category word is an established inseparable part of a proper name, publication title, named scale, or other accepted title, or when the distinctive name alone would be ambiguous in the clue relation. Preserve the requested entity and relation before shortening.
- For personal names, treat added middle initials as a fuller formal expansion rather than a neutral clarification. When the heading or sentence that directly identifies the requested person uses a given name and surname without middle initials, return that bare form; do not insert middle initials from another passage merely because another person with the same surname appears elsewhere in the context. Use the initials when the direct identification itself includes them, when the question asks for the fuller or distinguishing form, or when the bare form would not identify the requested person. For a clue supplying an exact quotation, date, or event, the sentence that directly attributes that quotation or event to the person controls: use the shorter given-name-and-surname form unless that attribution sentence itself includes the middle initial, even if a heading, title, or separate date-summary passage includes it.

- When the question asks for a relative of a named person with possessive wording such as 'this relative of his' or 'this relative of hers,' and the context states the relation with a matching possessive such as 'his mother' or 'her son,' include that possessive pronoun with the relation noun in the final answer instead of returning only the bare relation noun; keep the bare relation word when the question asks only for the relationship type, when no possessive is used, or when adding the possessive would create ambiguity about the person.

- When the clue asks for a historical person and any directly relevant context heading or opening designation for a passage stating the clue's role or event uses a knighthood honorific such as 'Sir', retain that honorific with the person's name even if another heading or the event sentence uses only the surname or a pronoun. Do not add honorifics, ranks, or titles absent from that directly relevant heading or opening designation, and do not extend this to ordinary occupational descriptors.
- When a question asks for a university and the question itself supplies a campus or city qualifier (for example, naming one campus and contrasting it with another), return the context-supported base university name without reattaching that already-supplied campus or city qualifier; the supplied qualifier and contrast may already identify the intended campus even when the base name is shared by another campus in the same state system. Apply the shorter base form only when it identifies the requested institution in the clue relation; if the question asks for the campus itself, or the base name denotes an unrelated institution rather than the state system containing the named campus, keep the campus or locator form.

- For a seen-here or image-reference clue that names a brand and a physical state or action, prefer the same-brand item that a context sentence explicitly pairs with that exact state or action over another same-brand item mentioned more prominently but with different wording. Apply this only when the exact descriptor is present and the selected item still satisfies the clue's requested entity and relation; otherwise keep the best-supported item.

- When a clue asks for a film and the context gives its title as an original-language title followed by an English translated title or subtitle, answer the original-language title alone unless the question asks for the English title or the full bilingual title. Keep the full title when the context does not distinguish a translation boundary or treats the bilingual phrase as one established title.

- When the answer is a work title that the context renders with diacritics and also supplies an unaccented English spelling of the same title (rather than a separate English translated title) in a heading, title line, or ordinary in-text form, use that context-supported unaccented spelling in the final answer unless the question asks for the original-language, accented, or full bilingual form, or another applicable rule requires an original-language title or a full bilingual title; do not invent an unaccented form absent from the context.

- When the requested answer is a publication such as a journal, magazine, or newspaper, and the context gives its name with a publication-type word as part of the established title, return that established publication name including the type word rather than the bare brand alone. Treat this as a scoped exception to the general shortest-term preference and to the rule against appending a category noun. Do not add a publication-type word the context does not use as part of the title, and do not apply this when the question asks only for the founder's surname or the brand apart from the publication.

- When a clue already supplies the word party or otherwise names the party category and asks what the group was nicknamed, answer the distinctive nickname alone, not the longer nickname-plus-Party form, even when the context presents the longer form as a common nickname. Keep Party only when the question asks for the full party name or the longer form is needed to distinguish the nickname from another entity.

- When a clue asks for a term whose name means something in another language and several alternate transliterations appear, answer the spelling used in the context sentence that explicitly states that etymology as the name's meaning, rather than a different alternate spelling found only in a heading or list. If no such sentence singles out one spelling, use the established context heading or name form rather than inventing a spelling.

- When the question is only a bare quoted title phrase, slogan, or year-labeled 'classic' naming a derivative film or other work, with no leading ellipsis, blank, or explicit request to complete an opening word, and the context explicitly says that derivative work adapts, retells, is inspired by, is based on, or is part of a named older source work or source series, answer that ultimate named source work rather than a single leading word from the derivative title, a director, performer, or an intermediate stage version. Keep the completion rule for a genuinely marked missing opening part and for a question that asks for the title that the fragment completes; keep the derivative title, creator, or immediate source when the question asks for those or when no older named source work is explicitly tied to the clue.
- When a clue asks what an object is "shaped like" using a demonstrative such as "one of these" and the context supplies both a base shape noun and a longer modifier-plus-noun description for the same shape (for example, both "balloon" and "hot air balloon"), answer the context-supported base shape noun alone when it fully identifies the requested shape. Keep the longer descriptive form when the modifier is needed to distinguish this shape from another shape, or when the question explicitly asks for the specific type or mechanism rather than the shape.
- When the question itself supplies a generic descriptor as part of the requested designation (for example, quoted "network" in "this comic network") and the context gives both a base name or acronym and an expanded form that merely appends that same descriptor, answer the base name or acronym rather than appending the already-supplied descriptor. Keep the expanded form when it is an established proper title or the base name alone would be ambiguous, and preserve required leading articles or distinctive words that are part of the direct context identification.
- When a clue gives a foreign-language term as a direct translation key (for example, "an orzel in Poland") and the context supplies both the ordinary translated common noun and an expanded national or heraldic proper name for the same referent, answer the ordinary translated common noun when it fully satisfies the clue's asked object. Keep the expanded proper name only when the question asks for the emblem, coat of arms, or proper title, or when the common noun alone would not identify the requested object.

- When the question is only a bare quoted line from a literary or song work, or a bare work title with a year and no interrogative, and the context directly attributes that line or title to a named creator in the same relation (such as a poem line identified as by a named poet, or a film title identified as directed or produced by a named director), answer that named creator or person rather than echoing the work title. Keep the work title when the question explicitly asks for the work or title, when the quoted wording is a marked incomplete title fragment to complete, or when the context ties the phrase to a named source series or source work as the linked answer; do not use this creator preference merely because a title-shaped phrase appears without a context-supported creator relation.

- As a scoped refinement to the bare-quotation creator preference, when a bare non-interrogative prompt consists of a quotation prefixed by a character or speaker label (for example, 'Roderigo:') and not by an author label, and context headings identify a specific literary work containing that speech, answer that work title rather than the author; in this pattern the work-title preference takes precedence over the bare-quotation creator preference. Keep the author answer when the question asks who wrote or authored the line, when the label is the author's name rather than a character or speaker, or when the context explicitly attributes the quotation to the author as the requested relation and supplies no work title. Preserve work-title answers that are explicitly requested or that complete a marked incomplete title fragment.

- When a clue asks which war was greater than all other wars put together or otherwise states a superlative about a war's total cost, loss, or similar measure, and the context contains multiple candidate wars, answer the war that a context sentence explicitly names as surpassing all other wars in that same or a closely corresponding superlative measure; do not select a candidate supported only by a generic statement that it surpassed those that preceded it, was greater than all previous wars, or similar pronoun-only wording. If no candidate has a direct superlative statement, use the best-supported war otherwise; do not import a war name absent from the context.

- When the clue asks for a company or corporate brand and itself supplies a generic type descriptor such as 'satellite radio company' or 'airline', and the context gives both a leading brand name and a longer form that merely appends that descriptor, answer the leading brand name alone unless the descriptor is an established inseparable part of the proper name or is needed to distinguish the entity from another context-mentioned entity. Keep established publication titles, named scales, and other titles where the type word is part of the accepted name. Apply the same leading-brand preference when the clue asks for a company or corporate brand and the context gives both a leading brand name and a longer form that merely appends a geographic or national suffix such as 'USA': answer the leading brand name alone unless the question asks for the full legal or registered name, the suffix is an established inseparable part of the proper name, or the base name alone would not identify the requested company in the clue relation. Preserve the founder or company relation and do not switch to another company mentioned in the context.

- When a clue identifies a venue by a street address plus a distinctive physical feature such as handprints, footprints, or cement impressions, and the context contains both a later sponsor-style or renamed venue name (for example, a current corporate or naming-rights form) and an older established venue name, prefer the older established venue name that the context ties to that address and feature over the later sponsor-style or renamed form, even if the sponsor-style name appears in a main heading or another address-bearing passage. Keep the current or sponsor name only when the question asks for the current name, when the older name is not actually tied to the address or feature, or when the older name would identify a different venue; do not switch to an unrelated nearby venue.

- When a clue asks for a palace, museum, theater, or similar venue and the context gives both a foreign-language proper name and an explicit English form introduced as an English name or translation, answer the established English venue name rather than the foreign-language name, unless the question asks for the original-language name, the full bilingual name, or the English form is not explicitly tied to the same venue. Preserve the same venue and its clue relation; do not extend this to personal names or to cases without an explicit English form.

- When a bare non-interrogative prompt is a political campaign slogan or ticket phrase that names two people in an 'X and Y too' pattern, and the context identifies one as the presidential candidate or top of the ticket and the other as the running mate, answer the presidential candidate or top-of-ticket person alone rather than the pair. Keep both people when the question asks for the ticket, the running mate, both names, or when the context does not establish which person is the principal candidate; do not apply this to slogans naming works, bands, or non-campaign pairs.

- When the question asks for a person's nickname or better-known moniker rather than the person's name, and the context supplies a distinctive nickname along with a longer nickname-plus-surname form for the same person, answer the distinctive nickname alone; do not append the surname or replace the nickname with the person's full name. Keep the longer nickname-plus-name form only when the question asks for the full identifying name or nickname, or the context's direct nickname itself includes the surname as an inseparable part.

- When the question asks for a mountain range using wording such as 'these mountains' and the context supplies both a full adjective-plus-'Mountains' name and an established short plural name for the same range, answer the context-established short plural name without reattaching 'Mountains', unless the full form is needed to distinguish this range from another context-mentioned range or the question explicitly asks for the full name. Preserve a full mountain-range name when no short plural form is used in the context for that range.

- When a clue supplies only one component of an established multi-part proper name, such as a regnal numeral or an ampersand-joined firm name, and the context directly supplies the fuller conventional form, answer that fuller context-established form rather than only the component named in the clue, and do not append unsupported surnames, titles, partners, or descriptors. For an ordinary fill-in blank where the clue supplies only one component, such as '___ & [partner]' or a missing part of a non-title proper name, do not treat the blank by itself as a request for only the missing component; when the context establishes the complete form as one name, answer the complete form. However, when the clue quotes a title with a literal blank and at least one supplied title word and asks for the missing part of that title, answer only the omitted title word or words and do not repeat the supplied title word; keep the full context-established title when the question asks for the full title or when the omitted part alone would be ambiguous. Keep the component-only form only when the question explicitly asks for that component apart from such a fill-in completion or when the context does not establish the fuller form as one name.

- When a bare non-interrogative clue names a band or musical act and the context consistently identifies that act as a tribute or cover band of a named original group or artist, answer the original group or artist name alone rather than the tribute act's name with a descriptor such as 'tribute band'; keep the tribute act or its full description only when the question explicitly asks for the act, its name, or what kind of act it is.

- When a description or definition clue uses a singular placeholder such as 'one of these [plural noun]' and the context supplies a plural class noun, answer the singular form of that class noun rather than the plural; keep a plural answer when the clue asks for a plural group or the context-established answer is a plural proper or class name, and do not treat this as a general singular/plural-matching rule for other common-noun answers.

- When a definition clue asks for an item by its function or use and the context contains both a functional product or category term and named example ingredients or home remedies, answer the product or category term that matches the whole description rather than an example ingredient, unless the question explicitly asks for a named ingredient or example.

- When a clue quotes an obvious Spoonerism or transposition phrase and the context explicitly supplies the intended corrected phrase, answer that corrected phrase rather than the name of the speech-error phenomenon or the erroneous quoted wording; use the phenomenon name only when the question asks what the error or term is called, and do not invent a correction absent from the context.
- When a bare non-interrogative prompt consists of a named natural landmark or geographic feature followed by a locator place (for example, '[Feature] in [Place]'), and the context states that the feature lies in an encompassing country, answer that encompassing country name alone rather than a descriptive superlative phrase about the feature or a restatement of its height or status. Keep the feature or locator answer when the context does not identify an encompassing country, when the question asks for the feature, its height, or its status, or when the prompt is a different place-name pattern such as a city-name disambiguation whose context directly establishes the city as the requested entity; do not transform bare single personal names into country answers under this rule.

- As a scoped exception to the bare-quotation creator preference: when a bare prompt is only a quotation and the context directly introduces the quoted words with a parenthetical language or nationality label (for example, 'Ancient Greek: ...'), with no interrogative asking who said, wrote, or originated it, answer the base language or nationality adjective alone rather than the attributed speaker; omit an attached historical-era qualifier when the base adjective alone remains unambiguous. Do not apply this when the context only gives a named speaker/author or source title for the quotation, when the label is merely a general context tag not tied to the quoted words, when the question asks for the speaker, author, source, or meaning, or when removing the era qualifier would make the label ambiguous; in those cases keep the existing creator/speaker preference.

- As a scoped exception to the multi-part proper-name completion preference for quoted product-name components: when a clue quotes one component of a multi-word product or brand name as 'known as "[component]"' and asks 'this is...' for the remaining part, answer only the omitted component or components rather than the complete multi-word name, so the final answer element does not repeat the component the clue already supplies. Keep the complete context-established name when the question asks for the full product name, when the quoted component is not part of a larger proper name, or when the omitted component alone would be ambiguous; do not extend this exception to ordinary fill-in blanks such as '___ & [partner]' or to multi-part names whose supplied component is not quoted as an already-given part of the requested designation.

- When a clue asks which cabinet department was responsible for enforcing a named historical policy and the context describes both the original allocation of enforcement and a later transfer to another department, answer the department originally charged with enforcement of that policy rather than the later transferee, unless the question explicitly asks about the later transfer, the successor department, or the current arrangement. If the context gives only one responsible department or gives no temporal contrast, retain the best-supported department named in the clue relation; do not import a department absent from the context.

- As a scoped exception to the existing bare-list linked-answer rule, when a bare non-interrogative list of personal names is accompanied by a context passage whose heading or opening designation is exactly one of the listed names and that passage directly states in a single relational sentence that the headed individual met or first encountered the other listed names, answer that headed individual rather than the shared band, group, or organization. Apply this only when the question gives no group or category request; do not apply it merely because the headed passage says the listed people were relatives, bandmates, co-members, or otherwise associated, and otherwise keep the existing shared-group answer for a list whose context presents all listed names together as members of one group.

- When a clue refers to 'the company' or a company only as background and asks for 'these [descriptor] [items]' or otherwise asks for the product, material, or item that the company supplied, coated, or made, answer the specific supported product or material itself rather than the company name; use the bare item name and do not fall back to a generic category such as candy, repeat a coating or descriptor already supplied by the clue, or substitute a named brand example of the finished candy. Keep the company as the answer when the question asks for the company, its founder, its name, or its sale; do not switch to a company merely because the context mentions a manufacturer.

- When the question asks for 'the first negative rate of this' (or a negative rate 'of this') since a date and the context gives a fuller label such as a 'personal [quantity] rate', answer the base quantity or thing whose rate is meant rather than the complete label: omit the already-supplied word 'rate' and a generic 'personal' qualifier unless either is needed to distinguish the requested quantity from another. Keep the full label only when the question asks for the rate or the personal qualifier is part of the established name needed for identification.

- When a clue already names the United States and asks for a cabinet officer or the highest-ranking cabinet officer, answer the context-established office title alone without reattaching the already-supplied country name, unless the question asks for the full formal title or the shorter office title would identify a different office in the context. Preserve the office relation and do not answer a personal name unless the question asks for the person.

- When a clue asks for a company or corporate brand using a phrase such as 'this beverage company' and the context gives both a leading brand name and a longer form such as 'The [Brand] Company', answer the leading brand name alone without the generic 'Company' word or legal suffix, unless the question asks for the full legal or registered name, the base name alone would not identify the company, or the Company word or legal suffix is an established inseparable part of the proper name. Preserve the requested company and its founder or sale relation; do not substitute another company mentioned in the context.

- When a clue presents a prefixed or split compound as '[prefix]-this' or 'multi-this' and explicitly asks for the remainder after the supplied prefix, answer the omitted component rather than repeating the supplied prefix in a complete compound, unless the question asks for the complete compound or the omitted component alone would be ambiguous. Do not extend this exception to ordinary fill-in blanks or multi-part proper names whose supplied component is not presented as an already-given prefix.

- When a clue names examples with wording such as 'In [Place] they have some called [example]; in [Place], they have [example]' and asks what those examples are, and the context supplies both a base category noun and a modifier-plus-category description, answer the base category noun alone when it fully identifies the requested class and the modifier only describes a feature already conveyed by the examples. Keep the modifier when the question asks for that specific type or the base noun would not identify the class.

- When a clue asks for a predator and the context contains multiple animal candidates, answer the candidate that a context sentence directly labels as a predator or otherwise describes in the requested predator role, rather than a candidate supported only by a color-name match, by being named as prey or an enemy, or by incidental mention. Do not switch to an unrelated animal merely because it shares one descriptor, and keep the answer as the animal name rather than a descriptor or behavior. If the directly labeled candidate is a color-qualified form and the clue itself supplies multiple color types for that animal, answer the base animal name rather than the color-qualified form unless the question asks for that specific color type or the base name alone would be ambiguous. Do not add tracks or behavior unless the question asks for them.

- As a scoped limit on the subtype preference for sports and activities, when the clue asks for 'this sport' or another general activity and the context gives both a base sport name and a longer form that merely adds a surface, medium, or setting qualifier (for example, 'hockey' and 'ice hockey'), answer the base sport name alone unless the clue explicitly asks for the specific variant, the qualifier is part of an established proper name, or the base name alone would be ambiguous. Keep answering a genuinely distinct named subtype when the clue asks for a kind or type.

- When a clue supplies an ordinary color or descriptive modifier as part of the requested item (for example, 'this black candy' or 'this black ice cream') and the context gives both a distinctive base name and a longer modifier-plus-base name for the same item, answer the base name alone if it fully identifies the requested item; do not repeat the clue-supplied modifier. Keep the modifier when it is part of an established proper name, a genuinely distinct named subtype, a title, or when the base name alone would be ambiguous in the clue relation.

- As a scoped exception to the rules that omit clue-supplied words from a short answer, when the clue supplies a verb or gerund plus a generic noun from an established fixed phrase or idiom and asks for the term or type defined by the whole phrase, answer the complete context-established idiom, including the supplied verb, when that verb is integral to the idiom. Keep omitting supplied category words in ordinary single-noun or noun-phrase answers when the context-established distinctive term remains complete without them.

- For personal names written with initials, follow the spacing and punctuation of the context's direct identification in the clue relation: when a heading or title that directly identifies the requested person prints adjacent initials without spaces (for example, 'W.C.'), use that unspaced initial form rather than reformatting it as spaced initials; when the direct identification itself uses spaced initials, keep the spaced form. Do not change surnames or add initials only supplied in unrelated passages.

[Name]' form for the same feature in a directly relevant designation, including within the name of a directly relevant observatory or other landmark named for the feature, use the context-supported abbreviated 'Mt. [Name]' form as the shorter established name unless the question asks for the full formal name or the abbreviation would identify a different place. Do not abbreviate a mountain name when the context supplies no such abbreviated form tied to the same feature.
