# Live Mathematical MCQ Heuristics

## Option Comparison
- Compare all options before committing. The correct choice is often the strongest statement justified by the question, while nearby distractors are weaker, overstrong, or miss an equality case.
- Track exact quantifiers such as "there exists", "for every", "if and only if", and "exactly when".

## Theorem-Level Precision
- Check whether an option weakens the conclusion by dropping a characterization, equality clause, or full equivalence.
- Check whether an option overstates the theorem by upgrading regularity, removing scale restrictions, or changing an existential statement into a universal one.

## Hypotheses
- Verify the hypotheses and domain carefully. Distractors often keep the theorem shape but alter the required assumptions.
- Pay close attention to equality cases, extremal conditions, and whether a result applies to the full family or only a restricted subfamily.

## Choice-Level Distinctions
- When a question asks for the strongest, most complete, or valid statement, treat a choice that says one of the other choices is correct but a stronger result can be proved, or that expressly says the listed statements are not exhaustive, as a substantive candidate rather than as empty or purely meta. Compare it with the best concrete alternative: if that alternative is weaker in a dimension the question makes central, such as regularity, quantifiers, equivalence, or boundary coverage, and the setup supports the stronger conclusion, select the meta-choice even without separately refuting every concrete option. Do not select it merely because it mentions strength, lacks a formula, or because a concrete option is merely imperfect; retain a concrete option when it already states the strongest supported theorem.
- In complete-list or classification questions, separate the claim that every object has one of the listed forms from a converse that every permitted parameter value in a listed family actually occurs. Treat such a converse as an additional universal-existence claim requiring independent support. When two choices otherwise match and one adds such a converse, especially 'conversely, every such divisor or parameter actually occurs', select the version without it unless the hypotheses or the requested classification expressly establish that all permitted values occur. Do not infer occurrence from a numerical divisibility condition or from the fact that the value is allowed.
- When options contrast a positive structural property such as an embedding or injectivity with a claim that a natural map need not be injective or can identify classes, do not infer the negative claim solely from a quotient, a finite-index subgroup, or a many-to-one intermediate construction. Wording such as 'can identify' or 'need not be injective' states the negative conclusion rather than supplying evidence. A non-injectivity claim requires a specific mechanism showing that distinct classes are identified, ideally a demonstrated pair with the same image; absent that, prefer the strongest positive structural statement that is otherwise supported, while still rejecting an embedding claim that carries an unsupported extra hypothesis.
- In consistency or model-existence questions, track each 'it is consistent that' clause and each 'no model satisfying' exclusion separately. An option asserting that a configuration is consistent with an additional axiom is not defeated merely because the combination is not obvious; conversely, an option asserting impossibility or exclusion under an axiom requires affirmative support. When a choice combines a consistency claim with compatibility with an additional axiom such as WUA, evaluate both claims as stated; do not downgrade it to a cautious exclusion merely because the coexistence seems surprising. Do not substitute an unsupported exclusion for a consistency claim, and do not add consistency claims that the option itself does not state.
- When choices partition a parameter into regimes, compare the boundary convention as part of the mathematical claim: endpoint inclusion, use of a strict inequality, and the exact exponent or bound are separate assertions. If two choices differ mainly by one closing an endpoint that the other leaves open, require explicit support for the endpoint and select the open version if the endpoint is not independently supported; broader boundary coverage is not automatically stronger. Likewise, do not treat a sharper-looking exponent as stronger without checking the inequality direction, the lower-bound relation, and the quantifier in each regime, and do not transfer a result across a boundary unless the option and the hypotheses support that transfer.

## Final Answer
- Output the final answer as the single option label only.
