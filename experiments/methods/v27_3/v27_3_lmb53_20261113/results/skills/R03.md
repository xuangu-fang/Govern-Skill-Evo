# Live Mathematical MCQ Heuristics

## Option Comparison
- Compare all options before committing. The correct choice is often the strongest statement justified by the question, while nearby distractors are weaker, overstrong, or miss an equality case.
- When an option states that a stronger result can be proven while the question asks for the strongest statement or a complete characterization, do not dismiss it or automatically choose the most sweeping concrete option. Compare each concrete option against the maximal claim in the relevant dimension: quantifier scope (existence versus universal, special case versus full family, restricted dimension or parameter versus arbitrary objects), conclusion strength (one implication versus equivalence, weak property versus full characterization), regularity or asymptotic strength (local versus optimal, lower bound versus exact or limsup), and completeness of a stated list. If no concrete option supplies that maximal claim and the leading concrete candidate is only a true weaker/special case, an overstrong or unsupported upgrade, or otherwise fails to reach the needed strength, select the 'stronger result' option. Preserve the requirement to choose a concrete option when it actually states the maximal claim; the trigger is the combination of a strongest/complete question, a meta-option, and concrete options that do not reach the needed scope or strength.
- Before selecting a concrete option under the preceding meta-option rule, if the question asks for the strongest or most complete statement and one option says a stronger result can be proved, do not stop merely because a concrete option is mathematically plausible or is the strongest among the concrete choices. List the independent strength dimensions actually present in the option set (for example, universal versus fixed element or conjugacy class, strict versus non-strict conclusion plus the derivative-zero case, auxiliary conditions such as a ≥ k/2, exact exponent and whether the implied constant is uniform in ε, global versus local or stepwise identity, dual version included, and all i ≥ 0 versus i > 0). If the strongest component in one dimension occurs in one concrete option and the strongest component in another dimension occurs in a different concrete option, and no single concrete option carries all of them, select the meta-option unless the question's hypotheses or a visible mathematical obstruction rules out the combined stronger statement. Do not let an elaborate formula or one impressive clause override a missing component; retain a concrete option only when you can state why it already contains the full maximal claim.
- When a strongest-statement or complete-characterization question includes a meta-option saying a stronger result can be proved, keep that meta-option live after listing the concrete options. Classify each concrete option by whether it gives the full exact theorem, not merely whether its displayed subset, inequality, decomposition, or construction is plausible. If every concrete option supplies only a one-way inclusion or inequality, a special construction, or a conditional or local statement while a stronger conclusion remains possible on the visible hypotheses, select the meta-option. Retain a concrete option only when its displayed statement already contains the full maximal conclusion with the same scope.
- Track exact quantifiers such as "there exists", "for every", "if and only if", and "exactly when".
- In finite-time blow-up questions where options differ by norm scope, compare the exact quantity whose time integral is asserted to diverge and compare the exponents. A claim about a single component such as ||u_x||_{L∞}^2 is a narrower, stronger-looking assertion than the full-gradient claim ∫ ||∇u||^2 = ∞; it is not guaranteed merely because u_x appears in the equation, and it may fail unless the equation visibly bounds all other gradient components by that component. When the question asks for the strongest guaranteed whole-solution consequence, prefer the full-gradient criterion actually supported by the theorem over a partial-derivative strengthening. A lower power or a limsup conclusion is generally weaker than squared full-gradient divergence unless the displayed statement only establishes that weaker form.
- When options share a consistency construction but one adds a universal negative under an extra axiom, such as 'no model satisfying WUA can contain such a pair', while another says the same phenomenon is also consistent with that axiom, do not infer the universal negative from the absence of an example under the axiom. Treat the 'no model' clause as a separate theorem requiring a specific incompatibility. If the question asks for consistency phenomena and no such incompatibility is visible, compare the joint-consistency option as a distinct stronger consistency claim rather than assuming the extra axiom rules the phenomenon out.

## Theorem-Level Precision
- Check whether an option weakens the conclusion by dropping a characterization, equality clause, or full equivalence.
- Check whether an option overstates the theorem by upgrading regularity, removing scale restrictions, or changing an existential statement into a universal one.

## Hypotheses
- Verify the hypotheses and domain carefully. Distractors often keep the theorem shape but alter the required assumptions.
- Pay close attention to equality cases, extremal conditions, and whether a result applies to the full family or only a restricted subfamily.

## Final Answer
- Output the final answer as the single option label only.
