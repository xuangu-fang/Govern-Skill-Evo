# Question Answering Skill

- When the question is an elliptical clue rather than a complete question, such as a bare list of names, a bare place, or a quotation fragment with an ellipsis, infer from the passages the omitted relation the clue is testing and return only the target entity or missing word.
- When a clue uses 'this' or 'these' as a pronoun-like placeholder for a missing word or noun phrase (for example, a prefixed compound or a phrase such as 'the [noun] of this'), answer only the component that fills that slot, not the full compound or the enclosing phrase.
- Do not repeat the clue's surrounding wording or expand the component into a broader category label, except that a possessive relative frame described below requires keeping the possessive relationship phrase; if the passages support both a bare missing word and a fuller synonymous phrase, prefer the component the clue's frame directly calls for.
- Do not apply this to a 'this [category noun]' frame that asks for a subtype or member (such as 'this type'); resolve that frame from the passages' stated label rather than by isolating one word.
- When the clue asks for a relative with an explicit possessive frame such as 'this relative of his' or 'this relative of hers', answer with the possessive relationship phrase supported by the passage rather than the bare kinship noun alone, and return only that phrase without explanation. Treat the possessive as part of the requested relation, not as redundant repetition of the clue. If the clue asks for a standalone kinship term without such a possessive frame, use the bare term.
- For a bare list of names, return the single shared referent the list identifies, not a possessive or descriptive label, sentence, or explanatory relationship phrase built from it. If the names are spouses or former spouses of one person, answer that shared person alone rather than a phrase such as 'that person's ex-wives'. For a bare list of people, give the shared state, country, organization, person, or other common referent or category supported by the passages, not a sentence describing their relationship. For a bare list of rulers or national leaders who share one country, answer the country name alone, not an office-plus-country phrase or a sentence about their relationship, and do not add an office label such as 'kings of' or 'presidents of' when the clue asks for their shared affiliation.
- For a bare list of works, answer with the shared subject or topic stated by the passages at its stated level; do not append a narrower form or genre label that the clue did not request, and do not replace the shared subject with a description of one listed work. For a bare list of fictional characters belonging to one work, answer that shared work alone rather than appending 'characters' or a similar category word.
- For a bare place, give the containing jurisdiction or common category indicated by the passage rather than repeating the place with extra location words.
- When a landmark appears under both a local-language formal name and a conventional English name, and the clue describes it in English by identifying features, use the conventional English name rather than the local-language formal name.
- When a landmark also appears under a historical public name and a later corporate naming-rights name, use the historical or conventional name when the clue's features identify the landmark itself and do not ask for its current name.
- Keep the current or local-language form only when the clue explicitly asks for it.
- For an incomplete quotation, give the missing word or words, not the visible fragment or a full title that already contains it, except when the clue is a campaign slogan or a real person's signature exclamation as described below. When the clue consists of a campaign slogan or a real person's signature exclamation, even when visibly truncated, and the passages attribute it to a named real person, answer that principal named person rather than completing the missing words or restating the quotation, unless the clue explicitly asks for the completion; if the slogan names a candidate together with a running mate or other secondary figure, return the candidate alone. Except for such a campaign slogan or real person's signature exclamation, when the entire clue is a complete quoted catchphrase or exclamation, or a quoted title, with no ellipsis or blank, return the work or franchise conventionally associated with that phrase, not a later film, episode, or special title that merely embeds the phrase and not a single function word taken from such a title. Do not apply that catchphrase-and-title rule to a complete line of poetry or a literary quotation; when the clue is a quoted line of verse and the passages attribute that line to a named poet or author, answer the poet or author rather than the poem title, unless the clue explicitly asks for the title or work. For such a fictional or work-associated catchphrase, exclamation, or quoted title, return the named speaker or character only when the clue explicitly asks for the speaker; otherwise keep the associated work or franchise as the answer. Do not apply the campaign-slogan or real-person rule to a fictional character's catchphrase or a quotation from a work; for those, keep returning the associated work or franchise rather than the character or speaker, unless the clue explicitly asks for the speaker. Continue to return the missing words when the clue explicitly asks for the completion, and continue to return a brand, work, or lyric when the quoted material names a product, title, or song rather than a real person's signature phrase. Reserve missing-word extraction for clues that visibly omit words or contain an ellipsis.
- If the passages do not support a single omitted relation, answer the most direct supported entity rather than adding alternatives or explanation, except for a paired-definition wordplay clue supported by both definitions as described below.
- When a wordplay clue gives two distinct definitions separated by a slash or similar paired separator and the passages support a homophone or near-identical pair satisfying both, return both forms in the clue's order rather than only the form for one definition.
- Do not apply this to ordinary multiple-choice lists or to a clue that asks for a single missing word.
- When a clue describes a character or work through a title or role ('in [year] he/she was...', 'this mongoose...'), return the character/work or named role rather than a performer, author, or other associated person; return the performer or creator only when the clue explicitly asks for that person.
- When a clue calls a person the 'current' holder of an office but also gives identifying education, career, or event details, do not select the present-day officeholder from the word 'current' alone; resolve the referent from the person to whom the clue's specific details are attributed in the supporting passages.
- If an earlier officeholder is tied to those details, treat 'current' as describing the office at the time the clue was written and answer that earlier holder, even when another passage labels a later person as current. Prefer the later present-day holder only when no earlier holder is tied to those details and the later person satisfies them.
- When a clue includes a short abbreviation, acronym, or place code together with a related phrase, resolve the abbreviation to the named entity it denotes and answer that entity or relation; do not substitute a landmark, object, or other item merely associated with the surrounding phrase when the clue supplies a code or abbreviation for the requested referent. Use the expansion supported by the passages and clue, not a looser association.
- When the clue asks for a general category or already makes the category clear (for example, a river, island, company, bird, or general term), answer with the shortest conventional name or term that clearly identifies the requested item. Do not append a redundant category word, and do not narrow the answer to a more specific subtype, title, event, or named variant unless the clue asks for that narrower specificity. Use a longer fixed label, title, or established proper name only when the shorter form would not clearly identify the requested entity or when the clue asks for that label; keep exact technical/category wording when the clue asks for that label, and do not add explanatory clauses or alternatives.
- When the clue asks for a nickname, moniker, or alias, return only the nickname itself as quoted in the supporting passage, not the nickname combined with the person's surname or a fuller identifying phrase; keep the full personal name when the clue asks for the person rather than the nickname.
- When the directly supporting passage gives a fixed technical label that the clue asks for, use that specific form in the answer rather than a broader synonym. For a person's name, use the conventional identifying form supported by the clue and passages. When a person's name includes initials and the supporting passages show both joined and spaced initial forms for that person, use the joined conventional initial form: write the initials consecutively without spaces between them, then a single normal space before the surname. Do not insert spaces between the initials, and do not run the surname onto the last initial. Do not expand a recognizable surname or familiar single name into a fuller formal version, and do not replace a familiar given name with a formal one. Include additional name components when the shorter form would be ambiguous or the clue's wording requires them; do not add initials, middle names, or other name components that the clue and passages do not use and that are not needed to distinguish the person from another candidate, and use the conventional first-and-last-name form when it unambiguously identifies the person and no initials are part of that conventional form. Preserve the rest of the conventional name, including required given names, surname components, and conventionally attached honorifics; retain a conventional honorific or a nobility or royal title attached to the person's name in the supporting passages, even when the clue identifies the person by office, role, or description, and do not drop it as an unnecessary office label. Do not add a generic elected or appointed office title that the clue and passages do not use as part of the person's conventional identifying name, and do not add an explanatory clause, alternative name, or passage example.
- For a named answer, when the clue itself already contains the answer's category word, role word, or country name, give the distinctive name, title, or fixed label alone and do not repeat that supplied word or prefix unless it is part of the entity's established proper name or fixed technical label; do not append a generic corporate suffix. Otherwise use the shortest conventional form that still clearly identifies the entity; do not attach a national or regional qualifier to a general term the passages use. Remove a trailing category label, a translated subtitle after a colon, or another framing word that does not change the referent. When the clue names a tribute or cover act, answer the original act it honors rather than describing the tribute act with a category label. When a work appears under both a longer title and a shorter distinctive title, use the shorter title unless the clue asks for the full title. Keep any qualifier or noun that belongs to the entity's established proper name or fixed technical label rather than returning only a modifying adjective, including when the label fills a 'this' slot; preserve a fuller form when it is the passages' established common name for the entity (for a plant, animal, place, or similar item) and shortening would drop identifying content; otherwise retain a category or designator word only when it is part of the fixed conventional name and the shorter form would be ambiguous or name a different entity. For a class illustrated by named examples, use the plain class noun rather than a modifier-plus-class description unless the modifier is needed to distinguish the class. This category-and-title rule does not authorize shortening a person's name.
