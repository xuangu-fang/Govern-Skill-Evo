# Question Answering Skill

## Answer selection

- For a clue that is only a bare place name with no other wording or explicit question, answer the containing jurisdiction named in the passage (normally the country or state/province), not a descriptive fact about the place. Give one proper geographic name; prefer the country or state/province over a county, district, or city, and do not stack a county with a state or append extra qualifiers.
- When a descriptive 'home to...' or natural-feature location clue asks where something is, prefer the containing state or country named in the passages over a more specific local park, forest, grove, or named site. Use the local site only when the clue explicitly asks for that named site.
- For a named landmark, institution, or geographic feature, when the passages give both a common English/historical name and a local-language, translated, or current commercial name, answer the common English/historical name unless the clue explicitly uses the other. Include a conventional generic element (for example, a feature word such as 'Lake' or an institutional word such as 'Palace' or 'Theater') when it is part of that established name; do not treat the generic element as an extra qualifier.
- Return one concise answer string using the term from the supporting passage that directly fills the clue. Do not append parenthetical expansions, alternative labels, near-synonyms, or exhaustive lists of rare or occasional variants. If an abbreviation is the expected organization name, give the abbreviation alone. When a source distinguishes a usual or primary item from occasional alternatives, answer the usual/primary item.
- For a work title containing a colon subtitle, answer the main title alone unless the clue explicitly asks for the subtitle, the complete title, or a distinguishing subtitle component. Do not append the subtitle merely because the passage gives the full catalog title.
- Treat the supporting passage as the source for both the answer's identity and its form. Do not replace a passage-supported candidate with a more familiar unmentioned name, and do not add a surname, title, or identifier that appears only in outside knowledge. Likewise, copy the spelling and diacritics shown in the supporting passage rather than restoring a canonical form absent from it.
- Before answering, fill the exact grammatical slot the clue leaves open with the shortest expression that still distinguishes the requested item, and stop at that slot's boundary. Do not repeat a category, role, or head noun the clue already supplies when the answer is a name or established term: for 'this beverage company', give the bare company or brand name; for 'the rate of this', give the underlying noun, not the noun plus 'rate' or unnecessary modifiers. When examples are introduced as 'some called X' or 'Y' and the clue asks for their shared category, give the bare category noun, not a descriptive subtype or translated modifier. If the slot is an adjective-only alternative in a list of types ('X, Y, or this type') and the bare adjective is not the established noun for that type, include the understood head noun and match the grammatical number of the category the clue supplies. If the clue gives a prefix followed by 'this' or a blank, answer only the missing component, not the whole compound. Drop descriptive modifiers not needed for identification, and do not replace a requested common noun with a longer proper-name version. Keep a distinguishing word only when the shorter form would identify a different entity. For a bilingual work title given as 'Original: Translation' or 'Original (translation)', answer the original title alone unless the clue explicitly uses or asks for the translated title, and do not append the alternate-language subtitle.
- When a clue is a date plus a quoted title fragment introduced by a leading ellipsis, answer only the missing preceding part that completes the title. Do not return the entire title or add words from after the fragment.
- When the clue is only two or more personal names of real people and no explicit question, answer the shared place or jurisdiction named for both people (such as their state, country, or royal domain). Do not answer with a generic relationship or role label, and do not prefer a shared party or organizational affiliation over the shared place. If no shared place is stated, give the shared proper-noun affiliation instead.
- When a clue is only a list of two or more names, possibly with role titles, and the passages identify them as fictional characters rather than real people, answer the work (series, film, or show) in which they appear, not their shared in-universe setting or department.
- When a clue is only a comma-separated list of two or more titled works with no explicit question, answer the shared subject, setting, or topic established for those works. Do not append a genre or format label unless the clue asks for the type of work.
- In possessive clues with an appositive relative ('his grandfather, the inventor...'), answer the possessor or central subject, not the appositive relative. This holds even when the predicate asks what that relative thought or said: use the relative's description only to identify the possessor. If the possessor and the relative have similar names, give the possessor's name as stated for the central subject, not the relative's fuller name.
- When the requested slot is a relationship term and the clue supplies the possessor, include the possessive in the answer rather than returning the bare kinship term; the bare term does not form the requested noun phrase.
- When the clue is only a verbatim quoted line from a poem or literary work, with no wording outside the quotation asking for a work, answer the author or poet rather than the title of the poem or work. Do not treat a literary quotation as a catchphrase; choose a titled work only when the clue explicitly frames the answer as that kind of work.
- In a clue that quotes a line of dialogue with a speaker or character label (for example, a character name followed by a quoted line), treat the label and quotation as identifying the literary work; answer the work, not the author. Reserve the author or poet answer for a bare, unattributed literary quotation with no character, speaker, or scene cue.
- When a clue quotes a line and ends with an open function word or blank (for example, 'with pail and' this), fill the next word that completes the quoted expression; do not answer with the title, author, or theme of the work.
- For an explicit wordplay clue that states a transformation from one term to another ('a clique ... gets rid of its F to be ...'), answer both the source term and the resulting term, in the passage's paired form when given. Do not return only the resulting term.
- For a bare quoted catchphrase or signature expression with no noun asking for a work, answer the originating work or franchise, not a derivative installment whose title repeats the phrase. Prominence or repetition of the installment's title in passages does not make the clue a request for that installment; choose the installment only when the clue explicitly frames the answer as a film, TV movie, episode, or other titled work.
- When a bare clue names a tribute or cover band, answer the original artist or group that the band tributes, not the phrase 'tribute band' and not the cover band itself.
- When a clue is a bare campaign slogan that pairs a nickname for one candidate with a named running mate, answer the candidate represented by the nickname (the head of the ticket), not both names.
- Before returning the answer, check for category echo. If the clue already supplies the category or role, give the established bare name or term rather than the name plus that category or role. Do not mechanically truncate a proper name; keep only words needed to form the actual name.
- In a final boundary check, strip a category word, formal institutional tail, or literal prefix that the clue itself supplies and that is not needed to identify the answer, provided the remaining distinctive name appears as part of the entity's name in the passage. Keep a word when the shorter form would identify a different entity or remain ambiguous even with the clue's category.
