# Question Answering Skill

## Workflow

- Determine the answer type the question asks for (such as person, place, work or title, term, event, or office) and select the candidate that fills that slot. If the question lists characters or features associated with a work, return the work's title rather than describing those items.
- After choosing the referent, use the direct passage-supported name for the requested slot rather than a descriptive epithet or longer synonymous variant; do not shorten a standard name by dropping a distinguishing component that the clue does not supply.
- When a clue includes a specific relation, date range, quantity, or superlative, require the supporting passage to state that same relation for the candidate; do not substitute a nearby or different attribute. Reject a candidate whose name is absent from the passages when a passage-named candidate matches the clue's wording, and do not import an unnamed candidate from outside knowledge. If no passage states the exact attribute, choose the candidate explicitly connected to the closest stated fact rather than inventing a referent.
- For a bare literary quotation with no explicit question or request for a title and no signaled missing or intended expression, first check who introduces or speaks the line. When the passages introduce the quotation through a named character or dramatic speaker rather than the author and attribute that speaker's line to a specific named work, return that work's title rather than the author. Use an author answer for such a speaker-introduced dramatic quotation only when no containing work is named in the passages or the clue explicitly asks for the author. For an unattributed bare literary quotation with no named character or speaker, use the passage's attribution: return the named author when the passage presents the line as a poem or work by that author, even if the passage also gives the title, and do not return the title merely because the line is labeled as a titled work. In that unattributed case, return the work title only when no author is named in the passages or the clue explicitly asks for the work or title. Preserve unchanged the separate originating-work treatment for a bare catchphrase, fragment, or quotation associated with a well-known work when the passages contain a later special, adaptation, episode, or spin-off whose title quotes that phrase: return the originating work or franchise rather than the derivative title, unless the clue explicitly asks for that specific derivative.
- When the entire clue is only the title of a song or musical number, and the passages identify it as a song or number from a named stage or film work, return the containing work's title rather than the composer, lyricist, performer, or a description of the number. Keep the attributed-quotation behavior when the clue is an actual spoken or written quotation or dictum rather than a song title, and keep returning the named author when the clue explicitly asks for the author or composer or when the passages give no containing work.
- When a clue reproduces a quotation or a distorted phrase and signals a missing or intended expression (for example an unfinished line, "this word," or a bracketed correction in the passage), answer that missing or corrected word or phrase. Do not answer the author, the work title, or the name of the linguistic device instead. If the clue instead asks for the title of a recited or quoted work, return the work title as requested.
- When a clue is only a familiar slogan, catchphrase, or signature line and does not explicitly ask for the next words, its meaning, or a title, return the person or brand the phrase identifies (or the originating work when the catchphrase rule above applies) rather than the phrase's continuation or a descriptive summary; a signature line merely cut off at the end is not by itself a request to complete it. For a bare political slogan, return the person represented by the distinctive nickname or epithet, not the running mate or the event. Apply the missing-expression behavior to such a phrase only when the clue contains an explicit gap cue such as "this" or another explicit marker of omitted words, or an explicit request to complete the line or supply the missing words.
- When a clue supplies an opening word or phrase of a multiword title, product name, or other name and includes a gap cue such as a quoted fragment, "this," an unfinished expression, or a leading ellipsis, return only the omitted or corrected missing component rather than the complete title, and do not repeat the supplied opening portion. This missing-expression reading takes priority over returning the full work title or the named author for the quotation. Do not apply it to an explicit request to supply the entire quotation or title, or to a clue that otherwise asks for the whole work; in those cases return the full requested expression.
- For a question asking for a term, title, or label, choose the single requested term or title, not a definition, explanatory sentence, list of alternatives, or the name of a related event or work.
- For a proper name or defined term, use the ordinary plain spelling and spacing supported by the supplied passages. Do not add accents, diacritics, hyphenation, or other typographic marks that do not appear in the passages. When passages show multiple variants, prefer the unaccented and unhyphenated form used in the direct answer statement or in a clean definition/heading; do not reproduce a visibly garbled or truncated heading, and use the complete plain form that appears in a running sentence instead. If the clue supplies the name, prefer the form used in the clue. Keep necessary distinguishing name components, but do not add typography from outside the passages.
- When the clue uses a non-English or alternate common name, select the passage that explicitly pairs that name with an English identity and return the paired identity. Do not choose a different species, place, title, or entity merely because another passage repeats the foreign term nearby or contains a superficially similar label.
- When the clue asks for a city but refers to the state only as an unnamed "its state" (without naming the state), include the state after the city when the passages give the city together with that state as the identifying answer. Treat the state as part of the requested identification, so this inclusion overrides any general preference against appending an unrequested geographic suffix.
- If the clue already supplies the state or country as part of the description, return the city alone; do not append a state or country that the clue treats as already known.
- For a clue built only from a place or landmark without an explicit question, return the enclosing geographic entity the passages use to locate it: when the clue already names a state or province, give the country that contains it; when the clue only says a feature is "home to" something, give the larger state, region, or country identified as its location. Do not answer with a superlative description of the feature or with a named subarea, grove, or park unless the clue explicitly asks for that feature, subarea, or title. If the clue explicitly asks for the feature or title, or the passages provide no enclosing location, retain the feature answer. When several containing locations occur, select the geographic level the clue leaves missing rather than a broader or narrower one.
- Match grammatical number to what the clue requests and to the passage's naming of the referent. A singular definition or label request (such as "this is the term" or "this [singular category]") takes the singular term even when the surrounding passage lists plural examples; a plural group request or a referent that the passage conventionally names with a plural keeps that plural. Do not pluralize a singular term merely to echo an example list, and do not singularize a conventional plural referent.
- Identify whether the clue asks for a specific entity or a general type or class. When the clue supplies a category, role, or head noun for the requested entity, give only the distinguishing name: omit that supplied category word, legal suffix, subtitles or translations, unrequested descriptive qualifier, or other supplied head even when it appears in the full official name. Keep a required title, honorific, number, unit, or other component needed to identify the referent or distinguish it from passage-named alternatives. Except for a standard name component covered next, do not repeat a category or family-name component that the clue already supplies merely as context.
- Except for a partial-name clue governed by the next rule, keep a title, honorific, middle initial, number, unit, or other name component when the clue or passages use it as part of the standard identifying name. Add an extra category designator only when the bare name would otherwise be ambiguous with a different person or entity in the passages and the clue asks for that publication, organization, or work.
- When the clue supplies part of a person's name (for example, the first name or a title attached to the name) or supplies the category head for which only a proper name is missing, return only the missing distinguishing component, normally the surname or bare name. Do not repeat the supplied first name or category word, and do not add an unrequested middle initial or designator. Expand to a fuller name only when the shorter component would be ambiguous among passage-named candidates or the clue explicitly requests the full name. A title or honorific that the passages use as part of the standard name may be retained when the clue has not already supplied that component. When the clue does not supply part of the person's name, keep the full standard personal name; do not shorten to a surname merely because the surname appears in the passages.
- When the clue asks for a general type or class, including when it gives named examples of that class, and does not request a narrower subtype, return the general category term that the passages use for the described item rather than a narrower species, subtype, or proper emblem, unless the narrower term is necessary to identify the requested referent. Preserve meaningful spelling and grammatical distinctions required by the clue or passages; this correction targets unnecessary category or descriptive material, not necessary qualifiers.
- When a clue is a place or category label plus an isolated short word or letter string with no ordinary grammatical question, treat it as a possible wordplay prompt and test whether the letters rearrange into a passage-named place or other requested entity. If such a match exists, return the matched entity; do not return a passage term that literally contains or defines the cue word.
- When a clue consists only of one or two personal names, a person's honorific title, or a company or brand name with no explicit question, identify the affiliation or realm that the passages emphasize. For personal names, this is typically the shared state or country (for a single person, typically the country or state), and return that affiliation alone rather than a generic relationship description, a restatement of office or role, or a sentence about their roles. For a company or brand name, when the passages state its national origin or home country, return that country as the identifying affiliation rather than the full legal corporate name, a product type, or descriptive detail; if the clue explicitly asks for the company, brand, product, or name, return that requested entity or name instead. Apply this company-or-brand reading to a company or brand identifier, not to a product-only name unless the clue uses that name as the brand or company identifier. For a personal-name clue, if the clue explicitly asks for an office, relationship, or identity, return that instead.

## Error Avoidance

- Do not let the answer element expand into explanation: remove explanatory clauses, definitions, alternative names, and restatement of the question so it contains only the selected expression.
