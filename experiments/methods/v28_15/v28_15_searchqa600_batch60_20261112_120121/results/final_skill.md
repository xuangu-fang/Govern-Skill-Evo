# Question Answering Skill

## Workflow

- **Identify the entity or shared affiliation behind a declarative or bare-list clue.** When a clue is a declarative statement or bare list with no explicit yes/no question, name the person, show, place, thing, or shared jurisdiction/affiliation filling the described role; do not answer 'Yes' or a descriptive sentence. If the clue explicitly asks for a yes/no judgment, answer that instead. For a bare character list, give the work title rather than a description of the characters or their in-universe employer/organization. For a bare comma-separated list of personal names (including honorifics or titles attached to those names) with no separate role or category request and no relationship question, answer the shared jurisdiction or affiliation emphasized by the passages, not a role phrase or relationship label. If the clue separately supplies a role or category or asks for a relationship, answer that instead. When the clue is only a bare place name and the passages place it within a larger jurisdiction, answer that containing country/state rather than a descriptive fact about the place. For a clue that says a place is 'home to' a superlative feature or 'contains' a specified share of a resource, answer the state/country/continent that the passage identifies as containing that feature or share, not the local park/forest or the ice/water/material. If the clue explicitly asks for the local site's own name, answer that site. If several places are named, use the clue's singular 'this/that' and the exact percentage to select the matching place rather than answering the storage medium. When the clue is only a quoted line or first line and the passages identify a work and its author, answer the author, not the work title plus author, unless the clue explicitly asks for the work title. Exception: when the clue contains a descriptive predicate that already asks for the place or work, answer that city/state itself, not the containing country. After identifying the entity, apply the answer-form rules for names, titles, and terms rather than copying the passage's fullest description.
  - 'Johnny Cash' not 'Yes' (clue begins 'He recorded many of his famous songs like I Walk The Line...')
  - 'Barney Miller' not 'New York City Police Department' (bare fictional-character list; work title, not in-universe employer)
  - 'Massachusetts' not 'U.S. Senators from Massachusetts' (bare list naming two senators)
  - 'Monaco' not 'Sisters' (bare list naming two princesses of one principality)
  - 'Germany' not 'second-largest city in Germany' (bare place name; containing country)
  - 'California' not 'Ancient Bristlecone Pine Forest' (home-to clue; passage places the grove in California)
  - 'Antarctica' not 'glaciers and ice caps' (resource-share clue; answer the geographic place, not the frozen material)
  - 'Joyce Kilmer' not 'Trees by Joyce Kilmer' (quoted first line; author, not work title plus author)
  - Example: Salt Lake City (descriptive predicate: Utah city whose Christmas begins at Temple Square)
  - Example: Georgia (descriptive predicate: 'peachy state' with Brasstown Bald)

- **Answer the requested label, not a longer nearby expression.** When the clue asks for a name, title, term, or type, choose the expression fitting that slot rather than the longest nearby phrase. Do not append a category, type word, surname, or other descriptor/qualifier the clue already supplies, even when the appended word is a surname or party label after a requested nickname, a network word after an acronym, or a qualifier before the requested structure; do not copy a definition clause. For a 'this sport' clue, give the base sport name and do not prepend a subtype modifier. Do not expand a general term with the specific object; when the clue asks what general object something is shaped like or defined as, use the broader object rather than a subtype modifier. Retain a word integral to an established place or work name or needed to distinguish the item, and keep an established subtype when the clue asks for a type; drop a generic company or product suffix when the core name identifies the entity. For a government department or office, use the conventional short name supported by the clue and passages rather than a formal legal title, an inverted official name, or a jurisdiction-prefixed full title; keep the longer official name only when the clue explicitly asks for it or the short form would be ambiguous. Drop a translated subtitle when the original-language title alone identifies the work and the clue does not request the English title. Exception: include the publication-type word when the clue asks for the name given to a journal and the passage presents it as '[Name] magazine'. When the clue gives a foreign-language term for a common object and asks what it is, answer the common English noun rather than a bilingual or proper-name form. Exception: when the clue quotes a lead word together with an object and the passage defines that whole fixed expression as an idiom, answer the full established phrase including the lead word; not every quoted title component is included, only a fixed verb-plus-object or idiomatic expression.
  - 'Boss' not 'Boss Tweed' (clue asks for a moniker/nickname; do not append the surname)
  - 'pyre' not 'funeral pyre' (clue asks for the funeral structure; do not repeat the clue's qualifier)
  - 'Scream' not 'Scream film series' (clue asks for 'this film series')
  - 'Vicar' not 'usually called a vicar or rector' (bare definition 'An Anglican parish priest')
  - 'a transit' not 'transit of Venus' (term for Venus passing in front of the sun)
  - 'Hallmark' not 'Hallmark Cards' (company name)
  - 'the Treasury Department' not 'Department of the Treasury' (cabinet-department clue)
  - 'the Secretary of State' not 'United States Secretary of State' (cabinet-officer clue)
  - 'Forbes magazine' not 'Forbes' (name given to a journal; passage says 'Forbes magazine')
  - 'Il Postino' not 'Il Postino: The Postman' (original-language title alone identifies the film)
  - 'a balloon' not 'hot air balloon' (clue asks the general object a chair back is shaped like)
  - 'an eagle' not 'White Eagle (Orzeł Biały)' (foreign-language common noun)
  - 'hockey' not 'Ice hockey' (clue asks for 'this sport')
  - 'turning state's evidence' not 'state's evidence' (clue quotes a lead word with an object; passage defines the fixed idiom)
  - Example: Gorky Park (integral established name)
  - Example: Padre Island (integral established name)
  - Example: drip irrigation (established subtype when the clue asks for a type)

- **Answer the component the clue asks for, not the whole compound expression.** When a larger title, phrase, or slogan supplies a component the clue asks for, answer only that component. A leading/trailing ellipsis or partial-title cue calls for the missing word(s), and a 'Barber of' city clue calls for the city alone. A bare campaign slogan or paired phrase that names a principal (often by nickname) and a running mate or secondary figure calls for the principal person, not both names, unless the clue asks for the ticket, the running mate, or both. If the clue asks for the work title, supplies a complete line and asks for its source/author, or asks for the words that complete a line or speech, give the full requested title/author/phrase rather than only a fragment.
  - 'The Owl' not 'The Owl and the Pussycat' (year-plus-ellipsis title clue)
  - 'William Henry Harrison' not 'William Henry Harrison and John Tyler' (campaign slogan 'Tippecanoe and Tyler too' naming principal and running mate)

- **Resolve what a wordplay or anaphoric clue actually requests before answering.** When a clue supplies a prefix or fragment and asks for 'this/these', answer only the missing completion, not the whole compound. When the clue already contains a measure noun and asks for 'this' after 'of', answer the measured quantity, not the metric name. Exception: when a letter-removal or substitution clue defines both the original term and the term left after removing the letter, answer both terms as requested, not only the resulting term.
  - 'lingual' not 'multilingual' (prefix-completion clue)
  - 'faction & action' not 'Action' (letter-removal clue asking for both terms)
  - 'savings' not 'Personal savings rate' (clue asks for the measured quantity after 'rate of this')

- **Use the name form supported by the clue and passages, not an automatic short or full form.** For a person answer, use the common name label the passages support. When the clue asks for the person by role or deed and the passages use a fuller given-name-plus-surname as the standard name, give that form. Keep an honorific consistently attached in the passages. Do not add a formal given name, middle initial, or honorific that the passages do not support. When the passages' common label omits a middle initial, drop it, but retain an initial integral to the established name. When the clue or passages use a surname or nickname as the standard answer label, keep that short form and do not expand it with additional formal given names or combine a formal name with a nickname. Exceptions: when the clue asks for a first name, surname, or nickname, answer only that element; keep a first name or fuller form when it is the common answer label or the clue's distinguishing cue requires it.
  - 'Pablo Picasso' not 'Picasso' (clue asks by role or deed; passages use fuller standard name)
  - Example: Sir Isaac Newton (honorific consistently attached in passages)
  - Example: Lord Robert Baden-Powell (honorific consistently attached in passages)
  - 'Lyndon Johnson' not 'Lyndon B. Johnson' (passages' common label omits the middle initial)
  - Example: Susan B. Anthony (initial integral to the established name)
  - 'Brutus' not 'Marcus Junius Brutus' (clue identifies the Roman by deed)
  - 'Genet' not 'Jean Genet'
  - '(Jimmy) Doolittle' not 'James "Jimmy" Doolittle'
  - Example: Helmut (clue asks for a first name)
  - Example: John Fogerty (first name is the common answer label)
  - Example: Warren G. Harding (fuller form required by the clue)
  - Example: Stuart Sutcliffe (alliterative answer)
