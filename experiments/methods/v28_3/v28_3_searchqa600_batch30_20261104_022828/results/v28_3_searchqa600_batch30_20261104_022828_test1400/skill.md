# Question Answering Skill

## Selecting the requested answer

- When a clue asks for an institution using a supplied category word such as 'univ.', 'college', or 'school', treat a context title of the form '<distinctive name> State University/College' as an expanded formal title rather than the minimal answer. Use the shorter standalone distinctive name inside `<answer>` when the passages use it for the same institution and it is unambiguous; do not return the formal compound merely because it is the lead heading. For these institutional clues, this specific boundary controls over the general lead-heading preference.
- Keep the full formal name when the clue explicitly asks for the full, official, or formal name, or when the shorter form is ambiguous among context entities; this institutional boundary does not authorize shortening a personal full name or a full work title, which remain governed by existing answer-form rules.
- When a clue asks for a person through a specific year-bound accomplishment or count, such as a number of works on a bestseller list in a given year, and several named candidates appear in passages about that broad domain, select the candidate whose passage directly pairs the clue's year and accomplishment with that person; do not answer a different named person merely because that person appears in a biography or bestseller-related passage without support for the stated count or year.
- If no passage states the count exactly, prefer the candidate directly tied to the clue's specific year, title, or list over candidates supported only by generic bestseller or author associations; preserve the requested person/name form and do not substitute a work title or category.
- When a clue asks for 'this predator' or another animal characterized by a defining behavior/category and by one or more color variants, and several animal names occur in the context, choose the candidate whose passage directly applies the clue's defining category or behavior to that animal (for example, explicitly calling it a predator) together with a matching color variant where one is available; do not choose a different animal merely because a color word and an animal noun appear nearby, or because that animal is named as prey, an enemy, or an incidental species. Use the color and behavior only to identify the animal: return the requested animal name itself inside `<answer>` and do not append the color variant, use a broader category, or substitute an associated person, place, or work unless the clue explicitly asks for that specific color/type variant.
- When a clue asks which government cabinet department or agency was in charge of enforcing a named historical policy, and the passages contain both a direct statement that a department was charged with or given that enforcement and a separate statement that enforcement was later transferred to another department, answer the department identified by the direct original/primary enforcement statement, not a department that appears only in the later-transfer or general law-enforcement sentence, unless the clue explicitly asks about the later period, the transfer, or a current successor; answer at the clue's department or agency level rather than naming a subordinate bureau, and use the department or agency name form attached to the direct enforcement statement inside `<answer>`.
- When the context contains a passage that reproduces the clue as a direct question-and-answer, flashcard, or quiz item with a short answer, use that short answer's answer unit and name form inside `<answer>` rather than expanding it with a fuller name or descriptive wording; do not apply this when the clue explicitly asks for a full or formal name, or when the short answer is only an ambiguous category label. Preserve any required title, honorific, qualifier, unit, date, or distinguishing modifier.
- When the clue refers to the requested answer with a pronoun such as 'he' or 'she' and gives an action or accomplishment (for example, writing, explaining, inventing, or condemning), resolve the pronoun to the named person who performed that action and return that person's name inside `<answer>`; do not return the title, text, or other product of the action unless the question explicitly asks for that work or document. Keep this separate from clues that explicitly ask what a person was called, nicknamed, or known as.
- When the clue is only a bare list of character/person names or attributes, with no explicit question, and the passages identify one shared named entity (such as a place, institution, or work title) as the connection among the listed people, answer with that shared name alone inside `<answer>`; do not reply with a descriptive label that combines the people with their shared office or affiliation, such as returning 'U.S. Senators from [state]' instead of the shared state name, and do not prefix or append explanatory wording. Preserve any article or punctuation that is part of an established title. Do not apply this rule when the clue explicitly asks for multiple items or when the passages support only a broad category rather than a named shared value. This answer-unit restriction applies inside `<answer>` and does not change the need to identify the shared named entity from the passages.
- When the clue is only a bare personal name or a bare title-plus-name with no explicit question, and the passages consistently identify one named country, state, or other shared entity as that person's realm, nationality, or defining association, answer with that named entity alone inside `<answer>`; do not return a role phrase that restates the person's office, such as '<office> of <place>'. Treat this as an extension of the existing bare-list shared-entity rule: the office or person is already supplied by the clue and is not the requested common link. Do not apply it when the clue asks for the person or office, when the passages support multiple competing associations at the same level, or when the clue is a full descriptive question rather than a bare name/title.
- When the clue is only a bare locality name with no explicit question, and the passages consistently identify that locality as lying within one enclosing state, answer with that state alone inside `<answer>` rather than the locality name or a combined 'locality, state' string. If no state is named but the passages consistently identify one enclosing country, answer with that country alone. Prefer the immediate enclosing state when a broader country is also named; do not apply when the clue asks for the full location or when the passages support multiple competing enclosing states/countries at the same level. However, when the clue is only a named geographic feature followed by 'in [enclosing state/region]' with no explicit question, and the passages consistently identify that feature as the broader enclosing country's highest peak or another explicitly national superlative, answer with that broader country's established name alone inside `<answer>` rather than repeating the already-supplied state/region or returning a descriptive phrase such as '[country]'s highest mountain'; do not apply this exception when the clue explicitly asks for a city, state, or location, when the passages support multiple competing countries at the same level, or when no broader enclosing country is consistently identified. When the clue is only 'In' followed by two or more place names joined by 'and' or '&', with no explicit question, and the passages consistently place those places in one country, answer with the adjectival/demonym form associated with that country rather than the country noun; do not apply this plural-locality form when the clue asks for a location, when the places do not share one enclosing country, or when the passages support competing associations. This condition qualifies the general named-entity rule for this clue only; keep the general named-entity rule for other named entities. Do not apply this rule to a bare list of people or attributes; that case still returns the single shared named entity.
- When the clue lists sibling geographic features by their bare distinguishing words (for example, a list of the Great Lakes) and asks for one of them, and the context uses a compound proper name with a leading feature-type word for that feature (such as 'Lake Superior'), return the complete conventional name including the leading feature-type word inside `<answer>` rather than the bare distinguishing word alone; treat the sibling enumeration as shorthand that does not establish the bare word as the feature's full name, and let this specific case override the general shortest-supported-form preference. More generally, preserve a leading feature-type word such as 'Lake', 'Mount', or 'Cape' when the context uses it as an integral part of the feature's established name, even if the clue already supplies the feature class. Conversely, do not insert a trailing descriptive category noun (such as 'River' or 'Desert') that is not part of the established name, do not add a feature-type word that appears only as generic surrounding prose, and continue to prefer a shorter supported name when a direct question-and-answer/flashcard short answer or heading gives the bare form as the feature's established name. Preserve required qualifiers, units, dates, ranges, and distinguishing modifiers, and apply this boundary only inside the `<answer>` element.
- When passages supply multiple synonymous labels for the same requested role, title, or object, return one specific label rather than joining alternatives with 'or' or similar wording. Prefer the label presented as the standalone headword or direct definition in the context, and use another listed synonym only when the preferred label is not supported. Do not replace the requested role/object with a description of it. This rule does not apply when the question explicitly asks for more than one item or for a set of alternatives.
- When the question asks for a nickname, moniker, alias, epithet, or pseudonym, return the nickname or alias component alone inside `<answer>`; do not append the person's surname or a role phrase merely because the passage places it next to the nickname, unless the joined form is presented as the single established alias and the question asks for that full form. Preserve a multiword alias when the words together constitute the requested name.
- When the clue asks for a 'type,' 'kind,' or 'variety' within a category, or otherwise supplies the category or medium noun and asks for a named subtype, answer with the specific named subtype's proper-name core without appending the already-supplied category or medium noun, using the differentiating subtype phrase rather than repeating the umbrella category; retain that noun only when it is inseparable from the established name or needed to avoid ambiguity (for example, answer 'Doppler' rather than 'Doppler radar' when the clue asks for this type of radar). For a 'type' or 'kind' question, do not answer with '<type> job' or '<type> kind'. Do not substitute a temporal or descriptive phrase that merely modifies the umbrella category, and do not answer the umbrella category unless no subtype is supported. If the passages only support a broader category, return the narrowest supported category noun rather than inventing a subtype. Apply this subtype boundary only inside the `<answer>` element.
- When the question asks for a general category, shape, meaning, or ordinary object rather than for a specific named subtype or title, especially with generic wording such as 'one of these,' 'this,' or 'what is an X,' and the context supplies both a simple noun and a more specific descriptive variant or proper-name emblem for the same referent, answer with the simple noun that directly completes the question.
- Do not expand a simple category noun into a longer subtype, a proper-name emblem with a parenthetical or bilingual alternate, or other descriptive elaboration unless the question explicitly asks for the subtype, proper name, or full formal title, or unless the simple noun alone would not identify the requested referent.
- This generic-category boundary does not override the existing type/kind/variety rule or the rule to return a specific named entity when the question asks for the name of a particular person, work, place, toy, organization, or other named referent; preserve qualifiers, units, dates, ranges, and distinguishing modifiers needed to answer the question.
- When the clue asks for a general or common-noun referent rather than a specific named subtype, return the base common noun that the direct defining passage supplies inside `<answer>`.
- When the clue asks for an ordinary object or class, including an animal's usual home or shelter, and the context supplies a direct base noun, return that base noun alone inside `<answer>`; do not narrow it with a source animal, material, brand, or example unless the question explicitly asks for the source or a specific subtype. Preserve the singular or plural form and any article or qualifier needed by the question; keep using the specific subtype when the clue asks for a 'type,' 'kind,' or 'variety.'
- Do not substitute a specific example, herb, brand, or product that also fits the described use; when the clue is a definition of a class or ordinary thing, use the class term rather than a particular instance mentioned elsewhere.
- Do not prefix a location phrase that merely describes where the item is found to form a more specific compound name; keep the location only when the question explicitly asks for the local variety/subspecies or when the location-plus-noun form is the established name needed to identify the referent.
- Use the singular base noun when the clue frames one item ('one of these holes', 'this ...'), and do not pluralize it merely because nearby passages use plurals. When plural wording such as 'these' introduces a mass or uncountable category noun rather than a countable set of several distinct individuals or items, use the established singular form for that category noun and do not pluralize it merely to agree with the clue (for example, answer 'Champagne' rather than 'Champagnes'); keep the established plural for countable sets of people, things, or species or when the clue explicitly requests a plural set (for example, keep 'cardinals').
- Apply these boundaries only when the clue asks for the general/common referent; if it explicitly asks for a specific ingredient, subtype, example, proper name, or local variety, return that specific requested item instead.
- Except as provided by the fill-in rule below, for partial-compound, repeated-category, rate, and brand-name clues, return only the missing specific component or established short name inside `<answer>`: for a partial compound with a prefix followed by 'this', return the missing suffix rather than the completed compound; for a phrase such as a rate 'of this', return the thing whose rate is meant rather than repeating the rate word; and when the clue calls an entity 'this <company/category>', return the established brand or proper name without a trailing legal suffix that merely restates the clue's category. Keep the complete established name or completed phrase when the clue explicitly asks for the full, formal, or official name, when the short form would be ambiguous among context entities, or when the repeated category word is an inseparable part of the established name; otherwise apply the existing fill-in and direct question-and-answer/flashcard rules. Preserve titles, qualifiers, units, and articles that are required by the question rather than supplied only as the clue's category.
- When a fill-in clue requires a blank or missing word to be supplied, first distinguish a blank inside an established multiword name from a missing word in a quoted line. If the words shown in the clue together with the missing part form one established proper name, company or partnership name, or work title, and the clue asks for that named entity, return the complete established name or completed phrase inside `<answer>`, keeping its articles, ampersand, and punctuation; this applies even though the clue already displays part of that name, and it overrides the general instruction to omit a component the clue already supplies or to give only the shortest supported form.
- If instead the clue quotes a line of text and asks for its next or missing word, including terminal wording such as 'and this' or an ellipsis, return only that missing word inside `<answer>`, even when it is an ordinary generic noun that forms no title, and even when the passages name the work the line comes from or its author.
- For a quoted line with an explicit missing-word slot, this fill-in answer unit takes priority over the guidance that answers a complete quoted line with its author or creator, and over the guidance preferring a title-forming or named-entity completion over a generic one; use those rules only when the clue asks for the source, work, or author, or when the clue contains no missing-word slot. Do not override the direct question-and-answer/flashcard rule when a passage reproduces the clue with the answer already supplied.
- When the clue is a bare title-like fragment with a leading ellipsis before 'and ...' (for example, a year followed by a quoted fragment beginning with an ellipsis and a trailing phrase), and no explicit question asks for the whole work, treat the omitted leading words as the missing answer unit and return only that omitted leading component in its established form inside `<answer>`, not the complete title formed by adding the visible suffix. This exception governs the rule that supplies a complete established multiword name from a clue fragment; keep that complete-name rule when the clue asks for the named work or title, or when the visible words themselves form the requested name, and keep the ordinary missing-word rule for quoted lines.
- When the clue is only a bare quoted phrase, catchphrase, or work-title fragment with no explicit question and no ellipsis requiring completion, and the passages associate that phrase both with a derivative or spinoff title that adds or changes words and with a broader named series, franchise, or originating work, answer with the broader named series or work alone rather than expanding the fragment into the derivative title. When instead the clue is a complete quoted line or passage with sentence structure, rather than a bare title, title-with-year, or title-like fragment, and the passages identify it as a quotation from a work by a named author or creator, answer with that author or creator alone rather than the work title, unless the clue includes a named character or speaker label before a quoted line that has no missing-word or ellipsis gap; in that labeled case, answer with the work title identified by the passages rather than the author or creator, unless the clue explicitly asks for the author, writer, or creator. Preserve the existing rules for bare titles, title-like fragments, work-identification clues, and ellipsis fill-ins; if the clue explicitly asks for the work, return the work.
- Apply the title-fragment rule above only when the quoted words are not the complete exact title identified as the answer and the clue contains no work-type noun such as film, movie, episode, or show, or other wording asking for the derivative work; if the clue exactly names a work or explicitly asks for a film, show, or episode, return the exact requested title, except as provided in the next bullet for a bare creative-name clue with a consistent source or tribute relation.
- When the clue is only a bare name or title of a creative entity (a band, film, work, or similar), possibly with a year, medium, or 'classic'-type label and no explicit question, and the passages consistently identify that entity by a defining relation to another named entity, answer from that relation: if they identify it as a tribute or cover act of a named original group, return that original group's name alone inside `<answer>`; if they identify it as an adaptation or retelling of one other named work, return that source work's name alone, keeping its articles and punctuation.
- In these bare-name clues, do not answer with a tribute-band or similar descriptor phrase built from the clue's own label, with the derivative title the clue already prints, or with directors, cast, or other associated people. Because a bare title plus a year, medium, or 'classic' label is not a request for that title, this rule governs over the general instruction to return the exact title named in the clue.
- Do not apply it when the clue contains a work-type noun such as film, movie, episode, or show, or other wording asking for the work or derivative itself; when the clue asks for those people; when the passages offer multiple competing source or tribute relations at the same level; or when a bare entity clue has no such source/tribute relation in the passages.
- Preserve the existing bare-list shared-entity rule and the ellipsis/fill-in rule for clues that clearly omit a word; keep articles or punctuation that are part of an established title.
- When the clue is only a quoted campaign slogan or paired phrase naming a principal figure and a secondary or running-mate figure, with no explicit question asking for both, and the passages identify one as the principal candidate or hero and the other as a running mate or supporting person, treat the clue as asking for the principal figure: return that principal person alone inside `<answer>` and do not return an 'X and Y' pair unless the clue explicitly asks for both. This overrides the bare-list shared-entity treatment for this paired-figure slogan/phrase, but it does not apply to bare lists of works or attributes whose shared named connection is the requested answer; keep both names when the question asks for a pair or set.
- When the clue presents a wordplay, malapropism, or spoonerism phrase and the passages explicitly map it to an intended corrected phrase (for example, with a bracketed correction or an 'instead of' statement), return the intended corrected phrase alone inside `<answer>`; do not return the name of the device/category, the speaker, or the erroneous phrase itself.
- Apply only when the corrected phrase is directly supplied or unmistakably identified in the context; if the question explicitly asks for the device name, definition, or speaker, return that requested item instead.
- When the supplied question and passages present a proper name in an unaccented/plain-ASCII spelling, preserve that visible spelling inside `<answer>` and do not introduce diacritics or accent marks that are absent from the supplied text; if the passages consistently use an accented spelling, use that accented form instead. This is a fidelity-to-context rule for the answer string, not a general normalization or transliteration rule, and it does not override a required qualifier, title, or name component.
- When selecting among multiple context-supported established names for a named place, work, landmark, organization, or other non-person named entity, follow the clue rather than automatically taking the lead heading. If the passages give an English established name alongside a foreign-language heading for the same identified place or work, and the clue is in English without asking for a native or formal name, use the English established form inside `<answer>`. If a landmark has both a later sponsor/current brand name and a historic/original name tied to the clue's distinctive feature, use the historic/original name unless the clue asks for the current or official name; if that historic name has variant spellings, use the spelling from the passage that directly supports the clue's distinctive detail. Do not append an alternate name or parenthetical expansion, and preserve a requested native/formal name or a context-supported short form. This selects among names already supplied rather than renaming the entity, and it controls over the following lead-heading preference in these cases.
 - The earlier flashcard short-answer and leading feature-type guidance are modified for a clue that already supplies a generic category or feature word (for example 'this missile' or 'mount') and asks for the named entity, or for the name it shares with a country: return the proper-name/shared-name core inside `<answer>`; do not retain or append that supplied category/feature word merely because a passage's flashcard answer, heading, or sentence uses '<ProperName> missile' or '<Mount> <Name>'.
 - Preserve the category/feature word only when it is an inseparable part of an established proper name and omitting it would fail to identify the referent (for example, an established 'Sea' title), and keep the leading feature-type form when the clue directly asks for the geographic feature itself rather than a name shared with a country.
 - When a clue asks for a relative with wording such as 'this relative of his/hers' and a passage directly supplies the relation as a possessive phrase ('his mother', 'her father'), include the possessive determiner in the answer unit inside `<answer>`; do not reduce the answer to the bare relation noun. Apply only to relational nouns directly tied to the possessive in the clue or passages, not to proper names or unrelated answers, and keep the relation word's singular/plural form and any required qualifier.
 - When a clue asks for a person and a passage that directly states the clue's accomplishment identifies that person with an honorific form such as 'Sir Isaac Newton', use that honorific-inclusive name form inside `<answer>` even if the lead heading or other passages use a bare 'Isaac Newton'. Do not drop a context-attached honorific merely because the clue supplies a role or office; such an honorific is not a role title that merely restates the clue and is retained under the general person-name rule, while a role title that merely restates the clue remains excluded. Conversely, do not add an honorific that the passages do not attach to the person.
- When the answer is a named person, organization, work, or other named entity, return the specific name or title itself rather than repeating the umbrella category, using the proper-name form given by the direct answer passage or heading. After identifying the referent, check whether the clue already supplies part of the name or the category: if the clue gives only a person's given name and asks for that person, return the missing surname or established short name alone rather than the full given-plus-surname form; if the clue says 'this <category>' (for example, this mint, city, state, or show) and the context gives a proper name that identifies the referent, return that proper name without appending the clue's category. When the context gives both a full work title and a shorter title that it repeatedly uses as the work's established name, and the clue asks for 'this ... show/book/film' without requesting the full or formal title, use the shorter established title. Keep the fuller form when the clue explicitly asks for a full or formal name, when the short form would be ambiguous among passage entities, or when the category word is inseparable from the proper name and no shorter supported name exists; do not reduce a personal full name merely because a surname appears incidentally in the passages. When the clue itself supplies a generic category, medium, role, or modifier that the answer would otherwise repeat, use the shortest context-supported form that still identifies the referent inside `<answer>`: omit the repeated generic element or use an established short name given elsewhere in the passages rather than a longer name-plus-category form. Do not append a generic feature noun that appears only in surrounding prose; keep a category word only when it is an inseparable part of the established proper name and no shorter supported name exists, or when omitting it would create ambiguity, and use a concise form that identifies the requested referent: except as provided above for a clue that already supplies a given name or category word, use the name form given by the direct answering sentence or lead heading for the requested person, retaining a title or honorific such as 'Sir' that is consistently attached to the person's name there, even if another heading uses a shorter bare name, but do not add a middle initial or a fuller expanded name when the passages also give a common shorter form and the clue does not ask for a full or formal name; do not retain a title, rank, or office that merely restates the role the clue already assigns to the person (for example, do not prepend 'President' when the clue says 'this president', or 'Count' when the clue calls him a count); keep a title or honorific only when the passages present it as part of the person's established name and the clue does not merely supply it as a role; when the question explicitly asks for a particular name component such as a first name, return that component in the form the passages give it rather than the full name; keep a qualifier needed to distinguish the referent; do not add a parenthetical expansion, alternate name, or family/category descriptor that the clue already implies; do not override the direct question-and-answer/flashcard short-answer rule. Apply this boundary only inside the `<answer>` element, while preserving any necessary qualifier, unit, date, range, or distinguishing modifier that the question requires.
