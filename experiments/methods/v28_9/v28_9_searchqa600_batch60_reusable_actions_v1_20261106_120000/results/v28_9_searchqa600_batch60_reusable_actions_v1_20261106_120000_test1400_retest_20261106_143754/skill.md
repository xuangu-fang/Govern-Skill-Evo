# Question Answering Skill

(No learned rules yet. Rules will be added through the reflection process.)
- Express the selected answer in its conventional, minimal identifying form. If the passage gives a main item followed by parenthetical exceptions or occasional alternatives, answer the main item and omit the alternatives. Give a commonly used acronym alone rather than appending its expansion, and do not add an extra generic noun or family name solely as explanation. Preserve an established honorific such as 'Sir' when the passages use it as part of the person's name.
- When the clue already supplies a person's name, a modifier, or a category, add only the missing distinguishing element. Omit optional middle initials or middle names unless needed to distinguish, do not repeat a clue-supplied modifier before the core term, and do not append the surname to a nickname or moniker requested by the clue.
- When a clue consists only of one or more associated place, person, or character names and states no relation, answer the encompassing label the passages attribute to them. For a single place, use the encompassing country or region; for a place pair whose wording calls for a nationality (such as an 'In X & Y' pairing), use the shared demonym. For character names, use the containing work, organization, or affiliation. In each case use the shared category the clue is classifying them under; do not choose an incidental nearby feature, an in-universe setting, or a role over that encompassing label, and do not answer with a relationship sentence.
- When a clue is only a bare brand, company, family, or product name and states no relation, answer the encompassing origin the passages emphasize—for a company or brand, its country (give the country name, not a demonym)—rather than expanding to the formal legal company name or a particular model. Use a classification label instead only when the passages clearly make that classification, rather than origin, the encompassing answer; use the formal name only when the clue explicitly asks for it or the passages make it the requested label.
- Choose the answer endpoint by the clue's requested slot, not by the longest official title in the passage. When the clue already names the category or descriptor (for example university, company, gland, missile, or mount) or asks for the term for a described phenomenon, answer with the distinctive name the passages use for that entity rather than the full formal title; strip formal category or descriptor words such as 'State University', 'Motor', 'gland', or 'missile' and give the common stem, and if the clue is built around a shared name, give that shared bare name. Do not append the phenomenon's object unless the shorter form would fail to identify the entity or the clue explicitly asks for the fuller title. Keep the longer form only when the bare form would be a common adjective or the clue explicitly asks for the full title, using the passage's established full label in that case (for example, 'Lake Superior'); use the bare proper name when it is independently identifying.
- When the passages give more than one name for the same place or venue, choose the form the clue's wording and passages establish as the common identifying label, not automatically the first or current official title. Prefer an English/common name over a foreign-language official form when the clue uses English and the common form identifies the same entity; do not default to a later corporate rebrand merely because it heads a source, but use the historic name when the clue's identifying details and the passages make that form the established label. Include a state qualifier in the conventional 'City, State' form only when the clue's wording makes the state part of the requested identification (for example, by referring to 'its state' without naming it) and the passages supply that form; omit it when the clue already supplies it.
- Resolve relation direction before answering: when a clue says 'the energy from this' or otherwise identifies what an instrument measures, return the source or entity from which the measured quantity comes (for example, the Sun), not the radiation, quantity, or technical description of the measurement.
- When the clue asks for a relative with wording such as 'this relative of his/her' and the answer is a kinship noun, include the possessive determiner in the answer ('his mother', not bare 'mother'). In that construction the possessive identifies the particular relative and should not be dropped as a clue-supplied modifier.
- For a person answer, do not automatically expand a unique identifying surname to a full name merely because the passage supplies it. Use the form the clue's description calls for: the conventional surname when no same-surname person needs distinguishing; a fuller name when the clue requests it or when the full name is the established identifying label in the passage. Keep an established honorific when the passages use it as part of the person's name.
- When the clue asks for a 'type' or 'kind' of something and supplies a distinguishing feature, normally prefer a passage-supported subtype that pairs that feature with the category; do not default to the broader category or to a temporal modifier such as 'modern' just because a generic definition sentence repeats the feature, and if no specific subtype is supported, answer the broad category in its simplest form. When the clue uses 'the [modifier] type of this' or similar wording, however, treat the supplied modifier as part of the clue, not the answer: give only the unnamed general category, and do not repeat the modifier before the category even if the passage uses the combined phrase.
- Distinguish complete recognizable quotations or bare work-title/year cues from incomplete fragments. For a complete quoted line or a bare title with a year and no explicit request for the work, answer the creator or person the passages associate with that work (author, director, performer), not the work title itself; reserve completion or wordplay treatment for genuinely incomplete fragments, and follow an explicit request for the work when one is present. When a character or speaker label precedes the quoted line, even with an ellipsis, treat it as a cue to the work containing the line: give the work rather than continuing the quotation unless the clue explicitly asks for an author or specifically asks to finish or continue the quotation (for example, with a blank or 'finish the line'); an ellipsis alone is not such a request.
- Treat a supplied slogan or distinctive catchphrase as a cue to the associated person even when the wording is slightly truncated; do not complete the missing letters or words, and do not substitute the work title, unless the clue explicitly asks for completion or names the work or slot requested. When the slogan names a running mate or secondary figure after 'and', answer the principal person or candidate, not the whole pair, unless the clue asks for the ticket.
- When a clue quotes or names a work, phrase, or wordplay, identify the requested slot before choosing among salient names. Complete an incomplete quotation with the next word(s) rather than giving the work title, except when a character or speaker label makes the quotation a cue to its containing work and the clue does not specifically ask to finish or continue it; for a quoted spoonerism or malapropism that does not ask for the error term, give the intended/corrected phrase rather than the phenomenon label. For a bare later-work title/year cue whose passages emphasize adaptation or retelling, answer the older source/retelling target when no creator is requested, not a same-title stage version or director. These do not displace the quotation rule above or an explicit request for a work or creator.
- For a place answer, identify the level the clue requests. If the clue only names a place or feature (including 'X in [state/province]') or asks for a described feature's location or container ('home to...' or 'that contains...'), answer the encompassing state or country the passages supply, not a superlative description, a specific sub-site such as a forest or park, or the material/feature itself. If the clue asks for the feature or site itself, answer that feature/site, not a nearby incidental feature.
- Answer only the slot left open by the clue. When the clue already supplies a prefix, modifier, category word, or generic noun that appears in a candidate phrase, give only the missing element: the stem after a supplied prefix or the bare term after a supplied descriptor; do not echo the full phrase or append a category/descriptor such as 'company' or 'rate' when the bare name/term identifies the entity. If dropping the supplied word would leave only a common adjective rather than an identifying name, keep or restore the category noun (for example, 'smooth muscles'), unless the clue's wording explicitly asks for the bare stem/term (as in a 'multi-this' word-formation cue).
