# Live Mathematical MCQ Heuristics

## Option Comparison
- Compare all options before committing. The correct choice is often the strongest statement justified by the question, while nearby distractors are weaker, overstrong, or miss an equality case.
- Before choosing among substantive statements, construct the strongest supported form of the theorem. Compare every substantive option with it, checking these strength changes: compactly localized or truncated estimates versus global estimates under the same hypotheses; parameters or constants depending on a point or object versus uniform choices that work for all; “at most” or existence versions versus exact degree, coefficient, or parameter range; an inequality containing an extra nonnegative boundary or correction term versus the same inequality without that term; and a structural property or classification versus a numerical consequence of it. When an option asserts that another listed statement is correct but a stronger result can be proved, treat that meta-option as a live candidate when the stem asks for a valid statement, a complete characterization, or a complete classification. Select the meta-option only if every substantive option is unsupported, overstrong, or only a genuinely intermediate consequence (including one of the weaker or partial forms above) and no substantive statement already gives the supported stronger form. If a substantive statement already gives the strongest supported form, choose that substantive statement.
- When two options differ only by an added conjunct, decide whether that conjunct follows from the hypotheses before treating the longer option as stronger. Added converses, existence claims for every object satisfying a description, closedness requirements, extra invariances, or extra relations are often unsupported strengthenings. Reject an option whose only advantage is such an unsupported conjunct. If the conjunct is supported, retain it with the rest of the characterization. If supported parts are split among different substantive options and their conjunction is a strictly stronger statement absent from every substantive option, the option asserting that a stronger result can be proved is the live candidate.
- Track exact quantifiers such as "there exists", "for every", "if and only if", and "exactly when".
- When alternatives contrast p-local homotopy equivalence with an unqualified homotopy equivalence, treat agreement of p-primary invariants or p-local equivalences as support only for the p-local conclusion. Do not upgrade to integral homotopy equivalence unless the option explicitly supplies a compatibility or assembly argument beyond matching the prime-local data. Choose the option whose equivalence symbol and sufficient hypothesis match the evidence: keep a p-local conclusion for p-local hypotheses, and reserve an unqualified equivalence for a statement that asserts integral equivalence itself.
- When one option gives only a necessary condition and another gives both the necessary condition and a supported sufficient condition, keep the necessary and sufficient parts together rather than selecting the merely necessary version.

## Theorem-Level Precision
- Check whether an option weakens the conclusion by dropping a characterization, equality clause, or full equivalence.
- Check whether an option overstates the theorem by upgrading regularity, removing scale restrictions, or changing an existential statement into a universal one.

## Hypotheses
- Verify the hypotheses and domain carefully. Distractors often keep the theorem shape but alter the required assumptions.
- Pay close attention to equality cases, extremal conditions, and whether a result applies to the full family or only a restricted subfamily.
- When threshold alternatives differ by whether a phenomenon is critical only at the threshold or for every parameter at or above it, check the exact parameter range stated. Also check whether the claim concerns the underlying sampled tree or block structure versus only a decorated version; do not replace a direct claim about the underlying object by a decorated-object analogue, or restrict an at-or-above regime to equality, unless the theorem specifically requires that distinction. Select the alternative whose object and parameter range match the supported result.

## Final Answer
- Output the final answer as the single option label only.
