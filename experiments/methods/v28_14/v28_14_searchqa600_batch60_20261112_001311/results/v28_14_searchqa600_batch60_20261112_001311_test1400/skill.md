When a passage identifies a preferred or usual member amid rarer alternatives and the clue does not ask for all, answer that member rather than listing the alternatives. Use the shorter identifying term when it fills the slot and do not add a generic category word such as 'power'; for a longer established name with an appended category or brand word, use the shorter established name if it fills the clue's slot: 'the Amazon', not 'Amazon River'; 'Hallmark', not 'Hallmark Cards'. Keep the appended word when the bare word is only a modifier and not an established name for the target by itself, as with 'Padre Island'. When the clue supplies the category noun and asks for a nickname, short name, or short title, answer the core name without repeating that category. When the passages themselves use a distinctive short form for a work or act and the clue does not ask for the full official title, prefer that short form over an extended form whose extra words are a descriptive category or subtitle. Do not append explanatory labels such as 'tribute band', and do not add a subtitle or location that merely elaborates a title, when the short form alone is an established, unambiguous identifier. This is the exception to 'Keep words that belong to an established proper title or event name' when the extra words are only a subtitle/location: the passage's short form controls unless the clue asks for the full title or the extension is needed to disambiguate. Keep the extension when the bare core is generic or ambiguous, when the extension is the established name, or when the clue asks for the full title. Keep the category word when the bare form would primarily name a different entity and the longer form is needed to identify the requested publication or thing; do not append a category word merely because a passage capitalizes the longer phrase. Do not repeat a generic noun the clue already supplies, and drop extra generic modifiers when a series or class is requested. Keep words that belong to an established proper title or event name.

For a generic type or sport, use the base noun rather than an added subtype. Keep the category word when the bare word is not an established name for the target, or when the full form is an established title, proper name, or idiom, or is needed to disambiguate.

- **Match the clue's name form and base institution.** When the clue itself supplies a shorter name form, use that form and do not add middle initials or a longer legal name. When the clue names a base institution plus a separate campus or location, answer the base institution. When the clue asks for a person by role without supplying a name form, use the person's common identifying name: retain an honorific such as Sir or Lord when it belongs to the passage's identifying phrase, but do not add an unused middle initial or formal first name. Keep an established common full name such as Stephen Hawking or Leonardo da Vinci, but if the clue asks for a nickname or moniker, answer the nickname alone rather than appending the surname; if it asks for only a first name, give only that name.
   - 'George Bush' not 'George H. W. Bush' (clue says 'George Bush')
   - 'the University of Wisconsin' not 'University of Wisconsin–Superior' (clue says 'this state university at Superior, not Madison')
   - Example: 'Stephen Hawking'; 'Leonardo da Vinci' (clue asks for a person by role and the passage gives the full name)
   - 'Sir Isaac Newton' not 'Isaac Newton'
   - 'Lyndon Johnson' not 'Lyndon B. Johnson'
   - 'Boss' not 'Boss Tweed' (clue asks for William Marcy Tweed's moniker)
- **Match the orthography of the requested term.** Use the spelling and spacing shown in the clue and passages. Do not add diacritics absent from the clue or passage, and use the spaced or hyphenated form shown there instead of a variant imported from general knowledge. Keep an established hyphenated form when the passage itself supplies it.
   - 'Vaclav Havel' not 'Václav Havel'
   - 'Picture in Picture' not 'Picture-in-Picture'
   - Example: 'hit-and-run' (passage supplies the hyphenated form)
- When the clue asks for a term or type ('this term for...', 'one of these birds'), answer the common term or class noun, not a specific proper-named instance, event, or work title built from it: 'a transit', not 'Transit of Venus'; 'a falcon', not 'Maltese Falcon'. When the clue asks for a generic type, or translates a generic noun, and supplies no distinguishing modifier, answer the base common noun; do not add a species name, national epithet, or other modifier taken from a passage unless the clue asks for that specific variety. If the clue instead points to a specific named event or work, such as asking for the film title, keep the proper name.
   - 'the lamprey' not 'Sea lamprey'
   - 'an eagle' not 'White eagle'
- **Answer the shared state, country, or organization for a bare list of names.** When the question is only a comma- or ampersand-separated list of named people with no explicit question, answer the shared state, country, or organization the passages state for them, as a short noun—not an explanatory sentence or an incidental relationship label. If the clue explicitly asks for the relationship or family role, answer that instead.
   - 'Monaco' not 'Sisters' (clue: 'Princess Caroline & Princess Stephanie')

- **Answer the containing jurisdiction for a bare place phrase.** When the clue is a bare geographic name or descriptive place phrase with no explicit 'this [place type]' question, answer the containing state or country that the passages supply as the broader named jurisdiction, not a superlative description or a local named site. If the clue explicitly asks for a city, state, province, or other place type, answer that requested type.
   - 'Australia' not 'Australia's highest mountain' (bare mountain-name clue whose passages place it in Australia)
   - 'California' not 'Ancient Bristlecone Pine Forest' (grove described in the White Mountains of California)
