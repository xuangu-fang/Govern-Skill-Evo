# Question Answering Skill

(No learned rules yet. Rules will be added through the reflection process.)
- When the question is terse—a named person or place, a pair of names, or a phrase containing an abbreviation or code—infer the omitted relation from the clue format rather than treating the supplied identifier as the whole answer. For a place clue, use the grammatical frame to decide whether the answer is a location or a shared nationality or language adjective: a bare single place can call for its containing state or country, while a pair of cities introduced by 'In' can call for their shared adjective form rather than the country noun. If the frame is ambiguous, choose the form that completes the implied phrase without inventing a relation. For a bare pair of place names, answer their shared containing place only when the frame asks for a location; otherwise give the shared adjective form. For an abbreviation or code, answer the place it denotes or expands to. Do not substitute a relationship description, a sentence about the entity, or an associated landmark.
- For a clue written as a dated plot logline or other narrative summary, decide whether the requested slot is the described literary work or the character performing the action. When the clue summarizes a plot without asking 'who' or naming 'this warrior', answer the work named by the context, not the protagonist.
- For a terse clue consisting only of a person's name or a title-plus-name, resolve the omitted relation from the passage and answer the single related proper noun on the other side of that relation. Do not answer with a role description, a tenure phrase, or a phrase describing the named person.
- When the clue asks for a relative ('this relative of his') and the passage expresses the relation with a possessive, give the kinship term with the possessive ('his mother') rather than the bare role noun, unless the possessive would be redundant or the clue explicitly asks only for the relation type.
- When the clue asks for a term or the word for a general phenomenon, answer the generic term itself rather than a longer proper-name phrase naming a specific occurrence or example. Check whether the requested slot is the term or the named event before stopping.
- When a clue gives a global superlative with a stated dimension (such as a conflict larger than all others combined), select the candidate that is globally superlative in that dimension or that the passages otherwise establish as the largest relevant conflict. Do not choose an entity merely because a nearby passage uses a comparative like 'surpassed' for a narrower or different comparison; check the comparison's scope and the measured attribute before answering.
- When the clue already supplies a generic category noun or a title fragment, answer only the missing distinctive part and do not append a performer or creator or repeat the supplied noun. Omit optional official or classifier tails. Keep a supplied word, including a classifier or title, only when it is integral to the established proper name and needed for disambiguation, rather than merely a generic classifier.
- Answer at the level of specificity the clue requests. If the clue asks for a base category, a shape, a nickname, or a title without a supplied subtitle, give the shortest term that satisfies all clue attributes; do not add a narrower species/emblem label, a translated subtitle, an appended organizational category such as 'Party', or a device modifier such as 'hot air' merely because the context contains it. Add such a modifier only when the clue itself asks for that narrower form.
- Keep the final answer to the requested value. Omit parenthetical glosses, acronym expansions, explanatory qualifiers, extra family names or roles already supplied by the clue, and exhaustive lists of alternatives. When several candidates appear, answer the most typical or representative one alone unless the question explicitly asks for all; a source that marks one with 'especially' or 'particularly' identifies that representative. Include an honorific such as 'Sir' only when it is part of the person's usual name, and add other wording only to resolve a genuine ambiguity; do not prepend office titles or descriptive labels.
- Return one conventional name or noun for the requested entity, not a decorated variant. Use the whole established name when a classifier or head word is integral to it, even if the remaining word can stand alone; do not shorten that head word away. Omit nonessential descriptive or narrowing modifiers, legal or corporate suffixes, a second co-named person already supplied by the clue, and parenthetical alternates. When passages give several forms, choose the form tied to the clue's distinctive wording—normally the common English or historically relevant name rather than an alternate-language, later sponsored, or current form—and do not list more than one name.
- For instrument or measurement clues phrased as “the energy from this,” identify the source that the preposition points to and answer that source, not the radiation type or quantity the instrument measures.
- When a clue quotes an incomplete line, decide whether the clue continues that line or only attributes it. If a continuation cue such as “pail and” this syntactically asks for the next words, fill the quoted continuation itself; do not replace it with the title, author, or genre of the containing work. If the clue is only a speaker label followed by a quoted literary line ending in an ellipsis, with no continuation cue, identify the work the line comes from rather than completing the quotation.
- When a terse clue is built around a named work, a character alias, or a tribute/cover group, resolve the relation left implicit: give the underlying source work, the original act, or the character’s usual identity. Do not stop at a genre description, a relationship label such as “U2 tribute band,” or the preliminary alias alone.
- Match the requested term’s orthography to the clue and context: for an acronym expansion, use the phrase as printed in the passage rather than a hyphenated or re-capitalized rewrite; for a personal name, use the plain spelling supplied or implied by the context and do not add diacritics that are absent there.
- When a clue explicitly describes a deletion or substitution in a word—an original term loses or changes a letter or part to become a second term—answer both the original term and the resulting term if the clue presents both sides of that transformation. Do not stop at the final transformed word or give only the source term.
- For a bare place description or fragment with no explicit answer-class noun (such as 'Mount X in Y', 'Home to...', or '...That contains...'), answer the containing geographic unit that the clue's description points to, not an internal named park or forest and not the material contained. If the clue explicitly names the answer class ('this city', 'this state', 'this forest'), answer that class instead.
