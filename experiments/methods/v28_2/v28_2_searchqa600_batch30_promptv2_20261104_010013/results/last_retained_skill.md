# Question Answering Skill

## Entity and answer form

- When the requested answer is a named entity, title, role, or other short label, return one concise label rather than a combined alternative. If the passages give two equivalent labels for the same role (for example, "usually called X or Y"), select the single label presented as the usual/standard term for the clue and omit the other; do not answer "X or Y". Do not add a generic type word or category label to a proper name merely because the context describes that type. If a geographic clue identifies a feature by its branches/tributaries and the passages also use a bare proper name for that feature, prefer the bare proper name unless the longer form is the established name needed to identify the referent. Preserve a feature/type word when it is part of the established name and the clue requires that entity (for example, a proper park or island name), and preserve other context-standard full forms when they directly identify the requested referent.
- When a clue describes a referent through role/title labels and the passages also name a performer or associated real person, first resolve whether the clue asks for the character/title or for the performer. Prefer the shared character/title described by the role/title labels unless the clue explicitly asks for the actor/performer, such as by asking who played the role or naming the performer as the target. Do not substitute an associated person merely because that person is mentioned in the passages; preserve the requested entity and relation.
- For a named entity or person, return the name form that directly identifies the requested referent and matches the context's conventional name, while keeping the answer element concise.
  - Keep a standard honorific or title when the passages consistently use it as part of the person's name; do not reduce such a name to a bare surname or given name when the honorific is part of the established form.
  - Do not append parenthetical acronym expansions, aliases, definitions, extra family names, or category labels already supplied by the clue. If the clue gives the category, return the distinguishing name/title; if the clue or context uses an organization's acronym or short name, use that short form alone.
  - Preserve necessary words in legitimate multiword official titles and full names when those words are themselves the requested answer; this rule removes unrequested additions and restores a context-standard honorific, not required components of the answer.
- When the question is only a bare comma-separated list of character names or role labels and the passages identify a shared work, series, team, or show, answer that shared title/name. Return the title/name alone; do not replace it with a sentence describing the listed individuals or with a category label. If the clue explicitly asks about the individuals themselves or their relationship rather than their shared work, follow that request instead.
- When the passages introduce a natural phenomenon or category term with a singular headword and the clue asks what the described things are, return the singular headword as the answer. Do not pluralize the term merely to agree with plural wording in the clue. Keep an established plural answer when the passage and requested class are conventionally plural, such as a people/group name or a plural animal category.
