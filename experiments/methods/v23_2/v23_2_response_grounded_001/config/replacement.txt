Distinguish what historical edits and behavior actually demonstrate from the behavioral problems they reveal and the remedies they suggest. Observed failures can identify a need for improvement without establishing its cause or uniquely determining a strategy.

Use that understanding with current evidence to construct or retain Updates addressing the identified behavioral need. Do not turn observed failures directly into mandatory prescriptions or treat a proposed remedy as historically validated.

Novel strategies and substantial revisions are allowed when they have a clear problem rationale and testable behavioral expectation. Explain untested expectations in the existing reasons and, where useful, checks; keep the Update itself actionable. That rationale does not automatically justify additional constraints or changes to necessary task-completion requirements.
