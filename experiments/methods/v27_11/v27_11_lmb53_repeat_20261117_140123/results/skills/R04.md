# Live Mathematical MCQ Heuristics

## Option Comparison
- Compare all options before committing. The correct choice is often the strongest statement justified by the question, while nearby distractors are weaker, overstrong, or miss an equality case.
- When an option asserts that a stronger result can be proven, or otherwise presents itself as a generic stronger-result choice, keep that option open as the provisional choice even if the question asks only which statement is valid. Then test each substantive detailed option as a claim to maximality: ask whether it weakens the result, overstates it, or adds an unsupported clause. If no detailed option states exactly the strongest supported conclusion, select the generic stronger-result option; if one detailed option states exactly that strongest supported conclusion, select that detailed option instead.
- Track exact quantifiers such as "there exists", "for every", "if and only if", and "exactly when".
- When a stronger-result choice is present and the remaining choices are close variants of a formula, bound, or structural claim that differ at an index, coefficient, exponent, quantifier, domain boundary, or equality/strictness clause, do not infer that the most detailed, strongest-looking, or numerically optimistic variant is established. Test each distinguishing clause against the stated hypotheses; reject a variant that adds an unsupported clause or changes a hypothesis. If the variants cannot be separated by the question's data and no variant has been shown to be the exact maximal statement, keep the stronger-result choice; a detailed variant is eligible only after its exact distinction from the closest rival is independently confirmed.

## Theorem-Level Precision
- Check whether an option weakens the conclusion by dropping a characterization, equality clause, or full equivalence.
- When choices contrast a full equivalence with a prime-by-prime or local equivalence obtained from matching local invariants, do not select the full equivalence merely because it is stronger, even when the full equivalence is the generic stronger-result choice. The global statement requires an additional assembly or integrality argument; if only the local or prime-by-prime equivalences have been established, select the local or prime-by-prime option as the strongest supported conclusion.
- Check whether an option overstates the theorem by upgrading regularity, removing scale restrictions, or changing an existential statement into a universal one.

## Hypotheses
- Verify the hypotheses and domain carefully. Distractors often keep the theorem shape but alter the required assumptions.
- Pay close attention to equality cases, extremal conditions, and whether a result applies to the full family or only a restricted subfamily.

## Final Answer
- Output the final answer as the single option label only.
