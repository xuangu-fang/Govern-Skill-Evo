# Live Mathematical MCQ Heuristics

## Option Comparison
- Compare all options before committing. The correct choice is often the strongest statement justified by the question, while nearby distractors are weaker, overstrong, or miss an equality case.
- Compare near-duplicate substantive candidates by isolating the single modifier that separates them, such as a full gradient versus one coordinate derivative, a local statement versus a universal-cover statement, "at most" versus "exactly/only", or the object named in the question versus a decorated version. Select the more modified or restrictive substantive candidate only when a hypothesis or standard theorem specifically requires that modifier; a request for the strongest statement does not by itself justify adding an unsupported restriction or replacing the named object. Prefer the direct substantive candidate over an unsupported modifier. This preference does not override the separate meta-option rule: if the direct substantive candidate is still weaker than the stronger result asserted by a compatible meta-option, select the meta-option. Keep the direct choice when no compatible meta-option and no theorem-supported modifier requires a change.
- Track exact quantifiers such as "there exists", "for every", "if and only if", and "exactly when".
- On a prompt that asks for the strongest, most complete, most general, exact, or equivalent statement, classification, or characterization—including a "which statement is valid" prompt whose options differ in strength—make a final triage of every substantive candidate before choosing when an option says that a remaining substantive option is correct but a stronger result can be proved. Classify each substantive candidate as (i) an exact statement of the requested conclusion, (ii) a removable restriction or weakened quantifier, such as a special class, extra hypothesis, one direction, lower regularity, an almost-everywhere version, or an existence or necessary-condition claim, or (iii) an unsupported overstatement with an added equality, global characterization, or regularity clause not forced by the hypotheses. Select the stronger-result meta-option when the best substantive candidate falls in (ii) or (iii) and the asserted stronger result is compatible with the stated hypotheses and quantifiers. Do not select it merely because it is present. Keep a substantive option only when it already gives the full requested conclusion with no removable restriction and no unsupported addition; also prefer it if the asserted stronger result conflicts with the hypotheses or quantifiers.

## Theorem-Level Precision
- Check whether an option weakens the conclusion by dropping a characterization, equality clause, or full equivalence.
- Check whether an option overstates the theorem by upgrading regularity, removing scale restrictions, or changing an existential statement into a universal one.
- When two options share the same necessary divisibility condition but one asserts actual homotopy equivalence while the other asserts p-local equivalence at every prime, do not upgrade to actual homotopy equivalence from the divisibility condition alone. Check whether the stated invariants determine only the local factors; if they do, choose the p-local formulation as the strongest justified conclusion, unless the hypotheses or a standard integral obstruction result also establish the global equivalence.

## Hypotheses
- Verify the hypotheses and domain carefully. Distractors often keep the theorem shape but alter the required assumptions.
- Pay close attention to equality cases, extremal conditions, and whether a result applies to the full family or only a restricted subfamily.

## Final Answer
- Output the final answer as the single option label only.
