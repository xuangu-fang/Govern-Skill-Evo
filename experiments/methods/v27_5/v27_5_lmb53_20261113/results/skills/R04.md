# Live Mathematical MCQ Heuristics

## Option Comparison
- Compare all options before committing. The correct choice is often the strongest statement justified by the question, while nearby distractors are weaker, overstrong, or miss an equality case.
- Track exact quantifiers such as "there exists", "for every", "if and only if", and "exactly when".

When the stem asks for the strongest statement or a complete characterization and one choice says another choice is correct but a stronger result can be proved, evaluate that choice as a candidate for the strongest true statement rather than dismissing it as vague. Compare the best concrete choice with the full theorem: does it give only a sufficient condition where an equivalence is at issue, only one witness or one-dimensional case where the full family is at issue, only a lower or limiting statement where sharper growth or regularity is indicated, or a weaker embedding or equivalence statement? If every concrete choice is strictly weaker or partial in such a way, select the choice asserting that a stronger result is provable, even though a concrete choice may also be true as a weaker statement. Do not select that choice if a concrete option exactly states the full theorem and the asserted stronger result has no support.

## Theorem-Level Precision
- Check whether an option weakens the conclusion by dropping a characterization, equality clause, or full equivalence.
- For an equivalence involving a fixed sign for all sufficiently large integers, test whether the offered condition really covers all integers. For Fourier coefficients or other multiplicative arithmetic functions, signs at primes do not determine signs at prime powers or composite indices, and equality of absolute values or a merely nonzero ratio does not give a nonnegative product. Treat a prime-only, absolute-value-only, one-way, or nonzero-ratio condition as partial. If no concrete choice states the all-integer sign condition together with the needed positivity, and a stronger-result choice is present, treat the all-integer sign theorem as the specific sharper result for the audit and select that choice.
- Check whether an option overstates the theorem by upgrading regularity, removing scale restrictions, or changing an existential statement into a universal one.
- When a question invokes a general conjecture, theorem, or characterization but restricts to a special family, separate the conjectural or general form from the relation actually established for that family. A full equivalence or all-cases characterization may be the conjecture, not a proved result in the restricted setting. If one option states a full symmetric relation and another states a partial equivalence together with a one-way implication, do not upgrade to the full equivalence merely because it sounds stronger. Choose the partially asymmetric relation only when the problem asks what holds or can be proved for the restricted family; choose the full equivalence only if the option treats it as established under the stated family hypotheses.

## Hypotheses
- Verify the hypotheses and domain carefully. Distractors often keep the theorem shape but alter the required assumptions.
- Pay close attention to equality cases, extremal conditions, and whether a result applies to the full family or only a restricted subfamily.
- Before selecting an option, match its quantifiers, domain, and named object to the stem. Extra restrictions such as frequency localization, compact subdomains, a universal cover, or a decorated or auxiliary version of the named structure make a claim conditional or partial; a bound under such a restriction does not establish the unrestricted theorem, and a claim about a decorated object does not answer a question about the undecorated named object unless that shift is requested. When an unrestricted version would assert more than the hypotheses support, do not upgrade to it merely because it is global; instead choose the strongest supported statement for the exact class, even if that statement includes a necessary local or conditional restriction. When the stem asks for behavior on the full class and a supported unrestricted option is available, prefer it over a localized, cover-level, or decorated substitute.

## Classification Checks

For identification or classification questions, check the named object's invariants separately against the construction: dimension or degree, singularity types and their multiplicities, combinatorial counts, and any stated extremal configuration. Reject a named object when one invariant already conflicts, even if its other descriptors look plausible. Do not replace a precise list of singularities or a stated degree by a simpler familiar object.

## Final Answer
- Output the final answer as the single option label only.
