# Question Answering Skill

- Preserve the grammatical framing the clue explicitly requests: when the clue asks for one member of a class, use the singular count-noun form; when it asks for a relative of his, her, or its and the passages supply a possessive label, keep the possessive pronoun. Do not change singular/plural or drop a requested possessive merely because the bare noun appears in the passages.
- Before naming an answer, resolve the clue's requested slot: for 'this [category]' clues or pronouns tied to a title or role, return the entity that satisfies that category or role, not a nearby related institution, performer, title, or other associated entity.
- When a clue supplies a company, person, or other actor only as context and asks for a demonstrative object such as 'these [plural item]' or 'this [item]', return the requested object rather than the actor or company that performs the action.
- For a clue that is only the name of a company, brand, or product and has no explicit question, when the passages repeatedly identify that entity's country of origin or national affiliation, return the country name rather than a descriptive definition, a nationality adjective, or the company/product itself; use a nationality adjective only if the clue explicitly asks for one. Do not use this treatment when the clue explicitly requests the company, product, or other named entity, or when the passages supply no country or national affiliation.
- In particular, do not answer with the company or person named in the opening clause when the rest of the clue asks what the company was founded to provide, coat, make, or otherwise act on.
- A clue that is only one geographic name, with no explicit question, asks for the encompassing country that contains that place; return the country name, not a descriptive fact about the place or a broader or adjacent entity. For a named park, landmark, or other specific subnational place that the passages repeatedly locate within a state or administrative region, return that region rather than the country; use the country only when the passages identify it as the relevant container or no more specific administrative container is established. This does not change the encompassing-country treatment for a bare city or other place name when the passages do not establish a more specific administrative container, or any place level the clue explicitly requests.
- A clue that is only a pair of place names introduced by 'In' likewise asks for the shared nationality or language adjective, not the country noun.
- Do not override an explicit place level supplied by the clue: when the clue says 'this [city/state/mountain range]', return a name at that requested level rather than replacing it with the country.
- For a clue fragment whose sole relation is containment ('Home to...' or '...that contains...') and whose object is a natural feature or resource, return the containing state, country, or continent identified in the passages rather than the contained feature or material.
- When the clue instead asks for a specific named building, work, or person, keep that requested entity rather than converting it to its containing place. Also keep the place level the clue explicitly supplies (for example, 'this city' or 'this mountain range') rather than replacing it with a larger containing place.
- For a clue that is only a bare list of two or more named fictional characters with no explicit question, return the encompassing work, series, or group containing all of them, not a role-describing sentence or a subset of names; use the title or name of that encompassing entity. For a clue that is only a bare list of two or more real people with no explicit question, identify the shared place, nationality, office, or affiliation that the passages explicitly attach to all of them and return that shared named entity in its shortest conventional form; when the passages supply a proper place or nationality as the common bond, prefer it over a generic role or descriptive group phrase, and do not answer with an article title that merely mentions the listed people. When a bare list of real people instead shares a work, series, or group named by the passages, return that encompassing work, series, or group.
- For a bare list of real people with no explicit question, when the passages identify them only as successive spouses or partners of one named person, return that named person rather than a possessive role phrase or generic group description. When the passages instead supply a shared place, nationality, office, affiliation, work, or group as the common bond, use that shared-bond treatment rather than returning the named person.
- When the clue is only a royal name or title, or a bare list of rulers, with no explicit question, return the country or realm they share, not a phrase that combines the title with that place. When the clue is only a quoted catchphrase with no explicit question, return the originating or encompassing work or series rather than a specific installment or spin-off that merely contains the phrase—but only when the passages present the phrase primarily as a line from a fictional work or series and do not identify a named real speaker as the requested referent. When the clue uses a pronoun or action to describe a person, or quotes a slogan, catchphrase, or signature line that the passages attribute to a named real person, return that person's name rather than the title of what was written, a work or fictional character associated with the phrase, or a generic role description; if the phrase or passages identify a principal person and a secondary partner, return the principal alone unless the clue explicitly asks for both. Do not apply this person-slot treatment when the clue explicitly requests a work, song, hit, or title rather than a person; return that requested work or entity even if a quoted line is associated with a named person. A bare work-title clue without an explicit question remains governed by the separate creator-slot treatment.
- When the clue quotes a distorted or reversed phrase associated with a named wordplay and the passages supply the intended phrase, return the intended phrase rather than the name of the wordplay phenomenon.
- Use the wordplay term itself only when the clue explicitly asks for that term rather than for the meaning or intended wording.
- When a clue is only a bare work title, optionally with a year, or a verbatim line of poetry, and the passages repeatedly attribute that work or line to a named creator, return that creator—the author, poet, director, or other named responsible person—rather than the work's title.
- This extends the creator-slot treatment to bare title/year and quoted poetry clues while retaining the separate treatment of a catchphrase identified as a line from a film or series where the encompassing work is the requested slot.
- For a clue that is only a bare song title with no explicit question, when the passages identify the musical, film, or show in which the song is performed as well as its songwriter(s), return that encompassing work rather than the songwriter or composer. This supersedes the general bare-work-title creator treatment for song titles; the creator treatment still applies to non-song bare titles, complete quotations, and quoted poetry.
- This song-title exception does not change the treatment of a quotation attributed to a named real person or of a film tagline presented as a line from a fictional work.
- When a clue quotes a work or verse line but leaves an explicit missing-word slot (for example, an incomplete line ending with 'this' or a blank), return the omitted word or short phrase that completes the quotation; do not return the work's title, author, or other creator. Do not apply the bare-title/year or verbatim-line creator-slot rule to such a gapped quotation.
- The creator/title treatment continues to apply when the quotation is complete, when the clue explicitly asks for its source or creator, or when the clue asks which nursery rhyme or work a person recited.
- When a passage presents a usual or typical answer alongside occasional, rare, or secondary alternatives, answer with the usual or typical answer and omit the alternatives; do not append parenthetical lists of secondary options. If more than one member is given as typical, prefer the single member the passages emphasize most (for example, by words such as 'especially', 'particularly', or 'most common') as the representative answer. Give multiple answers only when the question explicitly asks for all possibilities.
- Return the smallest conventional answer that fully identifies the requested referent, applying these form and granularity choices. When the question itself supplies the category or head noun (for example, 'this vitamin' or 'this market'), return the distinctive name or modifier without repeating that generic word, unless the passages give a conventional proper name, title, or named historical term that must remain intact. When the question states the level of the requested place (such as a city, state, or country), return the place name at that level and do not append a broader geographic qualifier the question did not ask for; likewise do not add an unrequested subtype, campus, or other narrowing qualifier, while keeping a subtype name when the question asks for that subtype. Use the passages' short form for the referent rather than a fuller formal expansion, but where the passages supply an explicit short answer label (a flashcard or Q&A answer, dictionary headword, or concise article title), that label's form controls. Do not append a trailing title phrase the question already supplies when the remaining title stem is independently recognizable; keep the conventional title intact when removing a question-supplied word would change or obscure it. For a proper name or title, keep its conventional form while dropping appended parenthetical explanations and repeated clue words. For a category, type, or generic source, keep the word that distinguishes the requested item (including a subtype name) but drop descriptive modifiers, repeated clue words, and trailing generic nouns that are not needed to identify it. Preserve units, ranges, conventional name and title components, and any qualifier needed to identify or distinguish the referent; this minimality rule does not authorize dropping required words or substituting a different entity.
- An exception to the preceding generic-word omission: when the clue's requested referent has a conventional multiword name in which the category noun is a fixed part—such as a standard anatomical term that conventionally includes the organ or category word, a proper place name, or a named building or institution—keep that full conventional name even if the clue repeats the generic category word; do not reduce it to the bare modifier, except that a trailing legal or institutional designator may be dropped under the shorter-institution-name rule below. Do not add a generic category noun or descriptive expansion merely because the passages contain a fuller descriptive form: use the passages' conventional short name for the referent rather than a longer variant that is not the established name. When the clue asks for a general object or category and the passages also give a narrower material, animal, or other subtype that is not needed to identify the requested referent, return the general object; do not append the unrequested subtype, and keep a subtype name only when the question asks for it or it is needed to identify the requested item. This preservation rule does not override slot resolution: if the clue asks for a different category or role, return the entity satisfying that requested slot.
- Also do not repeat a clue-supplied rate noun, country qualifier, or distinguishing descriptor in the answer, or expand the passages' short label into a longer formal title: drop a repeated measure word after 'rate of this', and drop a country qualifier already stated in the clue from an office title. When the clue already supplies the category or head noun and the passages use a shorter distinctive name for the same company, university, or other institution, return that shorter name and drop a trailing legal or institutional designator such as 'Company,' 'Corporation,' 'Motor Corporation,' 'University,' or 'State University' if the remaining name unambiguously identifies the same referent, even when the longer form is the institution's formal name. For a person, use the passages' recurring short name for that person and do not invent a surname or other name component absent from the passages, while keeping an honorific, middle initial, or fuller personal-name component when the passages present it as part of that person's conventional label. Use the spelling of the chosen passage label and do not add diacritics it does not contain. When the clue gives named examples and asks what they are, return the base class noun without adding a descriptive modifier that the examples already imply. This dropping rule does not apply to a conventional anatomical or biological category noun required to form the established term, a category word that is a fixed part of a proper place name or named building or institution, or a word needed to distinguish a different entity or requested place level; the trailing legal or institutional designators above remain droppable when the shorter passage name unambiguously identifies the same referent. Do not expand a short label into a longer formal title merely because a fuller form appears elsewhere.
- When the clue asks for a general type, material, shape, or the meaning/translation of a term, return the basic general term the passages use for that requested item, omitting optional descriptive modifiers and place-, event-, color-, technology-, or emblem-derived adjectives; do not reduce a specifically requested named entity to that generic word when the passages identify a distinct proper name.
- When the clue supplies a color or other distinguishing descriptor that the passages also use in a label for the requested item, return the passage-supported label without repeating the descriptor unless the descriptor is needed to identify a distinct proper name, title, species, or other entity; likewise, when the passages use a subtype qualifier the clue did not ask for, return the general requested item rather than appending the unrequested subtype, unless the subtype is needed to identify the requested entity.
- When the clue asks for a type named for a person and already supplies the generic category noun, return the person-derived modifier or name alone rather than repeating the clue-supplied category noun, unless the passages give the full compound as an explicit short answer label or the category noun is a fixed part of an established proper name or title, a conventional anatomical or biological term, a non-eponymous conventional device or technical term, or a fixed idiomatic or legal phrase; in those preservation cases, keep the full conventional expression.
- When the passages name a beverage or other mass-noun category, use its conventional singular or mass form rather than pluralizing it merely because the clue uses 'these' or gives plural examples; use a plural only when the clue asks for multiple countable varieties or instances.
- When the clue asks for a specific named entity, title, or named type/term rather than a general term describing a specifically named object or event, use an explicit short answer label supplied by the passages—such as a flashcard answer or Q&A answer field, dictionary headword, explicit name-change statement, or concise article title. If a flashcard answer or Q&A answer field gives an exact answer form, prefer it over a longer article heading or body mention. Keep the chosen label's name form and grammatical number rather than expanding it from a fuller sentence elsewhere, and prefer its common or familiar form over a more formal expansion. Drop a generic type word or corporate suffix when the clue already supplies the category and the remaining distinctive name is the passage's label for the same referent; otherwise keep the label's conventional proper-name components, including those needed to identify or distinguish the referent. This does not override the separate treatment of clues that ask for a general term or type for a specifically named object or event.
- When the passages provide both a full and a conventional abbreviated form of a proper name and the abbreviated form is independently identifying, return the abbreviated passage form rather than expanding it, unless the clue explicitly requests the full form or the abbreviation would name a different entity or place level. Preserve the passages' formatting for initials in personal names; do not insert spaces between initials when the passage label presents them closed up.
- When the same clue appears verbatim in the passages and is accompanied by an explicit short answer label—such as a flashcard or Q&A answer field, a form like '| answer' or 'right: answer'—treat that label as the intended answer for that clue and return its answer and form, even if another familiar phrase also fits the clue. Do not override the label merely because a longer article heading or body sentence offers a more familiar or expanded phrasing. Still follow a different requested slot when the clue explicitly asks for one (for example, a general term, a different category, or the source or creator rather than the quoted expression), and disregard the label when the passage makes clear it belongs to a different numbered clue. This label priority does not override the historical-name rule above or another explicit requested-slot rule.
