# Live Mathematical MCQ Heuristics

## Option Comparison
- Compare all options before committing. The correct choice is often the strongest statement justified by the question, while nearby distractors are weaker, overstrong, or miss an equality case; do not read 'strongest' as 'the most detailed concrete formula'.
- If one choice says that another choice is correct while a stronger result can be proven, treat that meta-choice as a leading candidate for an item asking for the strongest provable statement, and compare it explicitly against every concrete choice rather than dismissing it as filler.
- Before discarding the meta-choice, identify for each concrete choice its specific shortfall relative to the stated hypotheses: an extra restriction on the parameters or family, a weaker conclusion mode (liminf instead of limsup, a subsequence instead of a limit, existence of one object instead of a statement about every object), a missing converse or equivalence direction, or regularity/exponents below the sharp value.
- Select the meta-choice when no concrete choice states the exact sharp form for the stated hypotheses. This is especially indicated when all concrete choices carry such a shortfall and are near-variants differing only in a constant, exponent, endpoint, or one added clause, because the sharp theorem is often not among them. Near-variant grading is only a further signal, not a prerequisite: the meta-choice can also be correct when concrete choices differ substantially but each individually misses the sharp form.
- Select a concrete choice instead when that choice already states the exact sharp form for the stated hypotheses. Do not prefer the meta-choice automatically; the same item-by-item shortfall audit is required, and a concrete choice is preferred whenever it is the sharp statement.
- Track exact quantifiers such as "there exists", "for every", "if and only if", and "exactly when".

## Theorem-Level Precision
- Check whether an option weakens the conclusion by dropping a characterization, equality clause, or full equivalence.
- Check whether an option overstates the theorem by upgrading regularity, removing scale restrictions, or changing an existential statement into a universal one.

## Hypotheses
- Verify the hypotheses and domain carefully. Distractors often keep the theorem shape but alter the required assumptions.
- Pay close attention to equality cases, extremal conditions, and whether a result applies to the full family or only a restricted subfamily.

## Final Answer
- Output the final answer as the single option label only.
