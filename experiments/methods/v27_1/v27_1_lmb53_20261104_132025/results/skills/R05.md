# Live Mathematical MCQ Heuristics

## Option Comparison
- Compare all options before committing. The correct choice is often the strongest statement justified by the question, while nearby distractors are weaker, overstrong, or miss an equality case; do not read 'strongest' as 'the most detailed concrete formula'.
- If one choice says that another remaining choice is correct while a stronger result can be proven, make that meta-choice the default selection when the stem asks for the strongest provable statement, an equivalence, or a complete characterization; in other cases treat it as a leading candidate. Choose a concrete option instead only if it passes the exactness test below. Compare it explicitly against every concrete choice rather than dismissing it as filler, keeping the audit item-by-item and refusing any overstrong claim the stem does not support.
- Exactness test for overriding the meta-choice: the concrete option must visibly match the stem's stated hypotheses, scope, quantifiers, equality or sign conditions, and requested logical form; it must not add a restriction, omit a converse or equivalence direction, weaken locality, change the norm, or add an unsupported clause. Plausibility, detail, a familiar theorem shape, or an added "Moreover" assertion is not a pass. If the concrete options are near-variants distinguished by a constant, exponent, endpoint, norm, or one added hypothesis and none visibly states the exact sharp claim, select the meta-choice.
- For equivalence stems, check both directions and sign/scope explicitly: replacing "for all sufficiently large n" by a prime-only condition, allowing a negative constant when nonnegativity is at issue, or giving only a necessary condition is a visible shortfall unless the stem supplies the converse.
- Before selecting a concrete choice over such a meta-choice, identify a visible shortfall in the concrete choice relative to the stem's stated scope and requested logical form: an added subclass restriction, an extra restriction on the parameters or family, an extra 'provided' hypothesis, a one-way implication where equivalence is requested, a necessary-only condition, an omitted equality or rigidity clause, a weaker conclusion mode (liminf instead of limsup, a subsequence instead of a limit, existence of one object instead of a statement about every object), a missing converse or equivalence direction, or regularity/exponents below the sharp value all count as shortfalls, not as reasons to prefer that concrete choice.
- If the meta-choice explicitly says a stronger result is available, do not treat the strongest concrete choice as satisfying a request for the strongest provable statement. Select the meta-choice when no concrete choice visibly states the exact sharp form for the stated hypotheses; this is especially indicated when the concrete choices are a graded family or near-variants differing only in a constant, exponent, endpoint, or one added clause and none visibly matches the exact sharp claim. Near-variant grading is only a further signal, not a prerequisite: the meta-choice can be correct when concrete choices differ substantially but each individually misses the sharp form.
- The meta-choice remains the default unless a concrete choice passes the exactness test above; do not dismiss it merely because it is generic or lacks an explicit formula. Apply the same item-by-item shortfall audit to every concrete choice.
- Preserve concrete selection when no such meta-choice is present: continue to select the strongest concrete statement that visibly matches the stem, and do not convert this rule into a universal preference for generic/meta options or for the longest option.
- Track exact quantifiers such as "there exists", "for every", "if and only if", and "exactly when".

## Strength Modes
- Treat localized and unlocalized versions of a conclusion as different claims rather than automatic strength levels: p-local equivalence for every prime does not by itself establish ordinary homotopy equivalence without a stated compatibility or integrality condition. When a stem requesting the strongest provable statement explicitly uses p-local equivalence and offers both localized and integral conclusions, do not upgrade a gcd or hypothesis equality to integral homotopy equivalence unless the stem supplies support; compare the localized option against the stated hypotheses.
- Separate nonstable unboundedness from stable growth when options use different quantitative modes. A universal vanishing clause for a stable invariant is not a strengthening of an existential positive-growth clause; they are incompatible, so do not choose the option merely because it adds a universal vanishing or equality statement to an unboundedness assertion.
- Apply either mode check only when those conclusion forms are actually present; if a concrete option already states the exact integral, vanishing, or growth conclusion required by the stem, preserve that concrete option rather than replacing it mechanically.

## Theorem-Level Precision
- Check whether an option weakens the conclusion by dropping a characterization, equality clause, or full equivalence.
- Check whether an option overstates the theorem by upgrading regularity, removing scale restrictions, or changing an existential statement into a universal one.

## Hypotheses
- Verify the hypotheses and domain carefully. Distractors often keep the theorem shape but alter the required assumptions.
- Pay close attention to equality cases, extremal conditions, and whether a result applies to the full family or only a restricted subfamily.

## Final Answer
- Output the final answer as the single option label only.
